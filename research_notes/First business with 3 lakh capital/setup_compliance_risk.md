Scope notes for the report writer:

## 1. Business structure: sole proprietorship vs OPC vs LLP vs private limited (2026 cost and time, and which to start with)

### Takeaway
Start as a **sole proprietorship**. It has no separate incorporation step: it is created by the founder's PAN plus one or two business registrations (Udyam, GST, or Shop & Establishment). It costs about ₹0–1,000 and can be set up in days. A private limited company (about ₹7,000–20,000 to register, 7–12 working days, and roughly ₹32,000–65,000 in first-year compliance) only makes sense if the founder expects to raise equity or needs DPIIT recognition. An OPC or LLP adds cost and compliance with no benefit at the testing stage.

### Cited Findings
- **Registration cost ranges (2026, all-in, consultant-quoted):** sole proprietorship ₹500–1,000; partnership ₹1,000–3,000; LLP ₹4,000–8,000; OPC ₹5,000–10,000; private limited ₹7,000–15,000 — [incorpX via search summary](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/choose-business-structure-pvt-ltd-llp-opc-proprietorship). Another platform puts private limited at ₹12,000–20,000 excluding GST — [Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/company-registration-fees-in-india). Another puts OPC at ₹6,500–11,000 vs private limited at ₹10,000–18,000 — [incorpX OPC vs Pvt Ltd](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/opc-vs-private-limited-company).
- **Year-1 total (registration plus compliance):** proprietorship ₹500–1,000; partnership ₹3,000–8,000; LLP ₹12,000–23,000; OPC ₹15,000–30,000; private limited ₹32,000–65,000 — [incorpX 2026 guide](https://www.incorpx.io/guide/how-to-register-a-company-in-india-in-2026).
- **Official MCA fees for companies:** a company incorporated through SPICe+ with authorised capital up to ₹15 lakh pays **zero MCA filing fee**. State stamp duty on the MOA/AOA still applies (about ₹500 to ₹5,000+ depending on the state). Each director needs a Class-3 DSC at about ₹1,000–2,000. A realistic all-in cost for a two-director company is ₹12,000–20,000, and the process takes about 7–12 working days — [vjmglobal / taxaj summaries](https://www.vjmglobal.com/feeds/blog/mca-company-registration); [taxaj 2026 cost guide](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/company-registration-fees-india/).
- **LLP:** broadly ₹9,000–23,000 all-in. Government fees depend on the contribution amount — [taxaj](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=1004).
- **Company and LLP timelines:** 7–10 working days once documents are filed — [incorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/guide/how-to-register-a-company-in-india-in-2026).
- **Equity limits:** an OPC or LLP cannot issue stock to investors or set up an ESOP pool, so founders seeking venture capital use a private limited company — [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/business/solo-founders-and-partnerships-are-boosting-indias-incorporation-wave).
- **DPIIT (Startup India) recognition** is open only to private limited companies, registered partnership firms and LLPs. The 2026 framework also added cooperative societies. **Sole proprietorships are not eligible.** Other conditions: the entity is under 10 years old (20 for deep tech), works on innovation or a scalable model, and was not formed by splitting an existing business — [Taxmann DPIIT FAQs](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/faqs-dpiit-recognition-for-start-ups/); [Patron Accounting, Apr 2026](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/dpiit-startup-recognition-2026-guide).
- **Presumptive taxation under 44AD (now Section 58)** is available to resident individuals, HUFs and partnership firms, **but not LLPs** (or companies) — [ClearTax 44AD](https://cleartax.in/s/section-44ad-presumptive-scheme). A proprietorship can therefore use presumptive tax and an LLP or company cannot.

### Inferences
- **Recommendation: a sole proprietorship in the founder's own name, with a trade name.** It is the cheapest and fastest option and the only one that can realistically be trading inside 2–3 weeks while staying fully legal. It also keeps the simple presumptive-tax option (see section 3). The downside is **unlimited personal liability**. For low-ticket product sales or a service agency, that risk is manageable with clear client contracts and no borrowing.
- **When to incorporate later:** convert to a private limited company once there is real traction plus one of these: (a) the founder wants outside equity or DPIIT benefits, (b) a co-founder joins, (c) large corporate clients require a company vendor, or (d) liability exposure grows (for example, food at scale or large B2B contracts). An LLP fits a two-partner service firm that will never raise equity.
- **The OPC is not worth it here.** It costs nearly as much as a private limited company, cannot take equity investors, and has no presumptive-tax option.
- **Employment risk (not sourced; flag for the mentor):** the founder works full-time. Their employment contract may include moonlighting, conflict-of-interest or IP-assignment clauses. A proprietorship in their own name is fully visible (PAN-linked). They should read the contract before registering, especially for a B2B or agency business that could overlap with the employer's clients.

### Gaps
- No official MCA fee schedule was fetched for LLP (FiLLiP) by contribution slab. The LLP figures are consultant estimates.
- Proprietorship "registration" is not a legal registration. The ₹500–1,000 figure is a consultant convenience fee. The government cost of Udyam is ₹0 (section 2), and GST registration is also ₹0.

## 2. Registrations: GST, Udyam, bank account, FSSAI, Shop & Establishment, trademark, IEC (real 2026 timelines and costs)

### Takeaway
The minimum legal kit for a proprietorship is PAN, **Udyam** (free, same day), and a current account (banks want two proofs of the business: Udyam plus GST or Shop & Establishment). Most businesses also need **GST**, which is free and takes 3–7 working days for small applicants under the November 2025 simplified scheme. GST becomes **mandatory from the first sale** for anyone selling goods inter-state or on Amazon or Flipkart. A sole Meesho seller selling only within one state, or a service business under ₹20 lakh, can legally defer it. Food businesses need **FSSAI basic registration**, which since 1 April 2026 covers turnover up to ₹1.5 crore and has perpetual validity. A trademark application (₹4,500 official fee for an individual or MSME) can be filed on day one, and "TM" can be used immediately.

### Cited Findings

**GST: when mandatory**
- **Turnover thresholds (FY 2026-27):** ₹40 lakh for suppliers of only goods in most states; ₹20 lakh for services or mixed supply. Several special-category states and Telangana use ₹20 lakh for goods — [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-limit-registration-threshold-india/); [taxaj](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=943). These thresholds did not change in 2025–2026.
- **Inter-state supply of goods** is excluded from the threshold exemption. Section 24 of the CGST Act makes registration compulsory for such suppliers regardless of turnover — [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-limit-registration-threshold-india/).
- **Inter-state supply of services is exempt from compulsory registration** up to ₹20 lakh aggregate all-India turnover (₹10 lakh in special-category states) under Notification 10/2017-Integrated Tax dated 13 October 2017 — [GSTZen](https://gstzen.in/a/integrated-tax-notification-102017-seeks-to-exempt-persons-making-inter-state-supplies-of-taxable-services-from-registration-under-section-232.html). This matters for a social media agency billing clients in other states.
- **E-commerce sellers of goods (October 2023 relaxation):** Notification 34/2023-Central Tax (31 July 2023, effective 1 October 2023) exempts persons supplying goods through an e-commerce operator (ECO) from registration if their turnover in both the previous and current year is within the threshold. Conditions: no inter-state supply; selling through ECOs in **only one state or UT**; a PAN; declaring PAN, address and state on the GST portal; and obtaining an **enrolment number** before the first sale — [Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/no-gst-registration-required-for-small-dealers-to-supply-goods-through-ecos-notification); [IRIS GST](https://irisgst.com/gst-notification-34-2023-gst-notification-34-2023-central-tax-dt-31-july-2023/). The Taxmann summary words the turnover cap as ₹20 lakh, while other sources describe it as "the threshold limit". The writer should describe it as "within the registration threshold" and have a CA confirm the figure for goods.
- **How marketplaces apply it:** **Meesho** lets sellers without a GSTIN register using an enrolment ID or UIN, for intra-state sales only and in eligible categories (electronics and cosmetics still need a GSTIN). **Flipkart requires a GSTIN** before products can be listed, and Amazon is reported to require one for most categories — [Razorpay Learn](https://razorpay.com/learn/how-to-sell-online-without-gst-number/); [Fretron Meesho guide](https://www.fretron.com/guides/sell-on-meesho/).
- **GST TCS on marketplace sales:** ECOs collect 0.5% TCS (reduced from 1% on 10 July 2024) on net taxable sales. It is credited to the seller's cash ledger and recovered through returns — [ClearTax TCS](https://cleartax.in/s/tcs-under-goods-and-services-tax); [taxaj](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=918).

**GST: timeline and cost (Changed in 2025–2026)**
- **Simplified registration from 1 November 2025** (Rule 14A, Notification 18/2025-Central Tax): applicants who self-assess monthly output tax at **₹2.5 lakh or less** (CGST + SGST + IGST), or who are flagged low-risk by data analytics, receive registration **electronically within 3 working days of the ARN**. Aadhaar authentication of the primary authorised signatory and at least one promoter is required, and the scheme is voluntary. The Finance Minister said it would cover about 96% of new applicants — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/taxation/small-businesses-to-get-gst-registration-in-3-working-days-what-you-need-to-know); [CA Hemant Garg](https://www.cahemantgarg.com/2025/11/gst-registration-in-3-working-days.html).
- **Standard route:** 3–7 working days after the ARN with clean Aadhaar authentication. Without it, a REG-03 query or physical verification can stretch to about 30 days. Some applicants are sent for **biometric verification at a GST Suvidha Kendra**, which adds a week or more and must be completed within 15 days — [echai.ventures](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-gst-basics/gst-registration-timeline-cost); [ClearTax Aadhaar authentication](https://cleartax.in/s/aadhar-authentication-under-gst-act).
- **Cost:** the official fee is **₹0**. CA or platform charges are typically **₹1,000–5,000** — [echai.ventures](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-gst-basics/gst-registration-timeline-cost).
- **GST 2.0 rate rationalisation (Changed, effective 22 September 2025):** the 56th GST Council meeting replaced the 5/12/18/28% slabs with **two main slabs of 5% and 18%**, plus a **40%** slab for sin and luxury goods (tobacco, pan masala, ultra-premium cars, online gaming). About 99% of 12% items moved to 5%, and about 90% of 28% items moved to 18% — [WION](https://www.wionews.com/india-news/gst-meeting-council-gives-nod-to-two-tier-tax-structure-common-people-farmers-to-be-benefited-says-sitharaman-1756917961983/amp); [Kotak MF explainer](https://www.kotakmf.com/Information/blogs/gst-2-point-0). Pricing sheets and margin maths must use post-22-September-2025 rates. Old supplier quotes or competitor prices may still reflect the older slabs.

**Udyam (MSME) registration**
- **Free and paperless** on udyamregistration.gov.in. The portal warns "Beware of Fake Sites", and the MSME Ministry has warned that look-alike websites charge "processing" or "certificate" fees — [StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/msme-ministry-alerts-msmes-to-beware-of-fake-websites-charging-money-in-the-name-of-registration-91048.html); [Udyam portal](https://www.udyamregistration.gov.in/). It is Aadhaar and PAN based and usually completes the same day. The "same day" claim is a consultant summary ([calcguru](https://calcguru.in/?p=4361)) and was not checked on the portal.
- **Changed in 2025:** Budget 2025 raised MSME classification limits (investment ×2.5, turnover ×2) and announced **customised credit cards with a ₹5 lakh limit for micro enterprises registered on Udyam**, with 10 lakh cards planned in year 1 — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2098389); [ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/budget-2025-govt-enhances-msme-investment-limits-and-introduces-credit-cards-with-a-rs-5-lac-limit20250201130822/).

**Current (business) bank account**
- Under RBI KYC rules, banks typically need **two proofs of the proprietorship's existence** from a list that includes Shop & Establishment registration, the GST certificate, Udyam registration, a municipal trade licence, an ITR, an IEC, a utility bill in the business name, and a CA certificate. They also need the proprietor's PAN and Aadhaar — [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/documents-required-for-proprietorship-bank-account-opening-in-india); [Indian Bank checklist, June 2026](https://indianbank.bank.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Customer-Document-Checklist-.pdf).
- A practical low-cost combination is **Udyam (free, instant) plus Shop & Establishment**, or Udyam plus GST — [calcguru](https://calcguru.in/?p=4310); [mymoneyway, Aug 2026](https://www.mymoneyway.in/2026/08/current-account-opening-documents-proprietorship-firm.html).

**FSSAI (food) (Changed, effective 1 April 2026)**
- FSSAI notified the Licensing and Registration Amendment Regulations on 10 March 2026, effective 1 April 2026. **Basic Registration now covers turnover up to ₹1.5 crore** (previously ₹12 lakh). A State licence covers ₹1.5 crore to ₹50 crore and a Central licence covers above ₹50 crore. **Registrations and licences granted from 1 April 2026 have perpetual validity** unless suspended, cancelled or surrendered. The amendment also introduced deemed registration for street vendors and risk-based inspection — [SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/fssai-applicability); [LexComply](https://lexcomply.com/blog/fssai-registration-2026-6a7ebe5dc6153); [JustStart](https://juststart.co.in/blog/fssai-new-updates/); [PSR Compliance](https://www.psrcompliance.com/blog/fssai-turnover-limit-update-2026).
- **Fee and timeline:** the basic registration government fee has been **₹100 per year**, applied on foscos.fssai.gov.in, with about a 7-day typical turnaround (State licence about 30 days; Central 30–60 days) — [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/business-guides/fssai-license-fees-cost-of-food-business-registration/); [calcguru](https://calcguru.in/?p=4363).

**Shop & Establishment**
- This is a state law, so fees and portals vary. Karnataka: ₹300–4,000. Maharashtra (2017 Act): nil to ₹2,400. Delhi: ₹50–2,500. Some sources say small establishments are free in Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Delhi. Registration is usually due **within 30 days of starting business** (60 days in Maharashtra). Processing takes 1–15 working days and is often auto-approved — [incorpX state-wise](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/shop-and-establishment-license-state-wise-guide); [RegisterKaro](https://www.registerkaro.in/post/shop-and-establishment-act-registration-fees). Portals: Maharashtra aaplesarkar.mahaonline.gov.in (Gumasta), Karnataka labour.karnataka.gov.in, Delhi labour.delhi.gov.in — [incorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/shop-establishment-certificate-registration-2026).

**Trademark**
- **Official e-filing fee: ₹4,500 per mark per class** for individuals, proprietorships, MSMEs and startups, which is 50% of the ₹9,000 company rate. Physical filing costs ₹5,000 — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/ufaqs/trademark-filing-cost-inr-4500-india/); [SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/trademark-registration-fee-concession-msme-startup). Registration takes about **9–12 months** if there is no objection or opposition — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/ufaqs/trademark-filing-cost-inr-4500-india/).
- **™** can be used from day one, before or without filing. **®** may only be used after registration. Misusing ® carries a civil penalty under Section 107 (as amended by the Jan Vishwas Act, 2023, effective 1 August 2024) of **0.5% of turnover or ₹5 lakh, whichever is less** — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/trade-mark-symbols-india/); [disytax 2026](https://disytax.com/can-i-use-tm-without-registration/).

**IEC (only for exports or imports)**
- The official DGFT fee is **₹500**, filed online, PAN-linked, issued in 1–3 working days, and valid for life. An annual update between April and June is required, with no renewal fee. E-commerce exporters (for example, Amazon Global Selling) need one — [Shiprocket](https://www.shiprocket.in/blog/import-export-code-iec-india/); [Cashfree](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/what-is-import-export-code-iec/).

### Inferences
- **Day-by-day registration plan for a proprietorship (realistic):**
  - Days 1–2: choose a trade name, check the trademark search on ipindia.gov.in, file Udyam (₹0, same day), and use ™.
  - Days 2–5: apply for GST if needed, choosing the Rule 14A simplified route (expect 3–7 working days; plan for up to 30 days if biometric or physical verification is triggered). Apply for Shop & Establishment if the business has premises, or FSSAI basic registration (about 7 days) if it sells food.
  - Days 5–10: open the current account once two proofs exist.
  - Total official cost: about ₹0 for Udyam and GST, plus ₹100 FSSAI, plus about ₹0–2,500 Shop & Establishment, plus ₹4,500 trademark (optional), plus ₹500 IEC (only if exporting). With a CA doing GST, trademark and the bank paperwork, add roughly ₹3,000–10,000 in consultant fees. (These totals are assembled from the figures above.)
- **Which business types need GST from day one:**
  - Selling on Amazon or Flipkart, or shipping goods to other states via your own website or Instagram: **GST from the first sale.**
  - Meesho, or your own channels selling only within your state: GST can wait until the threshold (use an enrolment ID on Meesho).
  - Social media agency, content or sales services: legally optional below ₹20 lakh even with out-of-state clients. However, corporate clients often prefer a GSTIN to claim input tax credit, so voluntary registration may help win B2B work (the input-credit point is an inference, not sourced).
  - Corporate gifting: this is goods supply. If the buyer or delivery is in another state, compulsory registration applies.
- Do **not** pay any website for Udyam. A heavily advertised "Udyam registration" page that charges money is the classic trap.
- If the founder runs from home, Shop & Establishment may not apply or may be awkward to obtain. In that case the bank proofs are Udyam plus GST (inference; banks vary).

### Gaps
- The new FSSAI fee structure under perpetual validity (one-time vs annual ₹100) was not confirmed from the FSSAI gazette.
- The exact turnover cap for the Notification 34/2023 exemption (₹20 lakh vs the ₹40 lakh goods threshold) is described inconsistently across sources. Confirm against the CBIC notification text.
- Amazon.in's current rules for GST-exempt categories (for example, books) were not verified.
- The launch status of the Budget 2025 MSME credit card scheme as of October 2026 was not confirmed.
- Maharashtra's 2017 Act intimation-only regime for establishments with fewer than 10 workers is commonly cited but was not verified this session.

## 3. Taxes for a small proprietor: presumptive tax (44AD/44ADA, now Section 58), GST composition, TDS on e-commerce (194-O, now Section 393)

### Takeaway
A proprietor can declare **6% of digital turnover (8% of cash) as profit** for a trading or product business, up to ₹3 crore turnover. A professional or consultant can declare **50% of receipts** up to ₹75 lakh. Neither needs books of accounts. Under the **Income-tax Act, 2025 (in force from 1 April 2026)** these provisions were consolidated into **Section 58**. GST composition (1% for traders, 5% for restaurants, 6% for services up to ₹50 lakh) simplifies GST but **cannot be used for inter-state sales**, and e-commerce composition sellers are limited to intra-state goods. Marketplaces deduct **0.1% TDS** (former 194-O, now Section 393) and **0.5% GST TCS**. Both are credited back to the seller.

### Cited Findings
- **44AD limits (FY 2025-26 rules carried forward):** ₹2 crore turnover, or **₹3 crore if cash receipts are 5% or less of turnover**. Deemed profit is **8%**, or **6%** on digital receipts. Taxpayers who opt in and leave before 5 years are barred for the next 5 years. **100% of advance tax is due by 15 March.** Eligible taxpayers are resident individuals, HUFs and partnership firms, not LLPs — [ClearTax 44AD](https://cleartax.in/s/section-44ad-presumptive-scheme).
- **44ADA (professionals):** ₹50 lakh, or ₹75 lakh if cash is 5% or less. Deemed profit is 50%. No books are required. Sources say there is no 5-year lock-in for 44ADA — [ClearTax 44ADA](https://cleartax.in/s/section-44ada); [taxaj](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=1704). Eligibility covers specified professions (legal, medical, engineering, accountancy, technical consultancy and other notified ones) — [taxaj](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/?p=516).
- **Changed in 2026: the new Act.** Sections 44AD, 44ADA and 44AE of the 1961 Act are consolidated into **Section 58 of the Income-tax Act, 2025**. Reports say the CBDT confirmed that existing opt-in status carries forward — [taxconcept](https://taxconcept.net/income-tax/presumptive-taxation-new-thresholds-under-sections-44ad-44ada-and-44ae-for-2025/); [Finnovate / ICAI NIRC deck](https://nirc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/The-New-Income-Tax-Act-2025-nanda-sir.pdf). The CBDT confirmation and the section number come from secondary sources. The ICAI NIRC PDF was listed in results but not read in full.
- **TDS on e-commerce (former Section 194-O):** the ECO deducts **0.1%** of gross sales (reduced from 1% with effect from 1 October 2024). Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 this sits in **Section 393(1), Table Sl. 8(v)** (payment code 1035), still at 0.1% — [Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/section-194o-tds-0-1-percent-current-rate-history-india/); [IndiaFilings](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/section-194o-tds-on-e-commerce-transactions). The table reference comes from one secondary source.
- **GST TCS by ECOs (Section 52 CGST):** 0.5% from 10 July 2024 (0.25% CGST + 0.25% SGST, or 0.5% IGST). It is credited to the seller's electronic cash ledger — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/tcs-under-goods-and-services-tax).
- **GST composition:** turnover limit **₹1.5 crore** for traders, manufacturers and restaurants at **1% / 1% / 5%**. Service providers and mixed suppliers use **Section 10(2A) at 6% up to ₹50 lakh** (consultants, small agencies, salons). Composition dealers get **no input tax credit**, **cannot make inter-state outward supplies**, cannot charge GST on invoices (buyers get no credit), and file **4 quarterly payments plus 1 annual return** — [Busy](https://busy.in/gst/gst-composition-scheme/); [calcguru](https://calcguru.in/?p=926).
- **Composition for e-commerce sellers (allowed since 1 October 2023):** the Finance Act 2023 amended Section 10 so composition dealers can **supply goods through ECOs, intra-state only**. Notification 36/2023-CT (4 August 2023, effective 1 October 2023) requires ECOs to block inter-state supplies by composition dealers and to collect TCS. **Services through ECOs remain ineligible for composition** — [Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/cbic-notifies-special-procedure-for-ecos-in-respect-of-supply-of-goods-made-by-composition-dealers); [TaxNinja](https://taxninja.in/blog/budget-2023-composition-scheme-benefit-to-e-commerce-goods-suppliers).

### Inferences
- **Worked example (illustrative, not sourced):** the founder sells ₹10 lakh of products in the year, all via UPI or cards. Deemed profit under Section 58 is 6%, or ₹60,000. That amount is added to salary income and taxed at the founder's slab. With 44ADA-type services worth ₹10 lakh, deemed profit is ₹5 lakh. For a salaried founder that could push total income above the effective nil-tax band, so advance tax and the right ITR form need CA input.
- **Practical tax choice by business type:**
  - Online products sold nationwide: regular GST (composition is not workable because sales are inter-state) plus 44AD-type presumptive tax at 6%.
  - Local food or cloud kitchen: composition at 5% if only local, plus presumptive tax (note the input-credit loss).
  - Agency or consulting: often under ₹20 lakh, so no GST; presumptive 44ADA-type tax only if the service counts as a specified profession. Otherwise use 44AD at 6%. A social media agency's classification is unclear and needs a CA.
- **Keep digital receipts at 95% or more** (UPI, bank transfer, payment gateway) to qualify for the 6% rate and the ₹3 crore / ₹75 lakh higher limits.
- Marketplace TDS (0.1%) and TCS (0.5%) are small but appear on Form 26AS / AIS and in GST ledgers. The founder must file returns to recover them.

### Gaps
- Confirmation from an official CBDT or incometax.gov.in source of the new Section 58 and 393 numbering, and of whether the 5-year lock-in carries into Section 58.
- Whether a "social media agency" qualifies as a specified profession for 44ADA-type treatment (most sources list only notified professions).
- ITR form numbering under the new Act for tax year 2026-27 was not researched.

## 4. Government schemes: Mudra, Stand-Up India, PMEGP, state schemes, Startup India — useful within 3 weeks?

### Takeaway
**None will put money in the founder's account within the 2–3-week launch window in a way worth depending on.** Mudra Shishu (up to ₹50,000) or Kishore (up to ₹5 lakh) loans can be approved in about 1–2 weeks for well-documented applicants. They are best treated as a **later working-capital option once there is sales history**, not launch capital. PMEGP takes about 2 months or more after EDP training. Stand-Up India starts at ₹10 lakh and is only for women and SC/ST founders. Startup India (DPIIT) recognition is not available to a sole proprietorship. Udyam registration is the one "scheme" step worth doing on day one, because it unlocks MSME-linked credit later.

### Cited Findings
- **Mudra (PMMY) tiers:** Shishu up to ₹50,000; Kishore ₹50,000–5 lakh; Tarun ₹5–10 lakh; **Tarun Plus ₹10–20 lakh** (**changed:** announced in Budget July 2024, notified October 2024, for borrowers who have repaid a Tarun loan) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/economy/news/centre-doubles-loan-limit-under-pradhan-mantri-mudra-yojana-to-rs-20-lakh-124102501146_1.html); [Vajiram](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/government-doubles-mudra-loan-limit-to-rs-20-lakh-in-new-tarun-plus-category/).
- **Mudra processing time:** lender blogs say 7–15 working days, or about 2 weeks for loans up to ₹5 lakh, if documents are complete. The applicant must submit a projected earnings plan. Shishu loans can run up to about 6 years — [Tata Capital](https://www.tatacapital.com/blog/loan-for-business/mudra-loan-for-new-business/); [SMFG India Credit](https://www.smfgindiacredit.com/knowledge-center/mudra-loan-for-new-business.aspx). These are lender marketing claims, not official SLAs.
- **Stand-Up India:** bank loans of **₹10 lakh–₹1 crore** for **SC/ST and/or women** borrowers aged 18+, for **greenfield** (first-time) ventures in manufacturing, services or trading. It covers up to 85% of project cost, with a minimum 10% borrower margin. Repayment is over 7 years with up to an 18-month moratorium. It needs no collateral (CGSSI guarantee) and charges a 0.20% processing fee — [SBI](https://sbi.co.in/web/get-business-product-information/stand-up-india); [Go Digit](https://www.godigit.com/government-schemes/what-is-stand-up-india-scheme).
- **PMEGP:** the implementing agency (KVIC/KVIB/DIC) contacts the applicant within 5 working days of applying. **EDP training** is 10 working days for projects above ₹5 lakh, 5 days for projects up to ₹5 lakh, and not mandatory up to ₹2 lakh, and it is required before the first instalment. The total time to get the loan is **about two months** after training — [Paisabazaar](https://www.paisabazaar.com/business-loan/how-to-apply-for-pmegp-loan-scheme/); [Cashfree](https://www.cashfree.com/blog/pmegp-scheme-subsidy-rates-eligibility-how-to-apply/).
- **Startup India / DPIIT:** limited to private limited companies, partnerships, LLPs and (from 2026) cooperatives; not proprietorships. The turnover cap is reported as raised in the February 2026 framework (₹200 crore, or ₹300 crore for deep tech, vs the long-standing ₹100 crore) — [Patron Accounting, Apr 2026](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/dpiit-startup-recognition-2026-benefits-eligibility-application-tax); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/faqs-dpiit-recognition-for-start-ups/). One source flags that the ₹200 crore figure applies only "in certain cases", so this is unresolved.
- **Budget 2025 announcements:** a term-loan scheme of up to **₹2 crore for 5 lakh first-time entrepreneurs (women, SC/ST)** over 5 years; Udyam-linked **₹5 lakh MSME credit cards**; and credit guarantee cover for micro and small enterprises raised from ₹5 crore to ₹10 crore — [ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/budget-2025-govt-enhances-msme-investment-limits-and-introduces-credit-cards-with-a-rs-5-lac-limit20250201130822/); [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2098389).
- **Budget 2026 (1 February 2026):** the focus moved to equity and scale-up. It created a **₹10,000 crore SME Growth Fund**, a ₹2,000 crore top-up to the Self-Reliant India Fund, "Corporate Mitras" for compliance help, and GeM–TReDS linkage. These are aimed at existing MSMEs, not pre-revenue founders — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/budget/news/budget-2026-msme-roadmap-equity-liquidity-compliance-changes-126020100659_1.html); [IRIS MSME](https://irismsme.com/blog/union-budget-2026-27-measures-for-msmes/).
- **Scheme-related fraud warning:** state governments (for example, Nagaland) have cautioned against paid "scheme application" agents — [Morung Express](https://mail.morungexpress.com/nagaland-cautions-against-paid-scheme-applications).

### Inferences
- **Realistic within 3 weeks: no.** Even a fast Mudra Shishu approval needs a business plan, a bank relationship and usually a current account. A first-time borrower with no business track record tends to get smaller amounts. More importantly, the founder already has ₹3 lakh, and **borrowing before validation adds repayment risk to an untested idea.**
- **Better sequence:** register Udyam on day one, build 3–6 months of clean, digital sales history in a current account, then apply for Mudra Kishore or the MSME credit card if working capital is the bottleneck (for example, inventory for a proven SKU or corporate-gifting purchase orders).
- **If the founder is a woman (gender unknown):** Stand-Up India and the Budget 2025 first-time-entrepreneur term loan become relevant later, but their minimum sizes (₹10 lakh+) far exceed what a ₹3 lakh test-stage business should borrow.
- **State startup schemes** (for example, Karnataka Elevate or Kerala KSUM grants) generally require DPIIT recognition, an incorporated entity, and long application cycles. They are irrelevant at this stage (inference; not individually researched).

- The official Mudra fee policy (for example, no processing fee on Shishu) was not confirmed from mudra.org.in.
- State-specific startup schemes for the founder's unknown metro were not researched.
- The current operational status of the MSME credit card and the first-time-entrepreneur term-loan scheme is unconfirmed.

## 5. Capital allocation best practice for ₹3 lakh; avoiding fixed costs and franchise or "business opportunity" scams

### Takeaway
Treat ₹3 lakh as a **testing budget with a protected floor**, not as launch capital. Ring-fence about a third as an untouchable reserve. Spend a small fixed amount on legal setup (₹5,000–15,000). Release the test budget in tranches tied to evidence. Avoid rent, deposits, long leases, bulk inventory and any franchise or "distributorship" fee until sales prove demand. Franchise, distributorship and "e-commerce franchise" frauds are active in India in 2025–2026, often impersonating big brands (Tata, Domino's, Burger King).

### Cited Findings
- **Franchise and distributorship frauds (2025–2026):**
  - **July 2025:** a Tamil Nadu businessman was arrested for a **₹25 crore biryani-shop "franchise" scam** that cheated more than 240 people across five states — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!state/rs-dot-25-crore-biryani-shop-franchise-scam-absconding-businessman-arrested-enn25070705006).
  - **24 August 2026:** Mumbai Crime Branch busted a Malad call centre offering **fake courier and e-commerce franchises** and arrested nine people — [Franchise India](https://www.franchiseindia.com/insights/en/news/e-commerce-franchise-fraud-exposed-in-malad-nine-held.59598).
  - **2026:** a Bihar man was arrested for taking **₹15.19 lakh** from a victim by promising distributorships while impersonating Tata Power Solar and Tata 1mg — [The420](https://the420.in/?p=88321).
  - **April 2026:** a person lost **₹2.65 lakh** in a fake Domino's franchise scam — [The Hitavada](https://www.thehitavada.com/Encyc/2026/4/12/man-duped-of-rs-265-lakh-in-pretext-of-franchise-scam-.html).
  - **Patna:** a person lost **₹12 lakh** in a fake Burger King franchise scam — [Patna Press](https://patnapress.com/patna-businessman-duped-of-rs-12-lakh-in-fake-burger-king-franchise-scam/).
  - **Early 2026:** Hyderabad police cases against **Vihaan Direct Selling (QNET's Indian sub-franchise / master distributor)** — [Republic World](https://www.republicworld.com/initiatives/the-fraudster-vihaan-direct-selling-india-pvt-ltd-under-scanner-2026-06-12-128002).
  - A Mumbai firm's CEO was booked for a ₹48.2 lakh franchise fraud (July 2026) — [The Goan](https://www.thegoan.net/goa-news/mumbai-firm-ceo-booked-for-rs-482-lakh-franchise-fraud/150524.html).
- **Fake government-registration sites:** look-alike Udyam portals charge "processing" or "certificate" fees and hand over fake certificates — [StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/msme-ministry-alerts-msmes-to-beware-of-fake-websites-charging-money-in-the-name-of-registration-91048.html).
- **Cash burn as a killer:** the 2025 shutdown analyses say startups "assumed growth could be bought via paid users, endless discounting" and that **high customer acquisition cost** made consumer businesses especially vulnerable — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/729-startups-shut-in-2025-as-regulation-governance-reshape-indias-tech-landscape-86634.htm); [Deccan Founders](https://deccanfounders.com/2025/28/editor_picks/the-harsh-reality-of-indias-startup-ecosystem-why-11000-startups-shut-down-in-2025/).
- CB Insights post-mortems put **running out of cash** among the top two failure reasons (about 29–38% across editions) — [LaunchRock summary of CB Insights](https://www.launchrock.com/library/expert-advice/why-do-startups-fail).

### Inferences
These are the researcher's synthesis of common practice; no single authoritative Indian source was found for a ₹3 lakh allocation template.

- **Suggested allocation of ₹3,00,000 (mentor template):**

  | Bucket | Amount | Rule |
  |---|---|---|
  | Untouchable reserve | ₹1,00,000 (33%) | Not spent on the first idea. It funds a pivot or second attempt, or covers personal emergencies (the founder keeps their salary, so this is business reserve, not living expenses). |
  | Setup and compliance | ₹10,000–15,000 | Udyam ₹0; GST ₹0 official (₹1,000–5,000 if via CA); trademark ₹4,500 in one class (optional, but cheap insurance on a brand name); FSSAI ₹100 for food; Shop & Establishment ₹0–2,500; domain, Shopify/Dukaan or WhatsApp Business setup, and a basic accountant. |
  | Validation phase (weeks 1–3) | ₹30,000–50,000 | Samples or a small first batch, ₹10,000–20,000 of Meta or Instagram test ads, photography and content (which the founder can do), landing page or catalogue, packaging samples. |
  | Tranche 2 (unlocked only if kill criteria pass) | ₹75,000–1,00,000 | Inventory for the winning SKU, more ads, first hire or freelancer. |
  | Buffer for leakage | about ₹20,000 | Returns (RTO on COD can be high), marketplace fees, payment-gateway holds, 0.5% TCS and 0.1% TDS held back. |

- **Fixed-cost rules:**
  - Sign no lease or pay no deposit in the first 90 days. Use home, a cloud kitchen or shared kitchen on revenue share, a co-working day pass, or a 3PL.
  - Pay no annual software contracts; choose monthly plans.
  - Hire no salaried staff; use freelancers per task.
  - Pre-sell or take advance payments before buying inventory, especially for corporate gifting, where 50% advance on purchase orders is common practice (not sourced).
- **Red flags for franchise or "business opportunity" scams:**
  - Unsolicited contact by WhatsApp, Instagram ads or call centres offering franchises of famous brands (Domino's, Tata, Amul and so on).
  - Payment demanded to personal or unrelated accounts, or in fragments ("registration fee", then "NOC fee", then "security deposit").
  - Guaranteed monthly income or buy-back promises.
  - No **FDD-style written disclosure** or a refusal to let you speak to existing franchisees.
  - Domains that are not the brand's official site.
  - Recruiting-based income (MLM / direct selling).
  - Verification steps: check the brand's official website or investor-relations franchise page, check the company on the MCA portal, and never pay before an in-person meeting at the brand's registered office.
- **Avoid "paid courses" and "done-for-you dropshipping store" packages** sold as business opportunities. They are a related trap. This is an inference; it was not researched with sources this session.

### Gaps

- No authoritative Indian return-to-origin (RTO) or COD-return rate figures were gathered in this session (the other researchers may cover them).

## 6. Validation methods before spending: pre-selling, landing pages, WhatsApp/Instagram tests, small batches, Meta Ad Library; kill criteria

### Takeaway
Validate with **money changing hands, not compliments**, inside 7–14 days and for under ₹50,000. Use the Meta Ad Library (free, public) to find competitors with ads running 30+ days, which signals likely profitability. Then pre-sell via WhatsApp or Instagram, a simple landing page, or direct outreach before buying inventory. Set explicit, numeric kill criteria before spending.

### Cited Findings
- **Meta Ad Library:** a free public database of all active ads on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and Audience Network at facebook.com/ads/library, with no login or payment needed. Marketers use it to study competitors and validate products — [AdsUploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-library); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/meta-ad-library/).
- **Method:** list 5–10 direct and adjacent competitors; set the country to India and "All ads"; filter to **active** ads and by platform or media type; **prioritise ads running more than 30 days**, as "they are almost certainly profitable or the advertiser would have stopped them" — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/meta-ad-library-competitor-research).
- **Limits:** for ordinary commercial ads the library shows **no clicks, CTR, conversions, ROAS or spend** — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/meta-ad-library-competitor-research). Ad longevity is only a proxy.
- **Lack of market need is the #1 failure cause:** CB Insights post-mortems cite "no market need" in about **35–42%** of failures — [LaunchRock / CB Insights](https://www.launchrock.com/library/expert-advice/why-do-startups-fail).
- **2025 trend:** more Indian startups shut within 12 months of incorporation (7 in 2025 vs 1 in 2024). This is read as founders "terminating experiments faster when early traction is absent" — [Storyboard18 / Tracxn](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/729-startups-shut-in-2025-as-regulation-governance-reshape-indias-tech-landscape-86634.htm).

### Inferences
Standard lean-validation practice adapted to this founder's sales and content strengths. These are not drawn from a single cited source.

- **Validation playbook by business type:**
  - **Online product:**
    - Days 1–3: Meta Ad Library and Amazon/Meesho bestseller scan, then pick 2–3 SKU candidates.
    - Days 3–7: buy samples (₹5,000–15,000), shoot reels, post on a new Instagram account and personal WhatsApp, collect pre-orders by UPI or a Razorpay or Instamojo payment link with a stated 7–10-day ship date.
    - Days 7–14: spend ₹500–1,000 a day on Meta ads to a landing page or WhatsApp click-to-chat for 7–10 days per SKU.
    - Measure cost per add-to-cart or WhatsApp lead, cost per purchase, and contribution margin after shipping, marketplace fees, RTO and GST.
  - **Local food or service:** pre-sell to a defined micro-market (housing society WhatsApp groups, office colleagues, a weekend pop-up or flea market). Take advance UPI orders for a fixed menu day before investing in equipment. FSSAI basic registration first (about 7 days, ₹100).
  - **Agency, B2B or corporate gifting:** this is the fastest path to revenue for a sales and content founder. Build a 1-page offer and 3 sample pieces, then run 50–100 direct outreaches (LinkedIn, WhatsApp, warm network) in week 1. Validation means a **signed proposal with an advance payment**, not "send me details". Use the Diwali gifting window for corporate gifting (Diwali 2026 falls in early November; verify the exact date), and require 50% advance on purchase orders.
- **Kill or continue criteria. Set these before spending; illustrative thresholds for the mentor to adapt:**
  - **Product:** after ₹15,000–20,000 of ad spend on one SKU, cost per purchase must be below about 30–40% of selling price and contribution margin must be positive after RTO. Otherwise kill the SKU. Fewer than 10 paid pre-orders from the founder's own network and organic posts in 10 days is a strong negative signal.
  - **Service or agency:** fewer than 2 paying clients (or 1 retainer) from 100 qualified outreaches in 3 weeks means rework the offer or price. After two reworks, kill.
  - **Food:** a repeat-order rate below about 20–30% within 2–3 weeks among the first 30 customers is a negative signal.
  - **Global stop-loss:** if the ₹30,000–50,000 validation budget is spent without hitting criteria, stop, write a post-mortem, and choose the next idea using the reserve. Never dip into the ₹1 lakh reserve to "give it one more month."
- Run **two or three cheap parallel tests** (for example, one product SKU and one service offer) rather than one big bet, because the founder's time is constrained by a full-time job.

### Gaps
- No Indian-specific benchmark data was found for Meta ad CPMs or CPCs, or e-commerce conversion rates, in 2026. The other researchers may cover these.
- No sourced studies of pre-selling success rates in India.

## 7. Top reasons small Indian businesses and startups fail in year one (with data) and common first-time-founder mistakes

### Takeaway
Hard Indian data on first-year small-business mortality is thin. The best proxies are these:
- **More than 90% of Indian startups fail within 5 years** (IBM IBV / Oxford Economics, 2017, citing lack of innovation and copycat models).
- **Tracxn counted 729 startup shutdowns in 2025**, down from 978 in 2024, with B2C e-commerce hit hardest.
- **35,567 MSMEs deregistered on Udyam because they shut down in FY 2024-25**, nearly double the 19,828 in FY 2023-24.

The recurring causes are no real market need, cash burn on paid acquisition and discounts, copycat products with no edge, and fixed costs taken on too early.

### Cited Findings
- **IBM Institute for Business Value with Oxford Economics (May 2017):** **more than 90% of Indian startups fail within their first 5 years**. Lack of innovation was the main reason, and **77% of VCs** said Indian startups lean toward mimicry. The survey covered about 1,300 executives, including about 600 founders — [IBM India newsroom](https://in.newsroom.ibm.com/2017-05-18-IBM-Study-Innovation-Key-to-Startup-Success-in-India); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=93664). The data is old (2017) but still the most-cited Indian figure.
- **Tracxn 2025:** **729 startup shutdowns in 2025 vs 978 in 2024**. Seven startups folded within 12 months of incorporation in 2025, vs one in 2024 — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/729-startups-shut-in-2025-as-regulation-governance-reshape-indias-tech-landscape-86634.htm); [ScanX](https://scanx.trade/stock-market-news/stocks/india-s-startup-shutdowns-drop-to-730-in-2025-as-ecosystem-recalibrates-after-funding-winter/28624848).
- **Conflicting larger figure:** other reports say about **11,223 startups folded in 2025** (through October), led by **B2C e-commerce (5,776)**, enterprise software (4,174) and SaaS (2,785) — [Ascendants](https://ascendants.in/industry_events/indian-startup-shutdown-2025-11223-closures/); [Deccan Founders](https://deccanfounders.com/2025/28/editor_picks/the-harsh-reality-of-indias-startup-ecosystem-why-11000-startups-shut-down-in-2025/). The gap reflects different universes (Tracxn-tracked funded startups vs all registered entities). The sector split for the 11,223 figure comes from a lower-authority aggregator.
- **Government figure:** **6,385 DPIIT-recognised startups shut down** (cumulative up to October 2025), per a Winter Session 2025 parliamentary reply — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/winter-session-2025-6-385-recognised-startups-shut-down-till-oct-2025-says-govt).
- **MSME closures (Udyam):** **35,567 MSMEs shut down in FY 2024-25** (to 28 February 2025), vs **19,828 in FY 2023-24**. Maharashtra had the most (8,472), followed by Tamil Nadu (4,412), Gujarat (3,148), Rajasthan (2,989) and Karnataka (2,010). Cumulatively, **71,178 units were deregistered due to shutdown** as of 31 January 2025 — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/economy/over-35000-msmes-closed-in-2024-25-nearly-double-of-last-year); [Lok Sabha reply AU3164](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2973082/1/AU3164.pdf). The government notes that Udyam cancellations also occur for relocation or a change of business, and it does not publish causes for closures — [Lok Sabha replies](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2973082/1/AU3164.pdf).
- **Reasons cited in the 2025 Indian shutdown wave:** growth "bought via paid users, endless discounting and celebrity campaigns" became a liability when funding dried up; weak business models; not enough customers; high CAC and fickle loyalty in consumer internet — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/729-startups-shut-in-2025-as-regulation-governance-reshape-indias-tech-landscape-86634.htm); [Deccan Founders](https://deccanfounders.com/2025/28/editor_picks/the-harsh-reality-of-indias-startup-ecosystem-why-11000-startups-shut-down-in-2025/).
- **Global post-mortems (CB Insights):** **no market need about 35–42%**; **ran out of cash or failed to raise about 29–38%**. Most failures cite several causes — [LaunchRock summary](https://www.launchrock.com/library/expert-advice/why-do-startups-fail).

### Inferences
Common first-time-founder mistakes, mapped to this founder. This is the researcher's synthesis, linked to the cited causes above:

1. **Building or stocking before selling** (the no-market-need cause). Fix: pre-sell, as in section 6.
2. **Copycat product with no edge** (the IBM "mimicry" finding). Fix: compete on content, niche or service, which suits this founder's social media skills, not on price.
3. **Buying growth with ads and discounts before unit economics work** (the 2025 cash-burn pattern). Fix: kill criteria based on cost per purchase vs margin.
4. **Fixed costs too early:** a shop lease, deposit or kitchen fit-out. Fix: section 5 rules.
5. **Paying for "business opportunities"** (franchise, distributorship, dropshipping courses). See section 5.
6. **Compliance shortcuts that later block growth:** selling inter-state without GST (an illegal supply; marketplaces will suspend the account), using ® without registration (penalty up to ₹5 lakh), skipping FSSAI for food.
7. **Mixing personal and business money.** Open a separate current account early. It keeps presumptive-tax digital-receipt proof clean and builds credit history for Mudra later.
8. **Underestimating time:** a full-time job leaves evenings and weekends, so choose a model with low daily operational load (for example, a service or pre-order model rather than daily fresh food delivery) or plan for help.
9. **Ignoring the employment contract** (moonlighting or IP clauses).
10. **Not tracking hidden e-commerce leakage:** RTO, returns, marketplace commissions, TCS/TDS withholding and payment-gateway holds.

### Gaps
- No Indian dataset was found on **first-year survival rates for non-funded micro and small businesses** (shops, home businesses, agencies). The available data covers VC-tracked startups or Udyam deregistrations, and the latter undercounts informal closures.
- Neither the Udyam data nor the startup shutdown data gives a **cause-of-failure breakdown** for Indian micro-businesses. Reasons rely on media analysis and global CB Insights data.
- The IBM figure (2017) is dated. No more recent rigorous Indian cohort study was found.
