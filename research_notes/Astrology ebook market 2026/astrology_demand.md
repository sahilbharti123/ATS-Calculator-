Research date: 3 October 2026. Scope: Western astrology in English-language markets. Vedic/Chinese astrology mentioned only in passing. Source-quality flags: [HIGH] = primary or industry trade source; [MED] = reputable media / publisher self-description; [LOW] = vendor blog, market-research press release, SEO/aggregator site. ## 1. What sales data exists for astrology books in 2024, 2025 and 2026?

### Takeaway
No public source breaks out US or UK astrology book sales for 2024-2026. Circana BookScan and NielsenIQ BookData publish Body, Mind & Spirit (BMS) or astrology figures only to paying clients, and the trade press has not reported them. The public signals point to a category that is flat to soft after the pandemic peak: Canadian BMS print sales fell steeply in Q1 2024 and Q1 2025, Ireland's MBS General was down 7% in 2025, and the US trade press describes publishers turning toward practical "how-to" titles, while self-help (+14.7%) and religion (+5.4%) grew in the US in 2025.

### Cited Findings
**US market context (Circana BookScan, 2025)**
- US print unit sales were 762.4 million in 2025 (year ended Dec 27, 2025), up 0.3% on 2024. Adult nonfiction units fell 1.5%, self-help rose 14.7% (led by Mel Robbins), religion rose 5.4% to nearly 67 million units, memoir fell 7% and business fell 3.9%. The article gives **no Body/Mind/Spirit, occult or astrology figure**. [HIGH] — [Publishers Weekly, "Print Book Sales Rose Slightly in 2025," Jan 9, 2026](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/financial-reporting/article/99417-print-book-sales-rose-slightly-in-2025.html)

### Inferences
- The US data suggests readers' "inner life" spending in 2025 went to self-help (+14.7%) and religion (+5.4%) rather than visibly to astrology. Astrology looks like a stable niche, not a growth engine, in print.
- Canadian BMS fell year on year in both Q1 2024 and Q1 2025, and Ireland's MBS General fell 7% in 2025. Together these suggest the 2020-2021 surge has unwound in at least part of the English-language market. Whether the US and UK follow the same pattern is unconfirmed.
- Divination being the largest BMS slice (22%) means astrology and tarot are core sellers within a softening category. A new entrant competes for a stable pool of readers rather than riding a rising tide.
- The 2020 copy counts (about 12,000-15,000 for a strong traditionally published astrology or witchcraft title in a peak year) give a realistic ceiling for mid-list titles. A self-published ebook would typically sell far fewer.

### Gaps
- No public Circana BookScan BMS/OCC (occult) or astrology unit or dollar figures for 2024, 2025 or 2026 were found. PW and Publishers Lunch did not report them in accessible articles.
- No public NielsenIQ UK MBS value or volume for 2024-2026, and no Bookseller article on astrology sales, were found (thebookseller.com searches returned nothing relevant).
- No 2025-2026 sales comments from Chronicle Books, Ten Speed, Hay House or Penguin's astrology lines were found.
- No data on ebook versus print share for astrology specifically. BookNet's data is print only.

## 3. Who are astrology readers? Demographics and attitudes

### Takeaway
About 3 in 10 US adults engage with astrology at least yearly, but most do so "for fun." Belief has been flat at roughly 27-29% since 2017. The core audience is women under 50 (46% consult astrology yearly), adults aged 18-29, and LGBTQ+ adults (54% consult yearly). Lower-income and non-college adults are more likely to believe. UK belief is markedly lower than US belief.

### Cited Findings
**Pew Research Center** (survey of 9,593 US adults, Oct 21-27, 2024, American Trends Panel; published May 21, 2025) [HIGH] — [Pew, "3 in 10 Americans consult astrology, tarot cards or fortune tellers"](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/); [full report PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/05/PR_2025.05.21_astrology-tarot-cards-fortune-tellers_report.pdf)
- **Overall use:** 30% consult astrology, tarot or fortune tellers at least yearly. By practice, astrology/horoscopes 28%, tarot 11%, fortune tellers 6%. 6% engage at least weekly and 14% at least monthly.
- **Belief:** 27% believe in astrology ("the position of the stars and planets can affect people's lives"). The Pew page gives 29% in 2017 and calls the change not significant; AP-syndicated coverage describes the rate as essentially unchanged ([NY1/AP, May 21, 2025](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/ap-top-news/2025/05/21/how-many-americans-believe-in-astrology-and-rely-on-fortune-telling-a-new-survey-has-answers)).
- **Gender:**
  - Belief: women 35%, men 18%.
  - Women 18-49: 43% believe; women 50+: 27%; men 18-49: 20%; men 50+: 16%.
  - Women consulting astrology yearly: 37% overall, 46% of women 18-49.
- **Age:**
  - Belief: 33% of ages 18-29 versus 17% of ages 65+.
  - Tarot: nearly 25% of ages 18-29 consult tarot yearly, versus 6% of ages 50-64 and 3% of 65+.
- **LGBTQ+ adults:** 54% consult astrology yearly (LGBT women 63%, LGBT men 40%). 33% consult tarot. 21% rely at least "a little" on these practices for major decisions.
- **Income and education:** 37% of lower-income adults believe, versus 16% of upper-income adults. College graduates are less likely to believe.
- **Race and religion:**
  - Black, Hispanic and Asian Americans are more likely than White Americans to rely on these practices for major decisions (11%, 9% and 12% respectively, versus 4%).
  - Religiously affiliated (27%) and unaffiliated (28%) adults believe at similar rates.
  - 39% of Hispanic Catholics consult astrology.
- **Motivation:** 20% of all adults engage "mostly for fun" versus 10% mostly for "helpful insights." Only 1% rely "a lot" on these practices for major life decisions and 5% "a little." Among younger adults, 13% rely at least a little.
- **Politics:** liberals are more likely to consult. Conservatives' belief fell 4 points since 2017, the only significant subgroup change.

**YouGov (US)**
- **Apr 2022** (3,472 US adults): 27% believe in astrology, 51% do not, 22% unsure. Belief is 37% among under-30s and 16% among 65+. 90% of Americans know their astrological sign. [HIGH, 2022] — [YouGov, "One in four Americans say they believe in astrology," Apr 26, 2022](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/42292-one-four-americans-say-they-believe-astrology)
  - Note: unlike Pew, YouGov found men under 45 (38%) more likely than women under 45 (32%) to believe. This conflicts with Pew's strong female skew.
- **Older, undated (~2015):** 14% of Americans think horoscopes can tell the future; 30% think star signs reveal personality. — [YouGov US](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/12747-14-believe-horoscopes-can-tell-future)

**UK**
- **Older, undated (~2015):** only 8% of Britons believe horoscopes can predict the future. [MED, dated] — [YouGov UK](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/12731-8-of-Britons-believe-horoscopes-predict-the-future)
- British Religion in Numbers has a "Who believes in horoscopes?" analysis, which was not retrieved. — [BRIN](https://www.brin.ac.uk/who-believes-in-horoscopes/)

**Generational survey (non-probability)**
- A Feb 2024 survey reported by LSN Global found Millennials lead interest: 83% call themselves believers versus 62% of Gen Z, and 81% of Millennials have consulted or would consult the stars for relationship guidance versus 59% of Gen Z. LSN also notes that interest has hit "saturation," with many finding astrology talk "annoying." [LOW-MED; survey sponsor and method unknown] — [LSN Global](https://www.lsnglobal.com/article/view/30716)

### Inferences
- **Core buyer persona:** a woman aged 18-49, often LGBTQ+, more likely without a degree and on a lower-to-middle income, who uses astrology recreationally and for self-reflection rather than prediction. Pricing and tone should suit casual, "for fun" readers ($2.99-$6.99 ebooks, warm and practical voice) rather than technical students.
- Roughly 28% of US adults (about 70 million people) consult astrology yearly, but only about 10% engage for insight. The committed reader who buys a second or third astrology book is likely in the single-digit percentages of adults. That is still millions of people in the US alone.
- The UK addressable audience is proportionally smaller (8% believing horoscopes predict the future, ~2015, versus 14% in the US). The US should be the primary market.
- Pew's finding that belief is flat since 2017 indicates a stable, non-growing population of believers. Growth for an author comes from capturing share or riding transit news cycles, not from market expansion.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 YouGov, Gallup or Harris astrology polls were found. Gallup last asked about astrology in 2005 (not retrieved).
- No recent UK probability survey on astrology use was retrieved.
- No data links astrology belief to book buying specifically, such as the share of believers who buy astrology books.

## 4. What do digital signals show (apps, Google Trends, TikTok, podcasts)? Growing, plateauing or declining since 2020-2021?

### Takeaway
Digital interest peaked around 2020-2021 and has since plateaued at a high base. Searches for zodiac signs and birth charts hit a five-year high in 2020. The leading apps have shifted to aggressive paywalls rather than user growth, and Nebula has broadened into "spiritual guidance." TikTok astrology remains huge in absolute terms (#astrology has about 70 billion views). App revenue figures in circulation come mostly from low-quality vendor blogs and conflict wildly.

### Cited Findings
**Search trends**
- Online searches for zodiac signs and birth charts hit a **five-year peak in 2020** on Google Trends. Co-Star had 20M+ downloads by 2021 and had been downloaded by about a quarter of US women aged 18-25. [HIGH, 2021] — [TIME, "Why Personalized Astrology Apps Are Appealing to Gen Z," 2021](https://time.com/6083293/astrology-apps-personalized/)
- US Google searches for "astrology" were about 2× searches for "astronomy" in 2004 and about 5× by early 2022. "Horoscope" outnumbered "astronomy" about 15:1. [MED; attribution unclear in search snippet] — [Genetic Literacy Project, Sept 30, 2026](https://geneticliteracyproject.org/2026/09/30/astrology-homeopathy-vaccine-rejectionism-creationism-why-pseudoscience-thrives-as-science-advances/)
- Google's 2025 Year in Search top trends (Gemini, India vs England, Charlie Kirk) did not feature astrology. [MED] — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-reveals-the-top-searches-of-2025/563738/)

**Apps**

## 5. Which astrological events in 2026, 2027 and 2028 are astrologers and media hyping?

### Takeaway
2026 has been sold across astrology media as an "unprecedented" year:
- Neptune entered Aries (Jan 26, 2026).
- Saturn entered Aries (Feb 13, 2026), and the two met in an exact conjunction at about 0° Aries (Feb 20, 2026).
- Uranus entered Gemini (Apr 25, 2026).
- Jupiter entered Leo (Jun 29, 2026).
- New eclipse axes began, and the US Uranus return coincided with the July 4 semiquincentennial.

Several of these are multi-year transits. Neptune stays in Aries to 2038/39, Uranus in Gemini to 2032/33, Pluto in Aquarius to 2044, and Saturn in Aries to April 2028. 2027 brings Jupiter into Virgo (Jul 26) and the century's longest total solar eclipse (Aug 2, 2027, in Leo). ### Inferences
- Tarot reaches a younger, narrower audience than astrology. Its cultural momentum, as shown by publisher deck output and PW coverage, has been at least as strong as astrology's since 2015. Astrology-plus-tarot bundles (for example, a tarot spread for each transit or sign) can tap both audiences.
- Human Design is the clearest rising crossover. It already sits in Amazon's astrology category, and a "Human Design + astrology" ebook could reach an audience with less saturated competition than sun-sign books. This rests on qualitative evidence only.
- The "practical magic / how-to" shift (PW 2025) and self-help growth (+14.7%) suggest astrology ebooks framed as practical self-improvement tools (career, relationships, timing decisions, journaling workbooks) align better with current demand than abstract or predictive titles.
- Ebooks cannot ship deck-plus-book products, which are Moonology's format advantage. A self-publisher could approximate them with printable spreads, journal prompts or companion PDFs.
