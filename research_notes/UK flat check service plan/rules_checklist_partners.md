# UK "flat check before you pay": rules, checklist and partner model

**Status date:** 3 October 2026. **Main focus:** England, with notes for Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
**Who this is for:** a founder in India, working as an Indian sole proprietor, who will pay local UK partners per visit to check private flats and rooms for students and new arrivals.

**How sure is each point?** Every fact has a link next to it. Some points are also tagged:
- **[Official]**: taken from gov.uk, legislation.gov.uk, a council, a regulator or the RBI.
- **[Secondary]**: taken from a law firm, a university, a news site or a company page. It is probably right, but check it before you rely on it.
- **[Uncertain]**: I could not confirm this, or the law is unclear. Ask a UK lawyer, an Indian CA or the official body.

This is research, not legal advice. Laws in this area changed a lot in 2025–2026.

## 0. The 12 things to remember

1. **England's renting rules changed on 1 May 2026.** Fixed-term "assured shorthold" tenancies became rolling "assured periodic" tenancies, and "no-fault" (section 21) evictions ended ([gov.uk roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/renters-rights-act-2025-implementation-roadmap/implementing-the-renters-rights-act-2025-our-roadmap-for-reforming-the-private-rented-sector)). [Official]
2. **A big upfront rent request is now a red flag in England.** For most private tenancies, a landlord can only ask for up to one month's rent, and only after the tenancy agreement is signed and before it starts ([gov.uk guide to the Act](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act)). Asking for 6–12 months' rent before signing is now unlawful for these tenancies. Private purpose-built student halls are exempt ([gov.uk roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/renters-rights-act-2025-implementation-roadmap/implementing-the-renters-rights-act-2025-our-roadmap-for-reforming-the-private-rented-sector)). [Official]
3. **The holding deposit is capped at 1 week's rent. The main deposit is capped at 5 weeks' rent** when yearly rent is under £50,000 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tenant-fees-act-2019-guidance-for-tenants)). [Official]
4. **The landlord must protect the deposit in a government scheme within 30 days**: DPS, mydeposits or TDS ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection)). [Official]
5. **Checking who owns the flat costs £7** for a title register from HM Land Registry, which covers England and Wales ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/get-information-about-property-and-land/search-the-register)). [Official]
6. **Student visa holders cannot be self-employed** ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa)). Do not pay students per visit as freelancers. [Official]
7. **Graduate visa holders can be self-employed** ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa)). They, together with British, Irish and settled people, are your best freelance partners. [Official]
8. **From 1 October 2026, right-to-work checks also cover freelancers and gig workers in the UK.** Fines are up to £45,000 per illegal worker, or £60,000 for repeat breaches ([Capsticks](https://www.capsticks.com/insights/right-to-work-checks-to-expand-beyond-employees-on-1-october-2026)). The online check is free to run ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/view-right-to-work)). [Secondary + Official]
9. **You do not need client money protection if you never hold rent or deposits** ([gov.uk CMP guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mandatory-client-money-protection)). Whether you must join a property redress scheme is a grey area, and membership costs about £155–£280 a year ([Property Redress](https://www.propertyredress.co.uk/business), [TPO](https://www.tpos.co.uk/businesses/)). [Uncertain]
10. **The UK data protection fee is £52 a year for micro businesses** (Tier 1, since February 2025) ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/06/fee-payable-to-the-ico-to-increase-in-february-102jzb9)). Whether a business based only in India must pay it is unclear. [Secondary/Uncertain]
11. **India's data protection rules (DPDP) mostly apply from about May 2027**, 18 months after they were notified on 13 November 2025 ([AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)). [Secondary]
12. **A UK company costs £100 to set up online.** It needs a real UK address, and its directors must verify their identity ([gov.uk fees](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-fees/companies-house-fees), [gov.uk address](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/company-address), [gov.uk ID](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/verify-your-identity-for-companies-house)). [Official]

## 1. Tenant protection rules a checker can verify (England first)

### 1.1 The big change: Renters' Rights Act 2025 (England)

**Status:** the Act became law on 27 October 2025 ([gov.uk guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act)). Most tenant changes started on **1 May 2026** ([gov.uk roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/renters-rights-act-2025-implementation-roadmap/implementing-the-renters-rights-act-2025-our-roadmap-for-reforming-the-private-rented-sector)). [Official]

What changed on 1 May 2026 (all from the [gov.uk roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/renters-rights-act-2025-implementation-roadmap/implementing-the-renters-rights-act-2025-our-roadmap-for-reforming-the-private-rented-sector) unless another link is given):
- Section 21 "no-fault" evictions are abolished. Tenancies are now rolling (periodic), and tenants can leave with **2 months' notice**.
- **No rental bidding.** The landlord must publish an asking rent and cannot accept offers above it ([gov.uk guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act)).
- **Rent in advance is limited.** The landlord can ask for no more than **1 month's rent**, and only **after the tenancy agreement is signed and before it starts** ([gov.uk guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act)).
- Rent can only go up once a year, with 2 months' notice.
- Landlords cannot discriminate against tenants with children or tenants on benefits.
- **Private purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA) is exempt** from the new tenancy system.

Later stages:
- **PRS Database.** All landlords will have to register here. Regional rollout starts "late 2026" ([gov.uk roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/renters-rights-act-2025-implementation-roadmap/implementing-the-renters-rights-act-2025-our-roadmap-for-reforming-the-private-rented-sector)). **[Uncertain]** I could not confirm whether any area is live yet in October 2026. Once it is, "Is the landlord on the database?" becomes a strong check.
- **Landlord Ombudsman.** Expected in 2028 (same source).
- **Fines.** Councils can fine up to £7,000 for a first breach and up to £40,000 for serious or repeat breaches ([gov.uk guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act)).

**Why this matters for your customers.** Many international students used to pay several months or a whole year upfront. Student unions now warn that landlords may ask for a **UK guarantor** instead ([Durham SU](https://ask.durhamsu.com/support/solutions/articles/202000105942-advance-rent-what-landlords-can-ask-for-and-what-is-changing), [RHUL SU](https://www.su.rhul.ac.uk/news/article/surhul/-What-the-New-Renters-Rights-Act/)). [Secondary] Your report should say clearly whether the request is lawful.

**[Uncertain]** Rooms let by a landlord who lives in the same home ("lodger" arrangements) are usually not assured tenancies, so the rent-in-advance cap may not apply to them. Check this per case.

### 1.2 Fees and holding deposits (Tenant Fees Act 2019, England)

All points below are from [gov.uk guidance for tenants](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tenant-fees-act-2019-guidance-for-tenants) (updated 7 July 2026). [Official]

- **Holding deposit:** at most **1 week's rent**. The landlord can hold it for **15 days** unless you both agree a different time in writing. It must be returned within **7 days**. The landlord can keep it only if the tenant gave false information, pulled out, did not reply, or failed a Right to Rent check. If they keep it, they must say why in writing within 7 days.
- **Main deposit:** at most **5 weeks' rent** if yearly rent is under £50,000, or **6 weeks** if it is £50,000–£100,000.
- **Allowed payments:** rent, the refundable deposit, the refundable holding deposit, a fee to change the tenancy (£50, or higher if reasonable costs are shown), a payment for leaving early, bills, late rent fees, and replacing lost keys.
- **Banned:** almost everything else. Examples are inventory fees and admin fees. A landlord also cannot make the tenant buy a "professional guarantor service", contents insurance or a deposit replacement product, or "start a contract for a service as part of your tenancy".
- **Who to complain to:** the local council (which enforces the rules), the First-tier Tribunal, the agent's redress scheme, Shelter or Citizens Advice.

**Checker test:** ask for a written list of every payment and its amount. Compare it with the allowed list above. Any "viewing fee", "admin fee", "reservation fee" or non-refundable charge is a red flag.

### 1.3 Deposit protection (England and Wales)

**The rules** ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection), [gov.uk full guide](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection/print)). [Official]
- The deposit must go into a government scheme **within 30 days**. The schemes are the **Deposit Protection Service**, **mydeposits** and the **Tenancy Deposit Scheme**.
- Within 30 days the landlord must also give the tenant "prescribed information": the address, the deposit amount, which scheme protects it, how disputes work, and why money might be kept.
- If the landlord does not protect it, a court can order up to **3 times the deposit** to be paid to the tenant.
- The deposit must come back within **10 days** of both sides agreeing the amount.

**How to check, and what it costs:**
- *Before paying:* ask which scheme will be used, and get it in writing in the tenancy agreement.
- *After paying:* the tenant can check on the scheme's own website or phone line. The phone numbers are DPS 0330 303 0030, mydeposits 0333 321 9401 and TDS 0300 037 1000 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection/print)). The check is free.
- **Limit:** a checker cannot confirm protection before the deposit is paid. Offer a "day-30 follow-up" as an add-on.

### 1.4 HMO licensing (shared houses)

**The rules** ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/house-in-multiple-occupation-licence), England and Wales). [Official]
- An **HMO** (house in multiple occupation) is a home rented by at least 3 people who are not one household and who share a kitchen or bathroom.
- A **mandatory licence** is needed when **5 or more people from more than 1 household** share facilities.
- Many councils also license smaller HMOs, so check local rules.
- A licence lasts at most 5 years. Renting out an unlicensed HMO can lead to an **unlimited fine**.

**How to check:** every council must keep a **public register** of licensed properties under the Housing Act 2004 ([South Tyneside](https://www.southtyneside.gov.uk/article/7313/Checking-a-HMO-licence)). There is **no single national register**. Search the register of the council for that area. Examples are [Croydon](https://www.croydon.gov.uk/housing/landlords/houses-multiple-occupation-hmo/check-whether-hmo-licensed) and [Waltham Forest](https://www.walthamforest.gov.uk/housing/private-sector-housing/private-rented-property-licensing-prpl/our-public-register-licensed-properties). Some councils only share it by email, as a PDF or in person, and may charge for it. It is usually free online. [Official]

**Checker test:** count bedrooms and occupants. If 5 or more unrelated people will live there, look the address up on the council register. Note the licence number, the licence holder's name and the expiry date.

### 1.5 Gas safety

**The rules** ([gov.uk private renting](https://www.gov.uk/private-renting/print)). [Official]
- A **Gas Safe registered engineer** must check each gas appliance and flue **every year**.
- The tenant must get a copy of the gas safety record **before moving in**, or within 28 days of the check.

**Checker test:** photograph the current gas safety record. Check that it is dated within the last 12 months, the address matches, and the engineer's Gas Safe number is shown. Then look up that number on the Gas Safe Register website (free). **[Uncertain]** The Gas Safe Register site blocked my automated access, so I could not re-check how its lookup works today.

### 1.6 EPC (energy rating)

**The rules** ([gov.uk MEES guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/domestic-private-rented-property-minimum-energy-efficiency-standard-landlord-guidance), updated 5 May 2026). [Official]
- An EPC (Energy Performance Certificate) rates how energy-efficient a home is, from A (best) to G (worst).
- A private rental must have an EPC rating of at least **E**. Homes rated F or G cannot be let unless there is a registered exemption.
- The **PRS Exemptions Register is public**, so anyone can search for exempt properties and for penalties.
- Fines are up to £5,000 per property.

**Coming next:** from **1 October 2030**, private rentals must reach **EPC C**. This was confirmed in January 2026 alongside the Warm Homes Plan ([Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmkws6657001itp58cb0i2cse/the-warm-homes-plan-mees-for-privately-rented-homes-epc-c-by-2030), [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/en-gb/out-law/news/epc-reforms-mees-private-rented-property-confirmed)). [Secondary]

**How to check:** search by postcode on [gov.uk Find an energy certificate](https://www.gov.uk/find-energy-certificate), which covers England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland has its own register at [scottishepcregister.org.uk](https://www.scottishepcregister.org.uk/). It is free. A low rating (E, F or G) also means high heating bills, so put it in the report.

### 1.7 Electrical safety (EICR)

An EICR (Electrical Installation Condition Report) is an electrician's safety report on the wiring.

**The rules** (council guidance, e.g. [Wandsworth](https://www.wandsworth.gov.uk/housing/private-housing/private-housing-landlords/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-rented-sector/), [Hull](https://www.hull.gov.uk/private-landlords/electrical-safety-standards)). [Official]
- The wiring must be inspected by a qualified person **at least every 5 years**.
- New tenants must get the report **before they move in**. A **prospective tenant can ask for it and must get it within 28 days**.
- Any urgent work must be done within 28 days.
- Fines are up to **£30,000**.

**Checker test:** ask for the EICR and photograph it. Check the date (under 5 years old), the address, and the overall result: "Satisfactory", or "Unsatisfactory" with proof the repairs were done.

### 1.8 Smoke and carbon monoxide (CO) alarms

**The rules** ([gov.uk Q&A booklet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-alarms-explanatory-booklet-for-landlords/the-smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-alarm-england-regulations-2015-qa-booklet-for-the-private-rented-sector-landlords-and-tenants), rules in force since 1 October 2022). [Official]
- There must be at least one **smoke alarm on every storey** with living space.
- There must be a **CO alarm in any room with a fixed appliance that burns fuel**, such as a gas boiler or wood stove. Gas cookers do not count.
- Alarms must work on the day the tenancy starts, and must be repaired once the landlord is told about a fault.
- Battery alarms are allowed.

**Checker test:** with the landlord's permission, press the test button on each alarm on video. Note any boiler or fire without a CO alarm.

### 1.9 Right to Rent (England only)

**The rules** ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/check-tenant-right-to-rent-documents)). [Official]
- Landlords in England must check that **every adult tenant** has the right to rent. People on visas usually show this with an online **share code**, including students with an eVisa.
- For people whose visa runs out at a set date, the check must happen in the 28 days before the tenancy starts.
- The fine is up to £20,000 per tenant.
- Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland do not have this check.

**What it means for the checker:** a real landlord or agent will normally ask your customer for a share code before signing. A "landlord" who never asks for anything, and only wants money, is acting informally at best. This is a soft signal, not proof of a scam.

### 1.10 Does this person really own or control the property?

**Land Registry** ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/get-information-about-property-and-land/search-the-register), [service](https://www.gov.uk/search-property-information-land-registry)). [Official]
- A free **property summary** is available.
- A **title register** costs **£7** and a **title plan** costs £7. Official paper copies cost £11.
- This covers England and Wales. Records cover most property sold since 1993.
- The title register shows the owner's name. Compare it with the landlord's ID. Ask to see the original ID on the video call, but do not keep a copy (see section 5).

**If the names do not match**, the person may be a letting agent, a property manager or a "rent-to-rent" middleman, or a scammer. Ask for written proof that they are allowed to let it, such as the owner's signed authority or a management agreement. Then contact the owner or agent named on the title or the original listing **independently**: find their details yourself, never through the person you are checking.

**If the landlord is a company**, look it up free on [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/). Check that it is active and see who the directors are.

**If a letting agent is involved:**
- Agents in England must belong to a **redress scheme**, a complaints service such as [The Property Ombudsman](https://www.tpos.co.uk/businesses/) or [Property Redress](https://www.propertyredress.co.uk/business) ([legal basis: ERRA 2013 s.83](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2013/24/section/83)).
- Agents who **hold client money must belong to a Client Money Protection (CMP) scheme** and show the certificate on their website and in their office ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mandatory-client-money-protection); [summary](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/client-money-protection-letting-agents/)).
- **Checker test:** find the agent's redress and CMP certificate numbers, then confirm them on the scheme's own website.

### 1.11 Practical (non-legal) things the partner should check

These are good practice rather than legal rules:
- Does the property exist, and does it match the advert photos?
- Do the keys open the door?
- Is there damp or mould? Do the heating and hot water work?
- What is the water pressure like? Do the windows and locks work? Is there phone signal?
- Is there a bike or bin store?
- How far is it from the university? What is the street like at night?
- What are the meter readings? Who else lives there now?

## 2. Scams aimed at students, and what the checker should test

### 2.1 Common scam patterns
- **Fake or "already let" listings.** Ads offer homes that are already rented, not owned by the "landlord", or that do not exist. The scammer says it is "in high demand" and asks for a fee or deposit before any viewing ([Action Fraud](https://www.actionfraud.police.uk/news/students-scammed-into-renting-bogus-houses-and-flats)). [Official]
- **Pay before viewing.** Action Fraud's advice is: "do not pay any money until you, or a reliable contact, has visited the property". It specifically tells students arriving from overseas to get a friend or contact to check the property exists ([Action Fraud](https://www.actionfraud.police.uk/news/students-urged-to-watch-out-for-rental-scams)). **This is your service's core reason to exist.** [Official]
- **Cloned photos and ads.** Photos are copied from real listings. A reverse image search (Google Lens, TinEye) can catch them ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/4-ways-to-spot-a-rental-scam-a206R0v7tjRF)). [Secondary]
- **Rent too good to be true**, much lower than similar homes nearby ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/4-ways-to-spot-a-rental-scam-a206R0v7tjRF)).
- **Payee name does not match.** The bank account is in a different name from the landlord or agent ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/4-ways-to-spot-a-rental-scam-a206R0v7tjRF)).
- **Unusual payment methods.** Requests for cash, crypto, gift cards ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/4-ways-to-spot-a-rental-scam-a206R0v7tjRF)) or money-transfer services like Western Union, or transfers to an account abroad ([Minnesota AG guide](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Brochures/pubRentalListingScams.pdf)). That guide is from the US, but it describes the same pattern.
- **"I'm abroad, I'll post you the keys."** The fake landlord says they cannot show the flat and will post the keys after payment ([Swiss NCSC](https://www.ncsc.admin.ch/ncsc/en/home/aktuell/im-fokus/2025/wochenrueckblick_4.html)). [Secondary]
- **Size of the problem.** More than £4.2 million was lost to rental fraud in 2023, with an average loss of £1,720 per victim ([Which?, Sept 2024](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/4-ways-to-spot-a-rental-scam-a206R0v7tjRF)). [Secondary] **[Uncertain]** I did not find official UK figures for 2025–2026.
- **New since 1 May 2026, England:** asking for **more than 1 month's rent upfront**, or any rent **before signing**, or asking for **bids above the advertised rent** is now unlawful for most private tenancies ([gov.uk guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act)). Treat it as both a legal problem and a scam signal.

**Where to report:** since 4 December 2025, **Report Fraud** has replaced Action Fraud. Report online at reportfraud.police.uk or call 0300 123 2040 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/report-fraud-new-service-from-city-of-london-police)). [Official]

### 2.2 Red-flag tests for every check

| # | Test | How | Pass | Fail or warning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the listing a copy? | Reverse image search of the ad photos; search the address on Rightmove, Zoopla, SpareRoom and OpenRent | Photos are only on this one listing | The same photos appear under another agent or city |
| 2 | Does the place exist and match? | Partner visits; Street View before the visit | Same building, rooms and furniture | Different building, or no access |
| 3 | Can they get in? | Landlord or agent opens the door with keys, live on video | Opens normally | "Keys are with a friend", "I'm abroad" |
| 4 | Who owns it? | £7 Land Registry title register (1.10) | Owner's name matches the landlord's ID, or written authority exists | Names do not match and there is no authority |
| 5 | Is the agent real? | Redress and CMP certificate looked up on the scheme's own site | Listed | Not listed, or the certificate number is fake |
| 6 | Is the landlord registered? (Scotland, Wales, NI, and England once the database is live) | Public register search (1.12) | Registered | Not registered |
| 7 | Is the money request lawful? | Compare it with 1.1–1.3 | ≤1 week holding deposit; ≤5 weeks main deposit; ≤1 month rent, only after signing | Viewing fee, admin fee, months of rent upfront, bidding |
| 8 | Is the payment route safe? | Ask for the account name and method | UK bank account in the landlord's or agent's name | Crypto, gift cards, Western Union, personal account in another name, overseas account |
| 9 | Is there pressure? | Note what they say | Normal timeline | "Pay today or lose it", refuses a video call |
| 10 | Are the safety papers there? | Gas record, EICR, EPC, alarms (1.5–1.8) | All shown and in date | Missing or out of date (report it as "not provided") |
| 11 | Will the deposit be protected? | Scheme named in the contract | Named scheme | "We don't use schemes" |
| 12 | Is HMO licensing needed? | Occupant count and council register | Licensed, or not needed | Needs a licence and is not on the register |

**Report wording rule:** report facts ("the owner named on the title register is X; the person we met gave the name Y"). Never write "this is a scammer". This lowers your risk of being sued for defamation. [Practical advice]

## 3. Who can legally be a partner, and how to pay them

### 3.1 Visa rules for the people you want to hire

| Person | Can they freelance (be self-employed)? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Student visa** | **No.** gov.uk says students "cannot be self-employed" ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa)). Freelancing and gig work, including apps like Deliveroo or Uber, count as self-employment ([University of Exeter](https://www.exeter.ac.uk/international-students/visas/student/working-during-study/)). | They **can be employed** for up to **20 hours a week in term time** (degree level) and full time in vacations ([Exeter](https://www.exeter.ac.uk/international-students/visas/student/working-during-study/)). Breaking the rules can mean the visa is cancelled ([Exeter](https://www.exeter.ac.uk/students/international/visaconditions/workingintheuk/)). |
| **Graduate visa** | **Yes.** "Work in most jobs … be self-employed" ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa)) | Lasts 2 years if the person applies by 31 Dec 2026, or **18 months if they apply from 1 Jan 2027** (3 years for PhDs). It cannot be extended ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa)). Expect partner turnover. |
| **Skilled Worker visa** | Only in limited cases. Up to 20 hours a week in another job or their own business, but only in eligible higher-skilled jobs, jobs on the immigration salary list, or the same sector and level as their main job. Business admin counts toward the 20 hours ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/second-job)). | Property viewing work is very unlikely to qualify. **Avoid.** |
| **Dependants of students** | Only PhD/research students and government-sponsored students can now bring dependants ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/family-members)). | **[Uncertain]** I did not confirm their work rights. Rely on the share code check below, which shows their conditions. |
| **British, Irish, settled or pre-settled (EUSS), indefinite leave to remain (ILR)** | Generally yes, with no immigration limits. **[Uncertain]**: general knowledge, not re-checked this round. | British and Irish citizens cannot get a share code, so check their passport instead ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/view-right-to-work)). |

### 3.2 New rule from 1 October 2026: right-to-work checks for freelancers

- The Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act 2025 became law on 2 December 2025. **Section 48 started on 1 October 2026**. It extends right-to-work checks to businesses that use **individual subcontractors, casual or gig workers and online matching platforms** ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/blogs/be-aware-uk/2026/right-to-work-compliance-expands-beyond-employees), [Capsticks](https://www.capsticks.com/insights/right-to-work-checks-to-expand-beyond-employees-on-1-october-2026)). [Secondary]
- Fines are **£45,000 per illegal worker**, rising to **£60,000** for repeat breaches ([Capsticks](https://www.capsticks.com/insights/right-to-work-checks-to-expand-beyond-employees-on-1-october-2026)). [Secondary]
- **[Uncertain]** I could not confirm whether these fines can reach a business based only in India. **Do the check anyway.** It is free, takes minutes, and protects your partners and your brand.
- **How:** the partner gives you a share code and their date of birth. You check it at [gov.uk/view-right-to-work](https://www.gov.uk/view-right-to-work), which shows "the types of work they're allowed to do" ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/view-right-to-work)). Save a copy of the result.

### 3.3 Four ways to set up partners

**Option A: use an existing viewing marketplace (fastest and lowest risk).**
- [Viewber](https://viewber.co.uk/how-viewber-works) runs a UK network of vetted, DBS-checked local members. They do viewings, condition reports, photos and live-streamed visits, and are paid per job.
- An older trade article says members were paid **£10–£20 per viewing plus travel**, and a standard 30-minute viewing cost the client **£19** ([The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/revealed-viewber-pricing-agent-payments-work/)). **[Uncertain]** That article is several years old, so prices have probably changed.
- **Upside:** they handle recruiting, vetting and paying people in the UK.
- **Downside:** less control over report quality, and a lower margin. **[Uncertain]** Check whether their terms allow you to resell their visits under your own brand.

**Option B: recruit your own self-employed partners, but only people who are allowed to freelance.**
- Hire Graduate visa holders and British, Irish or settled people. Do the share code or passport check first (3.2).
- Ask them to get a **basic DBS check** (a criminal record check). It costs **£21.50**, they apply themselves, and it usually takes about 3 days ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/request-copy-criminal-record)).
- They report their own income to HMRC as self-employed ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/working-for-yourself)).
- **Employment status risk.** If you control their hours, stop them sending someone else, and supervise them closely, they may legally be "workers". Workers are owed the minimum wage and holiday pay ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/employment-status/worker)). Keep it a true per-job, accept-or-decline arrangement.

**Option C: employ people, including students, through a UK umbrella company or employer of record (EOR).**
- An umbrella company or EOR becomes the legal employer and runs UK payroll for you. This is the only legal way to use **Student visa** holders: as employees, within their 20-hour limit.
- They must be paid at least the minimum wage: **£12.71 an hour (age 21+) and £10.85 (age 18–20)** from April 2026 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates)).
- **Costs:** umbrella fees are about £15–£35 a week per person ([GoForma](https://www.goforma.com/umbrella-company/umbrella-company-fees)). EOR fees are about **£150–£550 a month per employee** ([Employment Hero](https://employmenthero.com/uk/blog/employer-of-record-pricing/)). [Secondary]
- Employer National Insurance is charged on top.
- **This is too expensive for a few visits a month.** Revisit it at scale.
- A foreign business can also register directly with HMRC as a "non-resident employer" and run UK payroll itself ([Parakar](https://parakar.eu/knowledge/uk/nrp-in-the-uk)). [Secondary] This is complex, so it is not for year 1.

**Option D: partner with university student unions or student accommodation services**, for referrals only, not as paid partners. **[Uncertain]** Idea only; not researched.

**Suggested start:** Option A for the first cities, or Option B with Graduate visa holders and British or settled people. Pay per completed visit plus report. Use the Viewber figures as a starting benchmark only, since a visit plus a live video call plus a written report takes longer than a 30-minute viewing. **[Estimate]** Budget roughly £25–£40 per visit, and test this in your own market.

## 4. Licences, liability and insurance

### 4.1 Do you need a licence or scheme membership?

- **Client Money Protection (CMP):** CMP is only needed by agents who **hold client money**. If you never touch rent or deposits, you do not need it ([gov.uk CMP guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mandatory-client-money-protection); [summary](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/client-money-protection-letting-agents/)). [Official/Secondary] **Rule for your business: never collect or pass on rent or deposits.**

- **Redress scheme (England): grey area.**
  - The law defines "lettings agency work" as things done in the course of a business "in response to instructions received from … a person seeking to find a dwelling-house … to rent" ([ERRA 2013 s.83](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2013/24/section/83)). [Official]
  - The only exceptions are pure advertising or information platforms, employees, local councils, and anything else exempted by a government order (same source).
  - A tenant-paid "check before you pay" service **could** fall inside this definition. **[Uncertain]** Ask a UK housing lawyer.
  - Membership is cheap insurance against this risk:
    - [Property Redress](https://www.propertyredress.co.uk/business): Entry plan **£155 + VAT a year** (plus £160 + VAT per complaint), or Enhanced plan £250 + VAT a year.
    - [The Property Ombudsman](https://www.tpos.co.uk/businesses/): about **£278 a year** plus a £74 joining fee.
  - **[Uncertain]** I did not check whether these schemes accept a business based outside the UK.

- **Tenant Fees Act.** It stops landlords and letting agents from **requiring** a tenant to pay banned fees or to buy a third-party service "in connection with a tenancy" ([TFA 2019 s.2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2019/4/section/2)). [Official]
  - Your service is chosen and paid for by the tenant, not required by the landlord, so it should be outside this ban.
  - **[Uncertain]** I could not confirm whether the Act's definition of "letting agent" includes agents acting for the tenant. Ask a lawyer.
  - **Never** take commissions or referral fees from landlords or agents. That would create a conflict of interest, and the law could see you as working for the landlord.

- **Scotland and Wales:** both have their own letting agent licensing or registration (for example, Rent Smart Wales covers agents, per [Property Reporter](https://www.propertyreporter.co.uk/landlords/landlords-could-you-be-breaking-the-law-after-recent-changes.html)). **[Uncertain]** I did not check whether a tenant-side check service falls inside them. **Launch in England first.**

### 4.2 Getting inside the property

- A landlord, or someone the landlord authorises **in writing**, can enter a let home "at reasonable times of the day" after giving **24 hours' notice in writing** to the people living there ([Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 s.11(6)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1985/70/section/11)). [Official]
- **Practical rule:** the partner only visits **with the landlord or agent**, at a time they arranged. If people live there now, the landlord must have given them notice. Never enter alone, and never film the current tenants or their belongings without their permission.

### 4.3 Liability risks, and how to reduce them

| Risk | Example | How to reduce it |
|---|---|---|
| Negligent report | The partner misses mould, and the student later loses money or gets ill | A clear scope on every report: "visual, non-specialist check on [date]; not a building survey; not a guarantee of the landlord's honesty." Use a fixed checklist. Keep photos and video as evidence. |
| Defamation | The report calls a real landlord a "scammer" | Factual wording only (see 2.2) |
| Data breach | Landlord ID photos or videos leak | Do not store ID copies. Use short retention periods (see 5) |
| Partner injury or damage | The partner trips, or breaks something in the flat | The partner holds their own public liability insurance; you hold yours |
| Partner misconduct | Theft or harassment | DBS check, ID check and the right-to-work check (3.2–3.3). Always visit with the landlord present |

**[Uncertain]** UK consumer law limits how far you can exclude liability for your own negligence. Have a UK lawyer review your terms.

### 4.4 Insurance cost (UK, rough)

- **Professional indemnity** (covers mistakes in your reports): for UK small businesses, 10% of customers paid **£62.72 a year or less** (February–April 2025). Example quotes are £114.50 a year for a sole-trader designer ([Simply Business](https://simplybusiness.co.uk/business-insurance/professional-indemnity-insurance)). [Secondary]
- **Public liability** (covers injury or damage to other people's property): about **£115–£155 a year** for £1m–£5m cover. Many micro firms pay under £100 ([NimbleFins](https://www.nimblefins.co.uk/public-liability-insurance-uk), [Forma](https://goforma.com/business-resources/how-much-does-public-liability-insurance-cost)). [Secondary]
- **[Uncertain]** UK insurers usually expect a UK-based business. As an Indian sole proprietor you may need an Indian insurer's professional indemnity policy that covers UK claims, or a UK company (see 6.4). Get quotes before launch.

## 5. Data protection

### 5.1 UK rules (UK GDPR)

- **Does the UK law apply to you? Probably yes. [Uncertain]**
  - Your partners collect personal data in the UK: landlord names, ID details, video of people and homes.
  - You also serve students who are, or soon will be, in the UK.
  - The UK law can reach non-UK businesses that offer services to people in the UK ([LegalVision on Article 27](https://legalvision.co.uk/data-privacy-it/uk-gdpr-article-27-representatives/)). [Secondary] Assume it applies.

- **UK representative.** A business outside the UK that falls under the UK law must normally appoint a **UK representative** (Article 27) ([LegalVision](https://legalvision.co.uk/data-privacy-it/uk-gdpr-article-27-representatives/)). [Secondary] **[Uncertain]** I did not check the cost. Paid representative services exist.

- **ICO data protection fee.** The ICO is the UK's data protection regulator.
  - "Organisations (including sole traders) that use personal information need to pay a data protection fee, unless they are exempt." There is a 10-minute self-assessment ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/self-assessment/)). [Official]
  - Fees since 17 February 2025: **Tier 1 (micro) £52**, Tier 2 £78, Tier 3 £3,763 ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/06/fee-payable-to-the-ico-to-increase-in-february-102jzb9)). [Secondary]
  - **[Uncertain]** It is not clear whether a business based only in India must pay. Do the ICO self-assessment. If you set up a UK company, it will almost certainly need to pay.

- **Legal reason to process data.** UK GDPR lists **seven** lawful bases, including consent, contract, legitimate interests and the new "recognised legitimate interest". **Consent is only one option** ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/)). [Official]
  - For your **customer's** data, "contract" fits.
  - For the **landlord's** data, "legitimate interests" probably fits (preventing fraud). Document why.

- **Recording video inside homes.**
  - Video and voice recordings of identifiable people are personal data. You need a lawful basis, must tell people, keep only what you need, and delete it on time ([DWF summary of ICO video guidance](https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2022/3/ico-guidance-video-surveillance)). [Secondary]
  - Good practice:
    1. Say "this call is being recorded for the tenant's check" at the start.
    2. Get the landlord's clear yes on camera.
    3. Never film current occupants, their faces or their private items without their permission.
    4. Do not record ID documents. Just note "ID seen, name matches title register: yes/no".
    5. Delete raw video after a fixed period, for example 90 days. Keep the report.
  - **[Uncertain]** I did not find a UK law that requires every party's consent to record. The main rules come from data protection law.

### 5.2 India: DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

- **Rules notified on 13 November 2025** ([S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/), [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)). [Secondary]
- **Timeline:** the Data Protection Board rules applied straight away. Consent manager rules apply from about **13 November 2026**. **The main duties for businesses apply 18 months after notification, around 13 May 2027.** These include clear notices, consent, security, breach reporting and deletion ([AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)). [Secondary]
- **What to do now:**
  - Write a plain privacy notice in English and Hindi.
  - Collect only what you need: the student's name, contact details, and the property link.
  - Get a clear tick-box consent for any marketing.
  - Use secure storage.
  - Have a breach plan.
  - Delete data when you no longer need it.

## 6. Money logistics

### 6.1 Taking money from customers

- **Indian parents (UPI, cards, netbanking):** [Razorpay's standard plan](https://razorpay.com/pricing/) charges **2% per payment**, with no setup or yearly fee, plus **18% GST on the fee**. [Official/company]
- **Customers abroad (foreign cards):** Razorpay charges **up to 3%** ([pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing/)). International cards are **not switched on by default**. You must apply and meet the bank's eligibility rules ([Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/international-payments/accept-payments-from-international-customers/)). [Company]
- **Stripe** is **invite-only** for Indian businesses ([Stripe](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-accounts-are-invite-only-in-india)). [Company]
- **Wise Business (India)** is mainly for **receiving** foreign payments into an Indian business ([Wise, 3 Sept 2026](https://wise.com/in/blog/how-does-wise-work)). [Company]

### 6.2 Paying UK partners from India

- **LRS** (the Liberalised Remittance Scheme) lets each resident Indian **individual** send up to **USD 250,000 per financial year** abroad. A sole proprietor **does not get a second limit** for the business, because legally the owner and the business are the same person ([RBI FAQ](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=115)). [Official]
- **[Uncertain]** Paying for services your business buys from abroad is normally a business ("current account") payment through your bank, backed by the partner's invoice, rather than a personal LRS transfer. Ask your bank which route and purpose code to use.
- **Form 15CA/15CB** (Indian tax forms for payments abroad): your bank may ask for them. Whether you need them depends on whether the payment is taxable in India ([Skydo guide](https://web.skydo.com/blog/understanding-form-15-ca-cb-comprehensive-guide)). [Secondary] **[Uncertain]** Ask a CA.
- **Wise:** individuals in India can send abroad, and TCS (tax collected at source) may apply ([Wise](https://wise.com/in/blog/how-does-wise-work)). One review says **new Indian users cannot currently send money out** ([Winvesta, a competitor](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/wise-india-review-2026-features-fees-limitations-verdict)). **[Uncertain]**
- **Payoneer** received **in-principle RBI approval** as a cross-border payment aggregator for money coming in and going out of India (January 2026) ([Payoneer](https://www.payoneer.com/press/payoneer-receives-in-principle-authorization-as-cross-border-payment-aggregator-in-india/)). It is used to pay overseas freelancers and suppliers ([Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/compare/payoneer-overview)). [Company/Secondary] Check that this works for a sole proprietor before you rely on it.
- **Practical tip:** pay partners once a month in GBP against their invoices, to cut transfer fees.

### 6.3 GST (India)

- **Your sales to Indian customers:** GST registration is normally needed above **₹20 lakh** turnover for services ([TaxWink](https://www.taxwink.com/blog/gst-registration-rcm)). [Secondary]
- **Reverse charge on imported services.** Under reverse charge, the buyer pays the GST instead of the supplier. Anyone who must pay reverse-charge GST **must register whatever their turnover** (section 24 of the CGST Act) ([TaxWink](https://www.taxwink.com/blog/gst-registration-rcm)). [Secondary]
- **But this may not apply to UK flat visits. [Uncertain]**
  - For services "directly in relation to an immovable property", the place of supply is **where the property is** (IGST Act s.13(4)) ([KnowYourGST](https://www.knowyourgst.com/gstlaw/igst-act/13-place-of-supply-of-services-where-location-of-supplier-or-location-of-recipient-is-outside-india-187/)).
  - If the property is in the UK, the partner's service may **not count as an "import of services"**, so no reverse charge would apply ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1066936)).
  - **Get a written CA opinion.** This decides whether you must register for GST from day 1.
- **Customers paying from abroad in foreign currency:** this may count as an export of services, which is zero-rated. **[Uncertain]** Ask a CA.

### 6.4 When to open a UK company

**What it costs:**
- **£100** to set up online (£156 same-day, £124 on paper), plus **£50** for the yearly confirmation statement. Page updated 25 September 2026 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-fees/companies-house-fees)). [Official]
- The registered office must be a **physical UK address** where post is actually received. PO boxes are not allowed. An accountant's or service provider's address is fine with their permission ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/company-address)). [Official]
- Since **18 November 2025**, directors and owners must **verify their identity**. This started a 12-month transition. People without suitable ID can verify through an accountant or other authorised agent ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/verify-your-identity-for-companies-house)). [Official]

**Indian side:** a resident individual can invest in a foreign **operating** company under the Overseas Investment Rules 2022, within the LRS limit. Rules include no step-down structures where the person has control, and filing an **Annual Performance Report** each year ([Razorpay ODI guide](https://razorpay.com/blog/fema-odi-regulations-guide/)). [Secondary]

**When it is worth it:** open a UK company when one or more of these is true. **[Judgement]**
- (a) You need UK insurance or redress scheme membership that will not cover an Indian business.
- (b) You need a UK bank account to pay partners cheaply in GBP.
- (c) You want to employ people through UK payroll.
- (d) You do enough visits a month that UK credibility and lower fees outweigh UK accounting costs.

Before that, stay an Indian sole proprietor and use Option A or B for partners.

## 7. The checklist (for partners, first draft)

**Before the visit (done remotely by you):**
1. Get the advert link, the address, and the landlord's or agent's name and phone number from the customer.
2. Reverse image search the photos. Search the address on the major property portals.
3. Buy the Land Registry title register (£7) and compare the owner's name. Run a Companies House search if the landlord is a company.
4. If there is an agent, check their redress scheme, and their CMP certificate if they hold money.
5. Check the EPC on gov.uk, including whether it is E or better and whether there is a registered exemption.
6. If it is an HMO, check the council register. In Scotland, Wales or NI, also search the landlord register.
7. Ask for the money breakdown in writing and compare it with the legal caps (1.1–1.3).
8. Do the right-to-work check on the partner (one-off), and confirm the partner's DBS certificate (one-off).

**At the visit (partner, live video with the customer):**
1. Announce that the call is recorded and get the landlord's verbal consent on camera.
2. The landlord or agent opens the door with keys.
3. ID is shown on camera but not recorded. Note "name matches title: yes/no".
4. Walk through every room: compare with the advert, look for damp or mould, test heating, hot water, windows and locks.
5. Press the test button on each smoke and CO alarm. Note any appliance that burns fuel without a CO alarm.
6. Photograph the gas safety record and the EICR. Check their dates and addresses.
7. Count bedrooms and occupants (HMO check).
8. Ask which deposit scheme will be used, how payments will be made, and to whose account.
9. Check outside: street, transport, phone signal, rubbish and noise.

**Report within 48 hours:**
- A one-page summary: Green, Amber or Red for each test in 2.2.
- Facts only.
- Photos and timestamps.
- A list of documents missing.
- A note of what is outside the scope of the check.
