# Who would buy a "flat check before you pay" service in the UK? (Demand notes, October 2026)

These notes look at the **buyers**: Indian students (and other people moving to the UK from abroad) who rent a **private** flat or room that they found themselves, and who pay a deposit before they have seen it.

**How to read these notes**
- Every fact has a link to where it came from.
- **(Estimate)** means I worked the number out myself from other numbers. Treat it as a rough guide, not a fact.
- **(Old data)** means the data is from before 2024.
- **(Not checked)** means I saw the fact quoted by a second-hand website but could not open the original source.
- "Study visa" numbers below are **main applicants** (the student, not family members) unless I say otherwise.

---

## The short version

1. About **82,500 Indian students** got UK study visas in the year to June 2026. That is **down 16%** on the year before. India is now the **second-biggest** country after China ([Home Office, YE June 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)).
2. In calendar year 2025 India was the **biggest** country, with **95,231** study visas, or 23% of all of them ([Home Office, YE Dec 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-december-2025/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)).
3. 2026 is weaker. Study visa applications from **all countries** were **down 22%** from January to August 2026 compared with the same months of 2025 ([Home Office monthly data tables, Aug 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aa129a962ec7fe7bedf51e0/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-data-tables-aug-2026.ods); my sums).
4. **81%** of Indian students come for a **master's degree** ([Home Office, YE June 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2025/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)). Master's students are more likely than first-year undergraduates to rent shared private houses ([Unipol survey, 2024](https://unipol.org.uk/media/eamh54s5/the-international-student-housing-survey-briefing-paper.pdf)).
5. There are two busy seasons. Most students hunt for housing for **September** between about **June and August**. A second, smaller group hunts for **January** in **October to December**. January starters find housing **harder** to get ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-january-2026/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-january-2026); [Unipol](https://unipol.org.uk/media/eamh54s5/the-international-student-housing-survey-briefing-paper.pdf)).
6. Rental fraud reported to Action Fraud cost victims **£4.2 million in 2023**, about **£1,720 per victim** on average. It is one of the **top 5 frauds** reported by people aged 11 to 29 ([Which?, Sept 2024](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/4-ways-to-spot-a-rental-scam-a206R0v7tjRF)).
7. A big London case shows the kind of trick this service would stop. One group of letting companies posted more than **18,000 misleading SpareRoom adverts** and made **£18 million** between 2016 and 2021. They took deposits from people, including international students, before those people had seen the room ([report on the Tower Hamlets Council case, Mar 2025](https://bebeez.eu/2025/03/25/rogue-letting-agents-face-jail-for-massive-fraud/)).
8. A new law changes the money involved. From **1 May 2026**, landlords in England can ask for **no more than one month's rent in advance**. Before this, international students were often asked for **6 to 12 months' rent up front**. Landlords may now ask for a **UK guarantor** (a person or company that promises to pay if the tenant does not) more often ([Goodlord](https://blog.goodlord.co/rent-in-advance); [Blueground guide](https://www.theblueground.com/blog/navigate-your-lease/uk-student-renting-guide-2026-renters-rights-act)).
9. There are other buyers too, but most of these groups have shrunk:
   - **Indian nurses:** about 4,200 joined the UK nursing register in 2025–26, down 54% ([NMC](https://www.nmc.org.uk/news/news-and-updates/sharp-drop-in-international-recruitment-slows-overall-growth-of-uk-register)).
   - **Overseas care workers:** this route has been closed since July 2025 ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-work)).
   - **Students from Nigeria, Pakistan, Nepal and Bangladesh:** about 90,000 more students a year in 2025 ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-december-2025/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)).
10. **(Estimate)** About **10,000 to 24,000 Indian students a year** probably pay for a private room or flat in the UK **before they have seen it**. The working is in section 7.

**The trend in plain words:** numbers peaked in 2022–23. They fell hard in 2024 after students lost the right to bring family. They held steady in 2025, and they are falling again in 2026.

### Other useful facts
- **2026 so far (all countries; India is not shown separately month by month).** There were **231,600** study visa applications from January to August 2026, against **298,500** in the same months of 2025. That is **down 22%**. August 2026 had **99,500** applications, against 120,300 in August 2025 and 144,200 in August 2022 ([Home Office monthly data tables](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aa129a962ec7fe7bedf51e0/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-data-tables-aug-2026.ods)).
  - **(Estimate)** If India is still about 23% of the total, that is about **50,000 to 55,000 Indian applications** from January to August 2026.
- **Who comes:** 81% of Indian students come for a master's degree ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2025/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)). Most master's courses last one year, so a new group needs housing **every year**.
- **Few bring family.** Family members coming with Indian students fell by almost **80%** after the January 2024 rule change ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/nri/study/story/indians-top-uk-visas-in-2025-with-over-95000-study-permits-leading-graduate-and-worker-routes-519709-2026-03-09)). That means more single students sharing rooms and houses, and fewer families renting whole flats.
- **Few refusals.** Only about 4% of Indian study visa applications were refused in 2025 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/nri/study/story/indians-top-uk-visas-in-2025-with-over-95000-study-permits-leading-graduate-and-worker-routes-519709-2026-03-09)).
- **Graduates staying on to work.** Indians got **90,153** Graduate Route visas in 2025, which is 42% of all of them ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/nri/study/story/indians-top-uk-visas-in-2025-with-over-95000-study-permits-leading-graduate-and-worker-routes-519709-2026-03-09)). They got **64,495** in the year to June 2026 ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)). These people often move to a new city for a job and need a new flat.
- **University data.** India was still the biggest source of overseas students at UK universities in 2024/25, but numbers were **down 12%**. There were 685,565 overseas students in total, down 6% ([Times Higher Education, Jan 2026](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/masters-plunge-10-cent-amid-record-fall-overseas-students)).

### Halls or private renting: where do they live?

- **The best survey.** Unipol surveyed **3,698 international students** at 30 universities in 2023 and published the results in June 2024. Indian students were the biggest group, with 618 answers. Main findings:
  - **38%** of international students without family lived in a **shared rented house**.
  - **55%** of Chinese students lived in one.
  - First-year undergraduates mostly live in university halls. **Master's and research students mostly live in shared private houses** ([Unipol](https://unipol.org.uk/media/eamh54s5/the-international-student-housing-survey-briefing-paper.pdf)).
- **Older data (Old data).** About **30%** of international students lived in university or private halls in 2020 ([HEPI, 2020](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/2020/01/08/students-accommodation-experiences-are-far-from-uniform/)).
- **London.** About **20%** of international master's students in London live **with friends or family** ([GLA, July 2026](https://data.london.gov.uk/download/20gq8/7br/Student%20accommodation%20need%20-%20analysis%20and%20review%20of%20evidence%20July%202026.pdf)).
- **How students found their home.** Only about **24%** of international students without family found their current home through a **university website**. Many used estate agents, letting websites or friends ([Unipol](https://unipol.org.uk/media/eamh54s5/the-international-student-housing-survey-briefing-paper.pdf)).
- **Student blocks.** "PBSA" means purpose-built student accommodation: blocks of student rooms run by private companies.
  - Across the UK there are **more than 2 students for every PBSA bed** ([Property Notify](https://www.propertynotify.co.uk/investment/latest-insights-into-the-2025-student-market/)).
  - Some cities now have **spare** beds because fewer overseas students are coming. Sheffield has 1.21 students per bed ([Property Notify](https://www.propertynotify.co.uk/investment/latest-insights-into-the-2025-student-market/)).
  - Almost half of new beds were built in just three cities: London, Nottingham and Leeds ([Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/739473)).
- **Fewer private landlords.** Tax, interest-rate and rule changes are making small private landlords and HMOs leave the market ([GLA, July 2026](https://data.london.gov.uk/download/20gq8/7br/Student%20accommodation%20need%20-%20analysis%20and%20review%20of%20evidence%20July%202026.pdf)). An HMO is a house shared by several unrelated people, which needs a council licence when it is big enough.
- **(Estimate)** Roughly **40 to 60%** of new Indian students end up in a **private** room or flat rather than halls or a student block. I based this on the facts above: most Indian students are on master's courses, and master's students mostly live in shared private houses.

- **September intake (the big one).** About **70%** of the year's applications come in **June to September**: 301,800 out of 427,600 in 2025. Applications peak in August ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-january-2026/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-january-2026)).
- **January intake (the second one).** The Home Office says there is a "second smaller peak in December" ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-january-2026/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-january-2026)).
  - December plus January applications were **63,900** for the January 2025 intake and **49,500** for the January 2026 intake, **down 23%** (my sums).
  - That is about 13 to 15% of the year.
- **The January 2026 intake was weak.** In a survey of universities by BUILA (a group for university international staff):
  - **70%** of universities had fewer master's students starting in January 2026, and numbers were **down 31%** overall.
  - **66%** had fewer students from **India**, and **82%** had fewer from Pakistan.
  - **60%** saw more visa refusals than usual ([Times Higher Education, Apr 2026](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/742468)).

### When do they hunt for housing and pay?
- **General advice to students.** Start looking **6 to 9 months** before arrival and book **3 to 6 months** before moving in ([Admistay guide](https://admistay.com/intelligence-hub/how-early-should-you-book-student-accommodation)).
- **Scams peak in July and August**, when students rush to find a home before term starts ([Action Fraud via The Negotiator, 2018, Old data](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/rental-scams-5-25m-a-year-says-action-fraud/)).
- **January starters have it harder.** **29%** of January starters said finding a home was "much more difficult than expected", against **15%** of September starters. This is because university halls are mostly full by January ([Unipol](https://unipol.org.uk/media/eamh54s5/the-international-student-housing-survey-briefing-paper.pdf)).
  - Some study-abroad websites say the opposite: that January is calmer ([GradRight](https://gradright.com/news/january-intake-study-abroad-trend/)).

## 3. How common are rental scams against international and Indian students?

### Official and police numbers

| What | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rental fraud losses reported to Action Fraud, 2023 | **£4.2 million**, average **£1,720** per victim. Rental fraud is one of the 5 most-reported frauds for people aged 11 to 29 | [Which?, Sept 2024](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/4-ways-to-spot-a-rental-scam-a206R0v7tjRF) |
| Rental fraud, 2024 | about **5,000 reports**, about **£9 million**, about **£1,800** each **(Not checked)**: quoted by student websites, original not found | [uni2study](https://www.uni2study.com/articles/trustpilot-google-reviews-student-housing) |
| Rental fraud, April 2014 to March 2018 (Old data) | **18,645** reports and **£22.1 million** lost, average **£1,396**. **930** were linked to universities. More than **400** cases were **£5,000 or more**. Busiest months: July and August | [The Negotiator, June 2018](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/rental-scams-5-25m-a-year-says-action-fraud/) |
| How much goes unreported | Action Fraud says the true student number is higher, because many victims do not say they are students | [The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/rental-scams-5-25m-a-year-says-action-fraud/) |
| For comparison, Ireland 2025 | **160** student housing fraud cases from January to June 2025, **up 22%**; **€385,000** lost | [Kildare Nationalist](https://www.kildare-nationalist.ie/news/accommodation-fraud-jumps-22-as-garda%C3%AD-warn-students-of-rental-scams_arid-68633.html) |

- **Typical amount lost.** The average is about **£1,400 to £1,800**, which is about ₹1.6 to 2.1 lakh at roughly ₹115 to £1 **(Estimate; check today's rate)**.
- Upfront-rent scams cost much more. The Goodlord example of a £1,200-a-month room paid 12 months ahead comes to **£14,400**, about ₹16.5 lakh ([Goodlord](https://blog.goodlord.co/rent-in-advance)).

### Real cases (2024–2026)
- **Tower Hamlets, London.** The council's trading standards team (the local office that enforces consumer law) brought this case.
  - A group of letting companies made **£18 million** from 2016 to 2021, using at least **18,000 misleading adverts on SpareRoom**.
  - They used **"bait and switch"**: they showed photos of one room, then gave the tenant a worse one. They also took deposits before people had seen the room, and did not protect or return those deposits.
  - The victims were "workers or students who were new to the UK", including international students. Some ended up paying for hotels or sleeping on friends' sofas.
  - Five people were convicted of 15 crimes in November 2024 ([report, Mar 2025](https://bebeez.eu/2025/03/25/rogue-letting-agents-face-jail-for-massive-fraud/)).
- **Brent, London, May 2025.** Council officers found **15 tenants in a 7-bedroom house**, with students sleeping on floor mattresses, 2 or 3 to a room. Smoke alarms were covered with tin foil. The landlords were fined **£20,000** ([The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/regulation-law-news/sibling-landlords-hit-with-20k-fine-for-horrific-overcrowding-in-unlicensed-hmo/)).
- **BBC, June 2026 (Not checked).** A 4-bedroom flat in Poplar, London, was advertised on the big property websites by an agency that falsely claimed to be a Propertymark member (Propertymark is the UK body for estate and letting agents).
  - More than **20 would-be tenants, many of them students**, paid months of rent up front plus deposits.
  - They arrived to find no keys and other groups expecting to move in. This was reported by a second-hand blog summary; I could not open the BBC original.

### What universities and the Indian government say
- **Swansea University warning to international students:** "Deposits are standard in renting; paying money upfront to secure a room is not." It also warns students not to pay by **Western Union or MoneyGram**. It warns about **"proof of funds"** tricks too, where a fake landlord asks you to send money to a friend and share the receipt to show you can pay ([Swansea University](https://www.swansea.ac.uk/accommodation/international-students/accommodation-fraud/)).
- **Indian High Commission and Consulate advice** ([CGI Birmingham student page](https://www.cgibirmingham.gov.in/page/student-related-information/)):
  - Live on campus "whenever possible ... for obvious security reasons".
  - Ask the university housing office which areas are safe.
  - Report fraud to the police and Action Fraud.
  - Most of its fraud warnings are about **fake Home Office phone calls** and **visa or job agents**. I found **no specific Indian government warning about rental scams**.

### Gaps here
- No UK source counts rental scams by **nationality**, so there is **no number for Indian victims**.
- I found no 2024–2026 Times of India or Hindustan Times story about a named Indian student losing a UK deposit. Such stories may exist; my searches did not find them.

## 4. Other housing problems international students face

### Unipol international student survey (2023 data, published June 2024)

Results for 2,964 international students who came without family ([Unipol](https://unipol.org.uk/media/eamh54s5/the-international-student-housing-survey-briefing-paper.pdf)):
- **42%** found it **harder than expected** to find somewhere to live. For research students it was 57%. For research students with families it was 79%.
- **Affordability** (being able to pay the rent) was the most common worst problem, named by **31%**.
- Between about **1 in 10 and 1 in 6** said the hardest part was **meeting landlord or agent conditions, such as paying rent up front or finding a guarantor**. Research students struggled most.
- **17%** said the information they got before arriving was **wrong**, and **9%** got **none**. So more than **a quarter** were working without good information.
- **27%** were worried about money. Among Indian students it was **39%**, the highest of the main countries.
- **7%** shared a bedroom, and **23%** said their home felt overcrowded.
- Overall satisfaction was poor. On a scale from minus 100 to plus 100 (the "Net Promoter Score", which measures how likely people are to recommend something), the score was **minus 25**. Indian students scored **minus 17**.
- One Chilean student in Manchester said they had to **pay 12 months' rent** to get a private home.

### Other surveys
- **Save the Student, 2025, about 1,300 students (Not checked; the page blocked me, figures from a search summary):**
  - **69%** had at least one problem with their home; **damp** was the most common (**31%**).
  - Average rent was **£563 a month**, and **£812 in London**.
  - **36%** had thought about **dropping out** because of rent ([Save the Student](https://savethestudent.org/money/surveys/national-student-accommodation-survey-2025.html)).
- **The same survey in 2024** ([Accommodation for Students](https://www.accommodationforstudents.com/student-blog/national-student-accommodation-survey-results)):
  - The top problems were **mould and damp**, then no water or heating.
  - **1 in 6** struggled to get their deposit back.
  - **60%** borrowed money to pay rent.
- **Private houses compared with student blocks.** **74%** of students in private rented homes would not recommend them because of building defects, and **59%** because of landlord problems. For student blocks the figures were 53% and 40% ([GLA, July 2026, quoting a national survey](https://data.london.gov.uk/download/20gq8/7br/Student%20accommodation%20need%20-%20analysis%20and%20review%20of%20evidence%20July%202026.pdf)).
- **Rents above budget.** **34%** of new students had to raise their rent budget, according to Knight Frank (a property firm) and UCAS (the UK university admissions service) ([GLA, July 2026](https://data.london.gov.uk/download/20gq8/7br/Student%20accommodation%20need%20-%20analysis%20and%20review%20of%20evidence%20July%202026.pdf)).
- **Rents rising.** Student rents rose about **8%** in 2024 ([Knight Frank, Dec 2024](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2024/12/inside-britains-student-housing-crisis-key-challenges-and-2025-predictions)).

### Guarantors and paying rent up front
- **1 in 5 renters** paid rent in advance because they **could not provide a guarantor**. This comes from the State of the Lettings Industry Report 2025 ([Goodlord](https://blog.goodlord.co/rent-in-advance)).
- **The new law (Renters' Rights Act).** From **1 May 2026**, landlords in **England** can ask for **at most one month's rent in advance**, and any contract term asking for more does not count.
  - The deposit is still capped at about 5 weeks' rent.
  - Tenants can still **choose** to pay more once the tenancy has started.
  - Experts expect landlords to ask more often for a **UK guarantor** or a **paid guarantor company**, and to check tenants more strictly ([Goodlord](https://blog.goodlord.co/rent-in-advance); [Blueground](https://www.theblueground.com/blog/navigate-your-lease/uk-student-renting-guide-2026-renters-rights-act)).
  - Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own rules. I did not check these.
- **Older reports (Old data).**
  - In 2022, Indian students were told they needed a **UK-based guarantor**, and some landlords got **200 to 300 applications within half an hour** ([Free Press Journal, Oct 2022](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/education/all-you-need-to-know-about-uk-accommodation-crisis-as-an-indian-student)).

### Indian workers, nurses and carers
- **2024:** Indians got **81,463** UK work visas, 22% of all work visas.
  - **30,301** were Health and Care visas and **27,922** were Skilled Worker visas.
  - **34,954** went to the workers themselves and **46,509** to their family members ([LBC / PA, using Home Office data](https://www.lbc.co.uk/business/c5e34178191548abba1a335dc2fe3f12)).
- **2025: a big fall.**
  - The UK **stopped recruiting care workers from abroad on 22 July 2025**.
  - Health and Care visas for care jobs fell by **more than 99%**, from 107,772 (year to December 2023) to **344** (year to June 2026), for all countries.
  - Across all countries, Skilled Worker and Health and Care visas together came to **95,263** in the year to June 2026. Of these, **34,122** were the workers themselves ([Home Office, Work, YE June 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-work)).

  - Health and Care: about **400 to 900 a month**, compared with **18,300 in August 2023** ([Home Office monthly data tables](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aa129a962ec7fe7bedf51e0/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-data-tables-aug-2026.ods)).

An extension is a new visa for someone who is already in the UK. These people live in the UK already, so they are a smaller "renting in another city" market rather than a "renting from abroad" one.

**What the universities predict.** UK universities expect **22.5% more** international students between 2024/25 and 2028/29. The Office for Students, which regulates universities in England, thinks this is **too hopeful** ([GLA, July 2026](https://data.london.gov.uk/download/20gq8/7br/Student%20accommodation%20need%20-%20analysis%20and%20review%20of%20evidence%20July%202026.pdf)).

### What this means for demand in 2027 (Estimate, my reading)
- **Fewer students than in 2023, but still many.**
  - New Indian study visas in 2027 will probably be about **65,000 to 85,000**. The low end assumes 2026's fall continues; the high end assumes numbers stay level.
  - The **January 2027** group is probably about **10,000 to 17,000 Indians**. I based this on about 50,000 December-plus-January applications from all countries, with India making up a quarter to a third of the January intake.
- **More single students on a budget.** Most will be single master's students at newer universities, sharing rooms, HMOs and flat-shares they find on SpareRoom, Facebook groups and WhatsApp. This is the group most exposed to scams and bad housing.
- **Smaller sums at risk, but scams continue.** The one-month cap means less money is paid up front in England. Scams based on holding deposits, the first month's rent and the deposit, fake agents and bait-and-switch will carry on. A check before paying still matters, and maybe matters more as landlords chase **guarantors**.
- **Less panic in some cities.** Where fewer overseas students are coming (Sheffield, Bedfordshire and Swansea, for example), rooms are easier to find. People may feel less need for a check there. London and the commuter towns around it are still tight.
- **Policy is unstable.** The 18-month Graduate Route, the stricter university checks, the levy and a new Prime Minister make the UK feel less certain to Indian families. That could push numbers down. It also makes families more anxious, and anxious families are more likely to pay for reassurance.
