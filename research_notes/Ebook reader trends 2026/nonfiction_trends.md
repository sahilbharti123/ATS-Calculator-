# Nonfiction Reader Demand 2025-2026: Hottest and Fastest-Rising Categories and Topics (for an aspiring ebook author)

Research date: October 3, 2026. US market primary; UK/Canada noted where found. Biography/memoir is covered for context only and is not a recommended target. Unit figures from Circana BookScan cover US print only (about 85% of print outlets) unless stated otherwise. They do not include ebooks, Kindle Unlimited or audio, which matters for an ebook author.

---

## 1. What were the bestselling nonfiction books of 2025 and 2026 so far, and what topics do they cluster around?

### Takeaway
2025 was dominated by one self-help title, Mel Robbins' *The Let Them Theory*: about 2.8M US print units, the #1 book of the year across all categories. Beneath it, the bestseller lists cluster around (a) mindset and self-help, (b) mental health and trauma, (c) midlife women's health and longevity, (d) communication and relationships, (e) politics and current affairs, (f) cookbooks (high-protein, budget, creator-led) and (g) celebrity memoir. In 2026 so far, the NYT #1 slot has mostly gone to memoir and politics, while self-help is cooling against the Robbins comparison. Health and longevity (*Heal Your Cells*), creator-led cookbooks and homemaking, retirement finance and spiritual self-help keep showing up in the top 25.

### Inferences
- Topic clusters, roughly ordered by how many 2025-26 top-seller slots they hold:
  1. Mindset/self-help: *Let Them*, *Atomic Habits*, Nguyen's books, *Limitless Daily*, *48 Laws of Power*.
  2. Politics/current affairs, mostly platform-driven authors.
  3. Memoir (context only).
  4. Health/longevity/women's midlife health: *Outlive*, *How to Menopause*, *The New Menopause*, *Good Energy*, *Heal Your Cells*, *100 Rules for Living to 100*.
  5. Mental health/trauma: *The Body Keeps the Score*, the DBT workbook, *The Anxious Generation*.
  6. Cookbooks, especially high-protein, healthy, budget and creator-led: *Skinnytaste High Protein*, *Shred Happens*, *Lorafied*, *Everyday Eats*.
  7. Communication/relationships: *The Next Conversation*, *The Art of Fighting*, *Situated*, *Why Men Love Bitches*.
  8. Narrative science and history: John Green, Mary Roach, *1929*.
  9. True crime: *The Idaho Four*, *The Back Bedroom*, Murdaugh.
  10. Faith/spiritual: *The Elijah Mandate*, *The Book of Possibility*, *Stop, in the Name of God*.
- Many of the biggest titles come from authors with big audiences elsewhere: podcasts (Robbins, Huberman, Galloway), TikTok/YouTube (Jefferson Fisher, Nguyen, Barbara Costello, the Lorafied and Shred Happens creators) or politics and TV. Topic alone did not drive the sales. An unknown ebook author should borrow the topics, not expect blockbuster numbers.
- Evergreen backlist titles (*Atomic Habits*, *The Body Keeps the Score*, *48 Laws of Power*, *Why Men Love Bitches*, *Subtle Art*) keep selling year after year. Demand for habits, trauma, power/strategy and dating-confidence topics is durable, not a fad.

## 2. Which nonfiction subcategories are growing fastest?

### Takeaway
Measured by sales data, the clearest 2025-26 growth in US print nonfiction is in self-help (+14.7% in 2025), religion/Bibles (+5.4% in 2025; Bibles at a 21-year high) and crafts/hobbies/games/puzzles (+7.7% in 2025 through September; +9.6% in Q1 2026). Biography/memoir, business/economics, travel, health/fitness/medicine and general nonfiction declined. In 2026, self-help fell about 24-26% against the Robbins comparison, so year-over-year category data now exaggerates its cooling. Within topics, the strongest signals are mental health (trauma, DBT, ADHD), menopause and midlife women's health, longevity and cellular health, high-protein and GLP-1-adjacent eating, communication and relationships, faith and spirituality, screen-free hobbies, and kids plus screens.

## 3. Which categories show strong demand on Amazon Kindle specifically (ebook-friendly) versus mainly print or audio?

### Takeaway
Self-help/popular psychology is the top nonfiction genre in ebooks and in audio (UK data), and digital buyers aged 25-44 dominate it. Computing/IT/tech has the highest ebook share of any nonfiction genre. Crafts, travel, art/photography and cookbooks remain overwhelmingly print, at over 90% print share for craft, travel and art. On Kindle specifically, the visible bestsellers are true crime, DBT/mental-health workbooks, health/science and mindset titles. Ebooks are flat to slightly down in revenue (-2.5% in H1 2026), while audio is growing (+14.7%), especially for self-help.

### Cited Findings
- UK 2024 NielsenIQ data:
  - E-books "took their largest share in computing/IT/technology," while audiobooks "had their largest in self-help/popular psychology."
  - Self-help/popular psychology was the top nonfiction genre overall and for both e-books and audiobooks; auto/biographies led print.
  - Travel, art/photography and craft "still exceed 90% print share"; food & drink has "only a sliver" of audio.
  - Print share of nonfiction fell below three-quarters for the first time in 2024.
  - Over five years, audiobooks nearly doubled their share of nonfiction purchases, while e-books gained only 1 point vs 2020.
  - Buyers aged 25-44 account for 54% of audiobooks and 60% of digital self-help/popular psychology purchases.

  — [NielsenIQ, "The changing shape of the non-fiction market," Jul 8, 2025](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/insights/commentary/2025/the-changing-shape-of-the-non-fiction-market/)
- US publisher revenue (AAP StatShot, H1 2026, adult trade): e-books -2.5% ($444.2M), digital audio +14.7% ($530.9M), hardcover -2.6%, trade paperback +8.4% — [PW, Sep 9, 2026](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/financial-reporting/article/101193-publishing-industry-sales-up-in-first-half-of-2026.html)
- Kindle nonfiction bestseller snapshot (undated, about Oct 2026): #1 *The Back Bedroom* (true crime), #3 *The DBT Skills Workbook*, plus *Nuclear War: A Scenario*, Huberman's *Protocols* and celebrity memoirs — [Amazon Kindle Nonfiction Best Sellers](https://www.amazon.com/Best-Sellers-Nonfiction/zgbs/digital-text/157325011)
- Amazon's "Most Read" chart, which measures Kindle and Audible daily readers, featured *The 48 Laws of Power*, *The Let Them Theory* and *The Anxious Generation* in early 2025 — [Amazon Charts Most Read Nonfiction, Feb 2, 2025](https://www.amazon.com/charts/2025-02-02/mostread/nonfiction/ref=dp_chrtbg_dbs_1)
- Indie context (PublishDrive): in 2025, print-on-demand grew faster than ebooks and Audible growth was nearly flat, but ebooks "remain the biggest part of the Amazon ecosystem for independent authors" (search excerpt) — [PublishDrive, Amazon book sales trends](https://publishdrive.com/amazon-book-sales-trends-on-publishdrive.html)
- Price sensitivity among active digital readers (Written Word Media survey, 3,589 respondents): median monthly book spend $15, mean $29.56; most spend under $5 per ebook and 25% download free books. The audience skews toward deal-seeking digital readers and is mostly fiction readers — [Written Word Media Reader Survey](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/reader-survey/). The 2026 survey page gives no nonfiction-specific breakdown — [Written Word Media 2026 survey](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/reader-survey-2026/amp/)
- Self-help works in self-publishing. Joseph Nguyen's self-published *Don't Believe Everything You Think* went viral on TikTok in 2022 and has been translated into 40+ languages — [getAbstract summary page](https://www.getabstract.com/en/summary/dont-believe-everything-you-think/49566). Nguyen has discussed selling via TikTok Shop — [Lean Marketing podcast](https://leanmarketing.com/podcast/navigating-tiktok-shop-proven-tactics-for-authors-and-creators-with-joseph-nguyen)

### Inferences
- **Most ebook-friendly:** self-help/mindset, popular psychology and mental health (including workbooks, if they can be completed in a notebook or downloaded as a PDF), relationships/dating, personal finance, practical tech/AI how-tos (the highest ebook share), true crime (strong Kindle/KU presence) and short spiritual or inspirational guides.
- **Mixed:** health/longevity/menopause. Readers buy in all formats, and a practical guide such as a "symptom tracker plus plan" works well as an ebook.
- **Poor ebook fit:** cookbooks, crafts/needlework, coloring and puzzle books, and travel guides. These are print-dominant, so an author in these spaces should plan print-on-demand or low-content print rather than Kindle-only.
- Audio is the fastest-growing format and its strongest nonfiction genre is self-help. An ebook author choosing self-help should plan an audiobook edition early.

### Gaps
- No US-specific breakdown of ebook share by nonfiction genre was found; the format split is UK data.
- No Kindle Unlimited page-read data by nonfiction category was found. K-lytics publishes this but behind a paywall.
- Kindle-only sales rankings for 2025 or full-year nonfiction could not be retrieved because Amazon blocked fetches.

## 5. Which formats and angles are popular in nonfiction?

### Takeaway
The winning formats are short, practical and specific: workbooks and skills manuals (DBT), "how to [life stage]" guides (*How to Menopause*), audience-specific problem guides (*The Overthinker's Guide to Making Decisions*), numbered or daily formats (*Limitless Daily: 366 Keys*, *100 Rules for Living to 100*), "playbooks" with a time frame (*A 10-Week Playbook for Stopping GLP-1 Medications*), guided journals and screen-free activity books, high-protein or budget recipe collections, and a single sticky idea with a memorable name ("Let Them"). Data-driven pop science with humor (Mary Roach, John Green) and contrarian or provocative takes (*Why Men Love Bitches*, *Abundance*, *If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies*) also perform.

### Cited Findings
- Workbook format: *The Dialectical Behavior Therapy Skills Workbook: Practical DBT Exercises...* was #3 on the Kindle nonfiction bestseller snapshot — [Amazon Kindle Nonfiction Best Sellers](https://www.amazon.com/Best-Sellers-Nonfiction/zgbs/digital-text/157325011)

### Inferences
- The strongest ebook-native recipe across the evidence:
  1. Pick a high-demand category (self-help, mental health, midlife women's health, relationships, money).
  2. Narrow it to a named audience and problem, for example "for overthinkers", "for women in perimenopause", "for adults with late-diagnosed ADHD" or "for people on GLP-1s".
  3. Use a practical structure: a workbook, a numbered plan, or a 30-day/10-week playbook.
  4. Keep it short, roughly 25k-60k words, at a low ebook price in line with the under-$5 norm among deal-seeking readers.
  5. Add a branded core concept, Let Them-style.
- Credible authorship and lived experience are a differentiator wherever AI-generated filler is common: AI how-to, self-help, gardening, pet care. Readers' reliance on reviews and recommendations (NielsenIQ) rewards trustworthy, specific books over generic ones.
- Contrarian or provocative framing is supported by examples (*Why Men Love Bitches*, *Abundance*, *If Anyone Builds It*), but the evidence base here is thinner than for the practical formats.

### Gaps
- No quantitative comparison was found of sales by book length, workbook vs narrative format, or "for beginners" vs general titles in 2025-26.
- No data was found on performance of "for women over 40/50" titles specifically, beyond menopause titles.
- The K-lytics claim that "AI-augmented" nonfiction (QR codes, templates, chatbots) is 34% of the Top 100 could not be verified from a primary source.
