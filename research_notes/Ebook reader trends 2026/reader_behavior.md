# Reader Behavior 2025-2026: Who Reads, Why, and How Consumption Is Shifting

Scope note: research conducted October 2026. US primary; UK and India secondary; brief notes on the Philippines and Nigeria. Every finding carries the source and its publication or fieldwork date. Older data is labeled with its year. Where a figure comes from an aggregator, vendor or self-selected sample, that is flagged.

---

## 1. Who reads (age, gender, income, country), how often, and in which format?

### Takeaway
US book reading is broad but shallow and concentrated. About 75% of adults read at least part of one book in the past year (Pew, Oct 2025), but the median American finished only about 2 books in 2025 (YouGov), and daily reading for pleasure has fallen about 40% in 20 years. Print still leads, yet ebooks (31%) and audiobooks (26%) have roughly doubled since 2011. Adults under 30 are the heaviest ebook users (41%). The typical ebook reader is younger, college-educated and more often female; Asian Americans over-index on ebooks. In the UK the shape is similar: a median of 3 books a year, with distraction the main barrier.

### Cited Findings
**US: incidence and formats (Pew, fieldwork Oct 6-16, 2025, n=8,046, published Apr 9, 2026)**
- 75% of US adults read all or part of at least one book in the past 12 months, in any format. 64% read print, 31% read an ebook and 26% listened to an audiobook. — [Pew Research Center, Apr 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/09/americans-still-opt-for-print-books-over-digital-or-audio-versions-few-are-in-book-clubs/)
- Trend from 2011 to 2025: print fell from 72% to 64%, ebooks rose from 17% to 31%, and audiobooks rose from 11% to 26%. — [Pew Research Center, Apr 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/09/americans-still-opt-for-print-books-over-digital-or-audio-versions-few-are-in-book-clubs/)
- By age: 18-29 year-olds have 78% any-format reading, 41% ebooks and 32% audiobooks. 30-49 year-olds: 76% / 33% / 33%. 50-64: 71% / 26% / 23%. 65+: 73% / 23% / 13%. — [Pew Research Center, Apr 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/09/americans-still-opt-for-print-books-over-digital-or-audio-versions-few-are-in-book-clubs/)
- By gender: 78% of women and 71% of men read a book. Women lead on print (68% vs 60%) and ebooks (33% vs 28%). Women are twice as likely to be in a book club (10% vs 5%); only 7% of adults overall are in one. — [Pew Research Center, Apr 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/09/americans-still-opt-for-print-books-over-digital-or-audio-versions-few-are-in-book-clubs/)
- By education: college graduates have 88% any-format reading and 42% ebooks. High school or less: 60% any format and 20% ebooks. — [Pew Research Center, Apr 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/09/americans-still-opt-for-print-books-over-digital-or-audio-versions-few-are-in-book-clubs/)
- By race/ethnicity: Asian Americans have the highest ebook rate (42%), compared with White 30%, Hispanic 29% and Black 27%. — [Pew Research Center, Apr 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/09/americans-still-opt-for-print-books-over-digital-or-audio-versions-few-are-in-book-clubs/)
- Income: the Pew summary I retrieved did not give an income breakdown (see Gaps).

**US: how many books (YouGov, fieldwork Dec 15-19, 2025, n=2,203)**
- 59% of Americans read at least one book in 2025, so 40% read none. 27% read 1-4 books, 13% read 5-9 and 19% read 10 or more. The median was 2 books and the mean was 8. The top 19% of readers account for 82% of all books read. — [YouGov US, Dec 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53804-most-americans-didnt-read-many-books-in-2025)
- Mean books read by age: 65+ read 12.1, 45-64 read 6.4, 30-44 read 8.2 and 18-29 read 5.8. Postgraduates had a median of 5 books (mean 13.6); people with a high school education or less had a median of 0 (mean 4.6). — [YouGov US, Dec 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53804-most-americans-didnt-read-many-books-in-2025)
- Formats in 2025: 46% read physical books, 24% digital books and 23% audiobooks; 29% used more than one format. — [YouGov US, Dec 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53804-most-americans-didnt-read-many-books-in-2025)
- Top genres among readers: mystery/crime 35%, history 30%, biography/memoir 27%, and thriller, fantasy and romance at 23% each. 51% have a library card; 24% borrow at least monthly. — [YouGov US, Dec 2025](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53804-most-americans-didnt-read-many-books-in-2025)
- Note on conflicting figures: Pew's 75% and YouGov's 59% differ mainly in wording. Pew counts "all or part of" a book; YouGov asks about books read in the year.

**US: long-run decline in daily pleasure reading**
- In the American Time Use Survey, the share of Americans reading for pleasure on an average day fell from 28% in 2004 to 16% in 2023, a drop of more than 40% (about 3% a year). Declines were steeper among Black Americans, people with lower income or education, and rural residents. — [University of Florida / UCL, iScience study, Aug 2025](https://news.ufl.edu/2025/08/reading-for-pleasure-study/); [UCL News, Aug 2025](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2025/aug/proportion-americans-reading-pleasure-fell-40-over-20-years)
- Gallup's most recent books-read poll (fielded late 2021) found an average of about 12 books, the lowest since Gallup began asking in the 1990s. I found no newer Gallup reading poll. — [NPR, Feb 20, 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/02/20/nx-s1-5298185/americans-are-reading-fewer-books-for-less-time-people-want-to-know-why)

**US: market by format (revenue)**
- In 2025, US trade revenue was $21.7B (+2.7%). Print accounted for 76.3% of trade revenue and digital formats for 21.5%. Trade ebook revenue rose 3.0% and trade digital audio 5.3%. Industry-wide, ebooks were $2.2B (+3.2%) and digital audio $2.5B (+5.3%). Adult fiction reached $7.1B (+3.9%) and adult nonfiction $6.0B (+1.3%). — [AAP StatShot Annual, released Aug 10, 2026](https://publishers.org/news/aap-statshot-annual-report-publishing-revenues-totaled-33-4-billion-for-calendar-year-2025/)
- Industry-wide, digital audio revenue ($2.5B) now exceeds ebook revenue ($2.2B). — [AAP, Aug 2026](https://publishers.org/news/aap-statshot-annual-report-publishing-revenues-totaled-33-4-billion-for-calendar-year-2025/)
- The KDP Select Global Fund, which pays Kindle Unlimited page-reads, ran at $51.9M-$59.9M a month in January-June 2025, about $340M for the half year. This points to a large parallel indie ebook economy that AAP does not count. — [Written Word Media KDP fund tracker, 2025](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/kdp-global-fund-payouts)

**UK**
- The median Briton read or listened to 3 books in the past year; 40% read none. Among people who read an ebook, 59% use a dedicated e-reader such as a Kindle, 20% a phone and 18% a tablet. 53% of Britons do not consider listening to an audiobook equivalent to reading. — [YouGov UK, fieldwork Jan 2025](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/51730-40-of-britons-havent-read-a-single-book-in-the-last-12-months)
- 53% of UK adults read regularly, down from 58% in 2015. 46% struggle to focus because of distractions, rising to 55% among 16-24s and 35-44s. 31% struggle to finish what they start (43% of 16-34s). 33% multitask while reading. — [The Reading Agency, State of the Nation's Adult Reading 2025, via World Book Night, Apr 2025](https://worldbooknight.org/news/nation-of-interrupted-readers); [Publishers Weekly, Apr 2025](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industrynews/publisher-news/article/97605-u-k-study-finds-growing-reading-crisis.html)
- Among children and young people aged 8-18, only 32.7% enjoy reading in their free time (a 20-year low) and 18.7% read daily. — [National Literacy Trust 2025 report, via ReadingZone, 2025](https://www.readingzone.com/news/national-literacy-trust-s-2025-reading-report-marks-20-year-low-in-reading-for-pleasure/)
- UK consumer publishing was £2.6B in 2025, of which print was £2.1B (+2%), digital £558M and audiobooks £255M. PA CEO Dan Conway: "In 2025 we were more likely to listen to a book, or to read content digitally, than ever before." — [Publishers Association 2025 figures, via Print Business, 2026](https://printbusiness.co.uk/publishing-figures-prove-books-retain-their-appeal/)
- UK fiction sales rose 8% in 2025, children's 7%, and nonfiction fell 3%. — [Jane Friedman, June 10, 2026](https://janefriedman.com/book-sales-update-uk-sales-grew-in-2025/)
- Ebooks and audiobooks make up about one-third of UK book purchases, and audiobooks reached their highest share on record in 2025. Overall purchases fell about 2% (Jan-Nov 2025). Women aged 13-44 account for 35% of purchases and men aged 13-44 for 27%. Crime/thriller is the favourite genre overall. Self-help and popular psychology rank highly for men under 45 and were among the growth genres. — [NielsenIQ BookData, "The UK Book Consumer 2025," Mar 13, 2026](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/insights/commentary/2026/the-uk-book-consumer-2025)

### Inferences
- The ebook audience skews younger, college-educated and female: 41% of 18-29s read ebooks against 23% of people 65+. This contradicts the popular claim that Gen Z rejects ebooks (see Section 2).
- A few heavy readers drive most of the market (YouGov: 19% of adults read 82% of books). An ebook author is effectively writing for that minority, and the Written Word Media survey in Section 5 profiles them as voracious, price-sensitive Kindle users.
- Audio is now the fastest-growing format and out-earns ebooks in US revenue. Being "ebook-only" leaves the growth channel unused.
- AAP figures cover publishers that report to it, so they likely understate self-published Kindle and Kindle Unlimited ebook reading. The roughly $340M paid out through the KDP fund in H1 2025 alone points to a large uncounted indie segment. This is an inference: I did not verify the exact Circana/AAP methodology.

### Gaps
- I found no Pew or YouGov 2025-26 breakdown by household income for ebook readers specifically. The ATUS study confirms only that pleasure reading fell faster among lower-income groups.
- No Gallup reading poll was fielded in 2025-26 as far as I could find.
- No 2025-26 National Literacy Trust adult survey turned up; NLT's 2025 headline data covers ages 5-18.

## 2. Gen Z and Millennials: BookTok, reading as identity, print vs ebook, apps, and reading challenges

### Takeaway
Gen Z is reading more than the panic suggests. 66% identify as readers (up 14 points since 2022), they read across print, ebook and audio, and BookTok is their top discovery engine (51% of UK 16-25s). Print is the visible, "aesthetic" format and the one the press favours, yet US 18-29s are the most likely group to read ebooks (41%). Social reading tools (Goodreads challenges, StoryGraph, Fable clubs and streaks) and reading-as-identity ("performative reading") shape behavior.

### Cited Findings
- 66% of Gen Z identify as readers (up 14 points from 2022), and 65% read at least one print book in the past 12 months (up from 59% in 2022). Gen Z reads "multimodally," switching between print, ebook and audiobook for the same book, and reads on Wattpad, Webtoon, Kindle Unlimited and similar platforms. 36% found books through librarians, up from 15% in 2022. Silent reading parties, literary cosplay and Bookstagram are rising. These are the authors' 2025 data, published in The Conversation. — [Kathi Inman Berens & Rachel Noorda (Portland State Univ.), The Conversation, Aug 4, 2026](https://theconversation.com/what-the-panic-over-gen-zs-reading-habits-gets-wrong-288338)
- From the same researchers' earlier study (2022-23 data): US Gen Z and Millennials read an average of about 2 print books a month, nearly double their ebook or audiobook rate. A wish for a "digital detox" partly explains the print preference. — [The Conversation / Berens & Noorda, 2023-2026 coverage](https://theconversation.com/what-the-panic-over-gen-zs-reading-habits-gets-wrong-288338); [World Economic Forum, Mar 2023](https://weforum.org/agenda/2023/03/gen-zers-are-bookworms-but-say-theyre-shunning-e-books-because-of-eye-strain-digital-detoxing-and-their-love-for-libraries)
- (2021-22 data) Print made up 80% of book purchases by UK buyers aged 13-24 (Nov 2021-Nov 2022) and ebooks 14%. — [World Economic Forum, Mar 2023, citing Nielsen](https://weforum.org/agenda/2023/03/gen-zers-are-bookworms-but-say-theyre-shunning-e-books-because-of-eye-strain-digital-detoxing-and-their-love-for-libraries)
- Conflicting evidence: Pew (Oct 2025) finds US 18-29s are the most likely of any age group to read ebooks (41%, against 31% of all adults). — [Pew Research Center, Apr 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/09/americans-still-opt-for-print-books-over-digital-or-audio-versions-few-are-in-book-clubs/)
- TikTok-commissioned research (Morning Consult, Apr 10-13, 2026, n=3,007 UK adults 16+) found TikTok is the #1 book discovery platform for young readers, with 51% of 16-25s discovering books there. #BookTok has 77M posts (+65% in a year). TikTok attributes 11M UK book sales and £86M in revenue to BookTok in 2025, and 78% of young BookTok users report increased interest in reading. TikTok is partnering with the UK's 2026 National Year of Reading and launching the first official monthly #BookTok bestseller list with Media Control. This is vendor-commissioned research. — [TikTok Newsroom, Apr 27, 2026](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-joins-the-national-year-of-reading-and-invites-teens-to-join-the-worlds-biggest-book-club?lang=en-GB)
- (2023 data) Among UK 14-25s, YouTube (34%) narrowly beat TikTok (32%) and Instagram (27%) for online book discovery. Friends remained the main source (41%), ahead of physical bookshops (36%). — [The Bookseller, on Nielsen's "Understanding the UK Children's Books Consumer," Aug 2023](https://thebookseller.com/news/youtube-narrowly-beats-tiktok-for-young-adults-discovering-books-online-according-to-nielsen-report)
- BookTok's sales effect is maturing. US adult fiction print units grew only 1% in 2025, against nearly 5% in 2024, and fantasy fell 8.7% (to 24.1M units) after the romantasy peak. Romance rose 3.9% to 44M units, graphic novels 9.2% and science fiction 22.1%. — [Publishers Weekly / Circana BookScan, Jan 9, 2026](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/bookselling/article/99417-print-book-sales-rose-slightly-in-2025.html)
- "Performative reading": commentary in 2025 (The Week, summarizing The Guardian and others) describes Gen Z using physical books as social-media props and personal branding. The physical book signals "taste and attention span" in contrast to AirPods. Commentators date the trend to the 2021 celebrity book-club and BookTok boom. — [The Week, 2025](https://www.theweek.com/culture-life/books/the-rise-of-performative-reading)
- In a Scribd/Everand/Fable survey of 1,600+ US adults aged 18-64 plus platform data (Jan-Nov 2025, released Dec 10, 2025), 64% of 18-24s said they read more than the previous year, against 56%+ of all respondents. 72% say reading has become more popular. Fable users averaged 20 books a year, and 55% more users than the year before met their 2025 goals. Average reading streaks reached 29 days (+300% YoY), and about 250,000 users kept 30-day streaks. 820,000+ people joined a new Fable book club in 2025. Fantasy became the #2 genre among male readers, and "spicy romance" peaks among Gen X rather than younger readers. — [Scribd Inc. "2026 State of Reading Report," Dec 10, 2025](https://www.scribdinc.com/newsroom/the-2026-state-of-reading-report-human-recommendations-surpass-algorithms-in-the-ai-era)
- The most-read titles on Everand/Fable in 2025 were Onyx Storm, Iron Flame and Fourth Wing (all Rebecca Yarros), Lights Out (Navessa Allen) and Sunrise on the Reaping (Suzanne Collins). — [Scribd Inc., Dec 10, 2025](https://www.scribdinc.com/newsroom/the-2026-state-of-reading-report-human-recommendations-surpass-algorithms-in-the-ai-era)
- StoryGraph has about 3M users (up from 2M in Dec 2023), against about 150M for Goodreads. The article is undated, probably 2024. — [Penguin Random House News for Authors, c. 2024](https://authornews.penguinrandomhouse.com/a-deep-dive-on-new-reader-platforms-fable-and-storygraph/)
- The Goodreads 2025 Reading Challenge counter showed about 7.5M participants pledging about 281M books. This is the site's live counter, read via search snippet. — [Goodreads 2025 Reading Challenge page, 2025](https://www.goodreads.com/user_challenges/63449103)
- Barnes & Noble planned about 60 new US stores in 2026, and independent bookstores grew about 70% in five years. — [The Conversation, Aug 4, 2026](https://theconversation.com/what-the-panic-over-gen-zs-reading-habits-gets-wrong-288338)

### Inferences
- For Gen Z the print copy works as a social object, but ebooks and Kindle Unlimited are where volume reading happens, especially for romance and romantasy binge-reading. An ebook author targeting under-30s should plan a print-on-demand edition with a "shelfie-worthy" cover for social posting, even if most consumption is digital.
- Reading-challenge and streak culture rewards books that can be finished. Shorter, "completable" books help readers hit their numbers, which favours novellas and series of shorter installments.
- BookTok's power has shifted from creating new mega-hits toward steady genre demand (romance up, fantasy cooling). Pitches built on "next romantasy doorstopper" are less safe in 2026 than in 2023-24.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025-26 Circana US survey splitting Gen Z format share (print vs ebook vs audio units). Claims about Gen Z ebook purchase share rely on 2022 UK Nielsen data.
- The Guardian's original "performative reading" piece was not retrieved directly; the claims come via The Week's summary.
- I found no 2025-26 official StoryGraph or Fable user totals beyond the figures above, and no official Goodreads press release on 2025 challenge totals.

## 3. Motivations: escapism, self-improvement, practical learning, comfort reads, and AI vs books for learning

### Takeaway
Leisure readers read mainly to relax, be entertained and escape (83-86%). Personal growth or learning is a minority motive (about 30%). Prescriptive "how-to" and self-help nonfiction is the category most exposed to AI: US self-help print units fell 26.3% in Q1 2026 after a strong 2025, and individual how-to authors report sales down 46-57%. Comfort reading (re-reading, cozy books, stress relief) is a strong, measurable motive.

### Cited Findings
**Why people read**
- In a self-selected sample of 3,589 active digital readers (Feb 2026), 86% read to relax, 83% to be entertained, 67% to escape and 30% for personal growth or learning. — [Written Word Media 2026 Reader Survey, Feb 2, 2026](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/reader-survey-2026/)
- In the Scribd survey (late 2025), 54% cite stress relief, 83% feel relaxed while reading and 50% re-read 3 or more books for comfort or escapism. Reading before bed rose 10% year over year. — [Scribd Inc. 2026 State of Reading Report, Dec 10, 2025](https://www.scribdinc.com/newsroom/the-2026-state-of-reading-report-human-recommendations-surpass-algorithms-in-the-ai-era)
- In the UK (Reading Agency coverage, 2025), 68% of regular readers report feeling happy, against 55% of lapsed readers and 43% of non-readers. Adults who read 30 minutes a week were 20% more likely to report greater life satisfaction. — [Twinkl News summarizing Reading Agency data, 2025](https://www.twinkl.co.uk/news/regular-adult-readers-in-the-uk-report-better-life-satisfaction-than-non-readers)
- Audiobook listeners value multitasking (86%), on-the-go listening (84%) and audio as an alternative to screen time (70%). — [Audio Publishers Association / Edison Research survey, Feb 2026, via Infodocket, Jun 5, 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/06/05/audio-publishers-association-reports-audiobook-sales-jump-9-to-2-43-billion/)

**Self-improvement demand: strong 2025, then a sharp Q1 2026 fall**
- US print self-help units rose 14.7% in 2025, led by one title selling more than 2.8M copies. The PW summary I retrieved did not name it; it is very likely Mel Robbins' The Let Them Theory, which was the #2 most-borrowed Libby ebook of 2025. Religion rose 5.4% to about 67M units. Business/economics fell 3.9% and biography/memoir 7%. — [Publishers Weekly / Circana, Jan 9, 2026](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/bookselling/article/99417-print-book-sales-rose-slightly-in-2025.html); [Libby Life, Jan 13, 2026](https://www.libbylife.com/blog/the-most-borrowed-books-in-the-libby-app-in-2025)
- In Q1 2026 (quarter ending Mar 28), total print units fell 3.1% to 163.5M. Adult nonfiction lost about 5.5M units. Self-help fell 26.3% and home/gardening 24.4%. Only crafts/hobbies/games (+9.6%) and religion (+1.6%) grew among the 16 adult nonfiction subcategories. — [Publishers Weekly, Apr 6, 2026](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20260406/100067-first-quarter-print-sales-fell-3-1.html)
- Tim Ferriss reports his BookScan print sales changed -5% in 2023, -13% in 2024, -46% in 2025, and are running at -57% in 2026. He links this to ChatGPT's launch (Nov 30, 2022) and argues prescriptive "information" nonfiction is collapsing into chatbots, while transformation- and story-driven nonfiction is more durable. This is one author's anecdotal data plus his interpretation; the causal attribution is his opinion. — [Tim Ferriss blog, Jun 12, 2026](https://tim.blog/2026/06/12/has-ai-already-killed-nonfiction/)

**AI as a learning substitute**
- 34% of US adults have used ChatGPT, including 58% of adults under 30. 26% of all adults have used it to learn something new (up from 8% in Mar 2023), including 46% of adults under 30. (Fieldwork Feb 24-Mar 2, 2025, n=5,123.) — [Pew Research Center, Jun 25, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/06/25/34-of-us-adults-have-used-chatgpt-about-double-the-share-in-2023/)
- Active ebook readers barely use AI for discovery: 7% use it occasionally, 21% are interested but haven't tried and 71% don't use it. Fewer than 1% name AI tools as a discovery source. — [Written Word Media 2026 Reader Survey, Feb 2026](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/reader-survey-2026/)
- Supply-side AI pressure: in a study of 14,419 self-published KDP titles (Jan 2023-Mar 2026, using the Pangram classifier), books with substantial AI text (>25%) reached about 20% of the catalog by Q2 2026 but earned only 12.1% of launch-window sales. Released titles grew 38.3-fold while revenue grew only 8.9-fold, so revenue per selling book fell in most genres. Human-written books' share of Top-25 rank slots fell from about 88% to about 63% in the most AI-exposed genres (crime/suspense romance, sci-fi/dystopian, mystery/thriller). The study covered genre fiction, not how-to. — [arXiv preprint "Generative AI floods and dilutes the market for books," Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20349v2)
- Over 80% of readers prefer human narration; fewer than 1% prefer AI narration. — [Written Word Media, Feb 2026](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/reader-survey-2026/)
- Only 16% of audiobook listeners have tried AI-voiced audiobooks. Willingness fell from 70% (2025 survey) to 61% (2026 survey). AI narration was 0.03% of 2025 audiobook revenue. — [APA/Edison, via Infodocket, Jun 5, 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/06/05/audio-publishers-association-reports-audiobook-sales-jump-9-to-2-43-billion/)

### Inferences
- An ebook built on information alone ("10 steps to X") now competes with a free, personalized chatbot, and Q1 2026 data suggests readers are acting on that. Nonfiction that survives is likely to offer what AI can't easily replicate: a trusted personal voice, lived story or case narrative, a structured transformation program, community, or accountability. Interactive elements such as worksheets and challenges also help. Note this is an inference from Ferriss's anecdote plus one bad quarter, not a settled trend.
- Escapism and comfort dominate leisure motives. For fiction, cozy, romance, thriller and "comfort re-read" positioning matches stated motives.
- Human authorship is becoming a selling point in a flooded market: readers reject AI narration and dilution hurts discoverability. Visible human authorship and author-reader connection (see newsletters, Section 5) are worth signalling.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025-26 survey directly asking readers whether they chose a chatbot or YouTube instead of a book to learn a skill. The evidence is indirect (sales declines, ChatGPT-for-learning adoption).
- Circana category data for Q2/Q3 2026 (to confirm whether the self-help drop persisted) was not retrieved.
- The "self-help" rise in 2025 was concentrated in one blockbuster, so the underlying category trend is ambiguous.

## 4. Attention and length: short books, novellas, summaries, and audiobook growth

### Takeaway
Distraction and difficulty finishing books are widely reported (46% and 31% of UK adults), and the industry is responding with short-form formats. Spotify actively commissions 1-2 hour "short-form audiobooks," and publishers talk up slim novels and short stories. Sales evidence for short fiction is mixed, though: UK short-story sales peaked in 2024 and fell about a third in 2025. Audio is the clear growth format: APA reports $2.43B in 2025 (+9%) and Spotify reports audiobook listening hours up 60% YoY.

### Cited Findings
**Attention**
- 46% of UK adults struggle to focus on reading because of distractions (55% of 16-24s), and 31% struggle to finish books (43% of 16-34s). Lack of free time (24%) is the top cause of declining focus; changes in screen or digital habits account for 10%. — [Reading Agency 2025, via World Book Night, Apr 2025](https://worldbooknight.org/news/nation-of-interrupted-readers); [The Bookseller, 2025](https://thebookseller.com/news/half-of-uk-adults-do-not-read-regularly-according-to-worrying-new-survey-by-the-reading-agency)

**Short fiction and novellas**
- UK Short Stories & Fiction Anthologies sold £6.7M (636,919 units) in 2024, the peak year, against £2.8M in 2020. Sales in 2025 year to date were down about one-third against 2024, and only one title exceeded 10,000 copies. Publishers nonetheless report an attitude shift ("something is slowly shifting"; "there's a real excitement around stories again"). They credit shorter attention spans, anthology TV, BookTok virality and lapsed readers wanting a lower time commitment, while noting "the sales ceiling is lower." — [The Bookseller, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.thebookseller.com/news/publishers-say-mindset-around-short-stories-shifting-as-readers-embrace-slim-fiction)
- Commentary in 2025 describes short novels and novellas as a trend (Esquire and LitHub pieces) and notes small presses favour 120-page novellas as cheaper and lower-risk for readers. This is opinion and trade commentary, not sales data. — [Shelf Media Group, 2025](https://shelfmediagroup.com/feature/shorter-is-stronger/)
- Counterpoint: the most-read and most-borrowed 2025 titles are long, for example Onyx Storm (a romantasy doorstopper) topping Libby audiobooks and Everand/Fable reads. — [Libby Life, Jan 13, 2026](https://www.libbylife.com/blog/the-most-borrowed-books-in-the-libby-app-in-2025); [Scribd Inc., Dec 2025](https://www.scribdinc.com/newsroom/the-2026-state-of-reading-report-human-recommendations-surpass-algorithms-in-the-ai-era)

**Short-form audio**
- Spotify solicits original short-form audiobooks of 10,000-20,000 words (about 1-2 hours) from indie authors in adult romance, mystery/thriller and sci-fi/fantasy, "written specifically for audio." Spotify pays an advance and royalties, and authors keep print and ebook rights. — [Publishers Marketplace, Mar 2025](https://lunch.publishersmarketplace.com/2025/03/spotify-audiobooks-solicits-short-audio-content-from-independent-authors/); [MIDiA Research, 2025](https://midiaresearch.com/blog/with-short-form-audiobooks-spotify-has-become-an-independent-book-publisher)

**Audiobook growth**
- US audiobook sales were $2.43B in 2025 (+9%) with 750,000+ active titles (+43%). Listeners averaged 3.8 audiobooks a year, and 26% listened to 4 or more. Access channels: 49% buy direct, 48% use subscriptions, 46% borrow via library apps and 42% use credits. General fiction is the top genre by revenue (27%), followed by SF/fantasy, romance and mystery/thriller. Humor, general fiction and children's/YA grew fastest. — [APA via Infodocket, Jun 5, 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/06/05/audio-publishers-association-reports-audiobook-sales-jump-9-to-2-43-billion/)
- In the 2026 APA/Edison survey, 63% listened in the last year and 35% in the last month. The base appears to be the survey's 1,706 spoken-word audio listeners, not all adults. — [APA via Infodocket, Jun 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/06/05/audio-publishers-association-reports-audiobook-sales-jump-9-to-2-43-billion/)
- In the 2025 APA survey, 51% of US adults (about 134M) had ever listened to an audiobook. 35% of listeners had used YouTube for audiobooks (up from 27%), much of it pirated. — [Book Riot on APA 2025 Consumer Survey, 2025](https://bookriot.com/audio-publishers-association-consumer-survey-2025/)
- Spotify (May 21, 2026 Investor Day): audiobook listening hours are up 60% YoY, nearly half of audiobook listeners started in the past year, and the catalog is 700,000+ titles in 22 markets. Audiobooks+ has 1M subscribers (about $100M ARR). PageMatch (switching between print/ebook and audio) gives up to a 55% monthly listening uplift. Family and Student Audiobooks+ plans are due later in 2026, and creation tools enter beta in June 2026. — [Spotify Newsroom, May 21, 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-05-21/investor-day-audiobook-features-updates/)
- In Libby library lending for 2025, audiobook checkouts rose 13% (to 315.9M) while ebooks rose only 3% (to 379.4M). — [OverDrive via Infodocket, Jan 21, 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/01/21/new-data-from-overdrive-libraries-break-digital-lending-records-in-2025-with-over-820-million-checkouts-and-over-1-billion-minutes-streamed/)

**Summary apps**
- Blinkist claims 9,000+ title summaries and describes Headway as having about 2,000. Pricing is about $8.33/month for Blinkist and $7.50/month for Headway, billed annually. This is a vendor comparison page; no independent 2025-26 user or revenue numbers were found. — [Blinkist, "Blinkist vs Headway" 2026](https://www.blinkist.com/learn/blinkist-vs-headway-which-book-summary-app-is-better-in-2026)

### Inferences
- The case for shorter ebooks rests on attention and completion data and on platform demand (Spotify short-form, reading streaks), not on proven sales uplift. Short works may sell at lower price points but suit series and "read-through" strategies.
- For nonfiction, the bite-size, summary-style niche is contested by apps and chatbots. A short nonfiction ebook should offer something summaries can't, such as a framework with exercises, a personal narrative or a specific niche.
- An audio edition, ideally human-narrated, or a script written for audio, is now close to essential. Audio grows 3-4x faster than ebooks in both library lending and Spotify listening, and a 10-20k-word piece fits Spotify's short-form spec.

### Gaps
- I found no US sales data comparing performance by book length or page count in 2025-26.
- There are no independent user counts for Blinkist or Headway in 2025-26.
- No US data on novella sales specifically was found.

## 5. Discovery: how readers find books, plus reviews and covers

### Takeaway
Personal recommendations remain the top discovery route for general readers (Scribd, Dec 2025). For heavy ebook readers, Amazon itself (68%) and email newsletters (64%) dominate, ahead of Goodreads, friends and social media. BookTok leads among under-25s. Libraries (Libby) are a growing, under-appreciated channel. AI-based discovery is negligible so far. Covers still drive choice: in 2023, 57% of Americans said they pick books by cover.

### Cited Findings
- "People I know personally" is the top discovery source, ahead of platforms, social media and AI tools, and sharing a book with friends or family is now the most common post-read action. Many readers describe a "discovery crisis" in which algorithms "don't quite get their tastes." In-person book club participation rose to 33% (+8% YoY); 37% participated in any club. — [Scribd Inc. 2026 State of Reading Report, Dec 10, 2025](https://www.scribdinc.com/newsroom/the-2026-state-of-reading-report-human-recommendations-surpass-algorithms-in-the-ai-era); [Publishers Weekly, Dec 15, 2025](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20251215/99270-word-of-mouth-recs-best-ai-at-everand-fable.html)
- Among heavy digital readers (Feb 2026, n=3,589, skewed to bargain ebook downloaders), discovery runs: Amazon 68%, email newsletters 64%, Goodreads 46%, friends/family 45%, social media 42%, author websites 30%, libraries 29%, bookstores 27%, AI tools <1%. Readers follow authors via email newsletters (49%) and Facebook (38%), with TikTok at only 2%. 67% want updates on future projects. Over 80% say they're extremely likely to read more by an author they enjoyed. 26% subscribe to Kindle Unlimited. Most spend under $5 per ebook (median $15/month, mean $29.56/month). The median reader reads 6 books a month, and a third read 100+ a year. 76% read ebooks, 61% on a dedicated e-reader, 51% read print and 22% listen to audio. — [Written Word Media 2026 Reader Survey, Feb 2, 2026](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/reader-survey-2026/)
- Reviews: 22% of these readers leave reviews often, 30% sometimes and 17% never; more than half actively track their reading. — [Written Word Media, Feb 2026](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/reader-survey-2026/)
- Among UK 16-25s, 51% discover books via TikTok (Morning Consult for TikTok, Apr 2026). — [TikTok Newsroom, Apr 27, 2026](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-joins-the-national-year-of-reading-and-invites-teens-to-join-the-worlds-biggest-book-club?lang=en-GB)
- Among Gen Z, 36% found books through librarians (up from 15% in 2022), and about a third bought at physical bookstores. — [Berens & Noorda, The Conversation, Aug 4, 2026](https://theconversation.com/what-the-panic-over-gen-zs-reading-habits-gets-wrong-288338)
- Covers (2023 data): 57% of Americans say they choose to buy or read a book based on its cover (ThriftBooks/OnePoll). — [Malay Mail, Nov 28, 2023](https://malaymail.com/news/life/2023/11/28/turns-out-most-readers-do-judge-a-book-by-its-cover/104565)
- Libraries as an audiobook channel: 46% of audiobook listeners borrow via digital library apps (APA, 2026), and 51% of heavy-reader audiobook users use Libby/Hoopla (Written Word Media, 2026). 63% value library-app access (APA, 2025). — [APA via Infodocket, Jun 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/06/05/audio-publishers-association-reports-audiobook-sales-jump-9-to-2-43-billion/); [Written Word Media, Feb 2026](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/reader-survey-2026/); [Book Riot, 2025](https://bookriot.com/audio-publishers-association-consumer-survey-2025/)

### Inferences
- The recommended discovery stack differs by audience. For adult genre-fiction ebook readers it is Amazon optimization (categories, KU, also-boughts), an author newsletter and price promotions. For under-25s it is TikTok/BookTok plus a print edition worth photographing.
- Newsletters are both a discovery channel and a retention channel (49% follow authors that way). Building one from book 1 is supported by the data.
- AI discovery tools are not yet a meaningful reader channel, so optimizing for chatbots is speculative in 2026.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025-26 representative US survey quantifying discovery channels for all adults with percentages; the Scribd report gives rankings but only partial percentages.
- There is no 2025-26 data on cover influence or star-rating thresholds; the cover stat is 2023.
- No podcast-as-discovery-channel data was found for 2025-26.

## 6. International: India (Kindle, Pratilipi, regional languages, genres) and other English-reading markets (UK, Philippines, Nigeria)

### Takeaway
India is a large, fast-growing, mostly print and education-driven market: ₹1.08 lakh crore in 2024-25, projected ₹2 lakh crore by 2030-31, with digital publishing about ₹7,048 crore. Growth is led by Tier 2 cities (twice metro growth on Amazon), regional languages (Malayalam and Tamil demand doubled YoY), exam and career prep (+30-50%), self-help/business and Indian-author fiction (thrillers, mythology retellings, "desi romance"). Mobile-first, micro-payment serialized fiction and audio (Pratilipi, Pocket FM), much of it in Hindi, is where young Indians read and listen digitally. The Philippines (Wattpad) and Nigeria (ebook-first indie publishing, romance) are mobile-first English markets, but hard data is thin.

### Cited Findings
**India: market size**
- India's book publishing market was about ₹1.08 lakh crore in 2024-25, projected to reach ₹2 lakh crore by 2030-31 (~11% CAGR). Print is ₹95,816 crore, digital publishing ₹7,048 crore (about 6.5% of the total) and scholarly journals ₹5,150 crore. India has 26,000+ publishers, and growth drivers include education, Indian-language content and digital adoption. — [India Book Market Report 2026-2030 (NielsenIQ BookData for the Federation of Indian Publishers), launched Sep 3, 2026, via India CSR](https://indiacsr.in/india-book-publishing-market-2-lakh-crore-2030-31-report/); [PublishNews (Brazil), Sep 8, 2026](https://www.publishnews.com.br/materias/2026/09/08/mercado-de-livros-impressos-da-india-e-estimado-em-rs-52-bilhoes)
- India is described as a "high-growth market" with revenue up 20.7% in 2025. The underlying source of this figure was not verified. — [All About Book Publishing, 2026](https://allaboutbookpublishing.com/?p=14889)

**India: Amazon demand signals**
- Amazon India reported double-digit growth in new book customers and category sales. Tier 2 cities grew at twice the metro rate. Literary fiction, self-help, business and children's books showed strong double-digit growth. Malayalam and Tamil book demand doubled YoY, government exam preparation grew 30%+, and engineering and law materials grew 50%+. The catalog is 20M+ titles across 14+ Indian languages. The release contains no Kindle-specific data. — [Amazon India press release, World Book Day 2025 (published Aug 26, 2025)](https://press.aboutamazon.com/in/news/retail/2025/8/celebrating-the-joy-of-reading-amazon-india-deepens-mission-to-make-india-read-more-on-world-book-day-2025)
- (2016 data, for context) Kindle Unlimited launched in India at ₹199/month, and Kindle supports Hindi, Tamil, Marathi, Gujarati and Malayalam ebooks. — [The Bookseller, 2016](https://www.thebookseller.com/news/amazon-introduces-kindle-unlimited-india-311414); [Deccan Chronicle, Dec 2016](https://deccanchronicle.com/technology/in-other-news/011216/kindle-to-support-e-books-in-5-indian-languages.html)
- Statista forecasts India's ebook users will reach about 144M by 2029 (+35.5M from 2024). This is a model-based forecast seen via search snippet. — [Statista Market Insights, ebooks India](https://statista.com/outlook/amo/media/books/ebooks/india)

**India: genres**
- According to Nielsen data cited by Penguin Random House India, adult fiction was the fastest-growing trade segment in India. PRH India's adult fiction share of value rose from 20% in 2022 to 27% in 2024. Thrillers, romance and mythology are rising, and self-improvement, entrepreneurship, personal finance, mythology, history, spirituality and leadership are cited as sought-after genres. PRH's June-Dec 2025 fiction campaign groups the market into "Love, Lies, Laash" (thrillers), "India's Mythoverse" (mythology retellings) and "Desi Romance." — [Penguin Random House India newsroom, 2025](https://www.penguin.co.in/newsroom/penguin-unveils-multi-pronged-fiction-campaign-to-champion-and-celebrate-the-surge-in-indian-storytelling/)

**India: mobile-first vernacular platforms**
- Pratilipi: 12M+ monthly active readers in 12 languages (Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Kannada, Telugu, Marathi, Gujarati, Malayalam, Punjabi, Odia, Urdu, English), 15M+ stories and 1.6M+ writers. FY25 revenue was about ₹86-87 crore, up from ₹58 crore in FY24. Pratilipi owns Westland Books, Pratilipi Comics and Pratilipi FM. This comes from a secondary newsletter profile and should be treated as indicative. — [Think Tank newsletter (beehiiv), 2025](https://think-tank-55e4a2.beehiiv.com/p/the-storytelling-startup-that-gave-bharat-a-voice)
- Pocket FM surveyed 20,538 people (Jun 5-Jul 20, 2025). 84% were aged 18-35 (over 50% aged 18-24), and 55% were male. 50% listen 90+ minutes a day and nearly half binge 10+ episodes a day. Genres: drama 40%, romance 37%, sci-fi/fantasy 37%, thriller/horror 34%. 86% prefer episodic audio dramas over audiobooks or podcasts. Hindi accounts for 53%, with Telugu, Kannada, Tamil and Malayalam growing. 75% prefer paying per episode over a subscription, and over 80% are comfortable with AI-created content "if emotionally resonant." This is a vendor survey. — [Pocket FM "Entertainment Insights '25," via MediaInfoline, Sep 4, 2025](https://www.mediainfoline.com/uncategorized/pocket-fm-entertainment-insights-25-how-audio-series-became-indias-new-screen)

**UK** (detail in Section 1)
- UK consumer publishing was £2.6B in 2025 (digital £558M, audio £255M). Fiction rose 8% and nonfiction fell 3%. TikTok attributes 11M UK book sales to BookTok in 2025. — [Print Business / PA, 2026](https://printbusiness.co.uk/publishing-figures-prove-books-retain-their-appeal/); [Jane Friedman, Jun 2026](https://janefriedman.com/book-sales-update-uk-sales-grew-in-2025/); [TikTok Newsroom, Apr 2026](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-joins-the-national-year-of-reading-and-invites-teens-to-join-the-worlds-biggest-book-club?lang=en-GB)

**Philippines**
- (Older data, date uncertain, likely mid-2010s) About 7M Filipinos visit Wattpad monthly, second only to the US, with 13M+ story uploads. Anvil Publishing partnered with Wattpad on the Bliss Books YA/romance imprint. — [All About Book Publishing, undated](https://allaboutbookpublishing.com/5507/anvil-publishing-encouraging-reading-in-philippines/)
- Statista describes the Philippine ebook market as targeted at ages 15-34 and driven by mobile reading apps. This is a model-based outlook. — [Statista, ebooks Philippines](https://statista.com/outlook/amo/media/books/ebooks/philippines)

**Nigeria**
- Nigerian writers are launching ebook-first, crowdfunding print runs and engaging readers via newsletters and WhatsApp. Romance, fantasy, crime, children's and short stories are thriving, while the country "continues to struggle with a weak culture of leisure reading." — [BusinessDay (Nigeria), "The State of Books and the Book Ecosystem in Nigeria, 2026," Jan 18, 2026](https://businessday.ng/life/article/executive-bookshelf-18-january-2026/)
- Masobe Books' romance A Very Gidi Christmas sold about 3,000 copies in under a year, cited as a notable success in the Nigerian market. — [Brittle Paper, Jul 2025](https://brittlepaper.com/2025/07/what-one-romance-novel-can-teach-us-about-nigerias-reading-culture/)

### Inferences
- For an English-language ebook aimed at India, the strongest data-backed lanes are career and exam prep, self-help and personal finance, business, and Indian-rooted fiction (mythology retellings, thrillers, desi romance). Digital is still under 7% of India's market, so ebook pricing must be low (KU at ₹199/month sets expectations), and a print-on-demand edition matters.
- Young Indian digital readers are mobile-first and serialized, prefer micro-payments and often use Hindi or regional languages. A serialized or episodic structure, and possibly a Hindi edition or translation, fits that behavior better than one long English ebook.
- In Nigeria and the Philippines, mobile-first romance and YA dominate digital reading. WhatsApp and newsletters are distribution channels, and price points must be very low. The data here is thin.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025-26 Kindle India-specific data (ebook unit growth, top Kindle titles, KU India subscriber counts) or a 2025 Amazon India "reading trends" list. The latest city-level Amazon reading reports found were from 2016-2018 and are omitted as outdated.
- No language split (English vs Hindi vs regional share) from the 2026 India Book Market Report was available in public summaries. A circulating claim that regional-language books are "35% of the market" came from an unverified aggregator and is not included as a finding.
- Pratilipi figures come from a secondary profile, not company filings.
- No reliable 2025-26 data was found on Philippine or Nigerian ebook market size, OkadaBooks usage, or UK Kindle/KU usage.

## 7. Library ebook lending (OverDrive/Libby 2025): volumes and what is borrowed

### Takeaway
Library digital lending hit a record 820.5M checkouts in 2025 (+10%). Ebooks grew only 3% while audiobooks (+13%), magazines (+31%) and comics (+22%) grew fastest. The most-borrowed ebooks are mainstream bestselling fiction (historical fiction, romance and romantasy, thriller). The one nonfiction title in the top ten is a self-help book (Mel Robbins' The Let Them Theory, #2).

### Cited Findings
- 2025 OverDrive totals (Libby and Sora): 820.5M digital checkouts (+10%), of which public libraries 737.6M (+9%) and schools 63.4M (+14%). By format: ebooks 379.4M (+3%), audiobooks 315.9M (+13%), magazines 125.1M (+31%) and comics 55.7M (+22%). By audience: adult titles 520.4M (+7%) and children's/YA 174.6M (+9%). More than 200 library systems passed 1M digital checkouts for the first time. — [OverDrive via Infodocket, Jan 21, 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/01/21/new-data-from-overdrive-libraries-break-digital-lending-records-in-2025-with-over-820-million-checkouts-and-over-1-billion-minutes-streamed/)
- For comparison, 2024 had 739M+ checkouts. — [OverDrive via Infodocket, Jan 27, 2025](https://www.infodocket.com/2025/01/27/new-data-from-overdrive-libraries-break-digital-lending-records-in-2024-with-over-739-million-checkouts/)
- Top 10 Libby ebooks of 2025: The Women (Kristin Hannah), The Let Them Theory (Mel Robbins), The Wedding People (Alison Espach), Onyx Storm (Rebecca Yarros), Great Big Beautiful Life (Emily Henry), Sunrise on the Reaping (Suzanne Collins), The God of the Woods (Liz Moore), A Court of Thorns and Roses (Sarah J. Maas), Fourth Wing (Rebecca Yarros), Just for the Summer (Abby Jimenez). — [Libby Life (OverDrive blog), Jan 13, 2026](https://www.libbylife.com/blog/the-most-borrowed-books-in-the-libby-app-in-2025)
- Top Libby audiobooks of 2025: Onyx Storm, The Women, Pride and Prejudice, The Crash (Freida McFadden), Fourth Wing, The Housemaid (McFadden), The Wedding People, Great Big Beautiful Life, The Tenant (McFadden), The Four Winds (Hannah). — [Libby Life, Jan 13, 2026](https://www.libbylife.com/blog/the-most-borrowed-books-in-the-libby-app-in-2025)
- Top authors by digital checkouts in 2025: Jeff Kinney, James Patterson, Freida McFadden, Sarah J. Maas, Lincoln Peirce, Rebecca Yarros, Dav Pilkey, Kristin Hannah, David Baldacci, Nora Roberts. — [Libby Life, Jan 13, 2026](https://www.libbylife.com/blog/the-most-borrowed-books-in-the-libby-app-in-2025); [OverDrive via Infodocket, Jan 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/01/21/new-data-from-overdrive-libraries-break-digital-lending-records-in-2025-with-over-820-million-checkouts-and-over-1-billion-minutes-streamed/)
- The leading lending systems were Los Angeles Public Library (US), Toronto Public Library (Canada) and the National Library Board, Singapore. — [OverDrive via Infodocket, Jan 2026](https://www.infodocket.com/2026/01/21/new-data-from-overdrive-libraries-break-digital-lending-records-in-2025-with-over-820-million-checkouts-and-over-1-billion-minutes-streamed/)

### Inferences
- Library digital demand mirrors retail: women's fiction, romance and romantasy, domestic thrillers (McFadden appears three times in the audio top 10) and one mega self-help title.
- Ebook lending growth (+3%) lags audio (+13%). This is consistent with Pew, AAP and Spotify data showing audio as the expansion format.
- Indie or self-published ebooks rarely reach library catalogs at scale. An indie author would need distributors that feed OverDrive (such as Draft2Digital or PublishDrive) to use this channel. This is an inference; distribution mechanics were not researched here.

### Gaps
- OverDrive's public 2025 summary does not include genre or subject share of checkouts (for example, the percentage of fiction vs nonfiction or self-help), or a dedicated nonfiction top-10.
- No Libby user demographics (age, gender) were published in the 2025 release.
