# Families, NRIs, elderly parents and life moments

### A printed family newspaper for grandparents, every month
**In one line:** The family sends photos and news on WhatsApp; grandparents get them as a printed newspaper every month.
**What's missing in India today:** Many grandparents find busy WhatsApp groups and small phone screens hard to follow, so they miss most family photos and news. A quick search found no one in India who turns a family's updates into a printed paper they can hold, reread and show friends.
**How it works:**
1. A son, daughter or grandchild (often abroad) signs up, and you add the whole family to a WhatsApp group that you run.
2. Through the month, everyone sends photos with a line or two of news.
3. Your freelance designer puts them into a ready-made layout: 8–12 pages, big letters, in English or the grandparents' own language.
4. A local printer prints it and a courier delivers it to the grandparents' door in the first week of every month.

**Who pays and how much:** NRI and out-of-town children. About ₹999 a month, or about ₹9,999 a year paid in advance. Design, printing and courier cost you about ₹350–450 a copy.
**Proof it works abroad:** Famileo, France: over 260,000 families have subscribed to its printed family "gazette" for grandparents, which starts at $9.99 a month. ([source](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1018182135))
**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search. Only foreign services (Famileo, Lineage, Familink) came up.
**To start:** About ₹60,000 (layout design, sample issues, ads aimed at Indians in the US, UK, Canada and the Gulf). About 3–4 weeks to the first paying family. Main risk: families stop sending photos after two months, so you must keep nudging them.

### Picture books read aloud in a grandparent's own voice
**In one line:** Grandparents in India record themselves reading a picture book; grandchildren abroad hear them every time they open it.
**What's missing in India today:** NRI children mostly hear their grandparents on rushed video calls across time zones. Books that play a grandparent's recorded voice on each page are a common gift in the US, but a quick search found none sold in India, and none in Hindi, Tamil, Bengali or other Indian languages.
**How it works:**
1. The NRI parent chooses a book (a classic picture book or a short Indian folk tale) and orders online.
2. Your partner, a trained student helper, visits the grandparents with the book. The grandparents read each page aloud in their own language and add a short message.
3. A printer adds a QR code on each page that plays that page's recording on any phone. A costlier version has a small built-in speaker in the book.
4. You ship the finished book abroad, or hand it over when the family visits in December.

**Who pays and how much:** NRI parents and grandparents. About ₹2,999 for the QR-code book and about ₹4,999 for the built-in-speaker book, plus overseas shipping.
**Proof it works abroad:** Hallmark, US: sells 29 recordable storybook titles, most at $39.99, so family members far away can "read" to children. ([source](https://www.hallmark.com/baby-and-kids/kids-books/recordable-storybooks/))
**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search. Hallmark sells these books in the US, Canada and Australia.
**To start:** About ₹80,000 (first 30 books, printing, home-visit helpers, ads). About 4 weeks to the first order. Main risk: imported sound parts can be unreliable, so start with the QR-code version, which needs no electronics.

### Flight companions for parents flying alone to visit their children abroad
**In one line:** A checked, friendly helper flies with elderly parents from the Indian airport to their child waiting at arrivals.
**What's missing in India today:** Every year many Indian parents fly alone to the US, UK, Canada or Australia to visit children, often for months. Many fear transit airports, forms and long walks, and their children lose sleep. Airlines give a wheelchair, but no one sits with them through the whole journey.
**How it works:**
1. The child abroad books online and shares the parents' flight details.
2. You match a companion who is already flying that route (for example, students and young professionals going back after holidays). Every companion has police verification, a short training and a written agreement.
3. The companion meets the parents at check-in, sits near them, helps through transit and immigration, and sends WhatsApp updates to the child.
4. The companion hands the parents over to the child at arrivals.

**Who pays and how much:** NRI children. About ₹15,000–25,000 per one-way trip; you pay the companion about ₹8,000–12,000.
**Proof it works abroad:** Juurnee, Australia: more than 12,000 people signed up in its first year to match travellers with paid flight companions; listings include helping solo parents on flights to India, and one helper earned £270 for a 25-hour trip. ([source](https://travellingforbusiness.co.uk/news/travel-companion-service-juurnee-launches-uk/))
**Who does this in India now:** No paid one-to-one flight companion service found in a quick search. Close but different: SeniorWorld (Haryana) runs escorted group holidays for people over 55, and Goodfellows offers companionship to seniors in their own city.
**To start:** About ₹70,000 (verification, training, a simple booking form, ads in NRI Facebook groups). About 3–4 weeks to the first booking; December–January is the busiest season. Main risk: a health problem mid-flight. Companions must never give medical help, and every traveller must have travel insurance.

### Portrait day for grandparents, with hair and make-up
**In one line:** Grandparents get dressed up, have hair and make-up done, and receive a beautiful framed portrait.
**What's missing in India today:** Most families have no good recent photo of their parents, only blurry phone pictures. When one is needed, for a 75th birthday, an anniversary or sadly a prayer meeting, they scramble. A quick search found no Indian studio that specialises in making older people look and feel their best.
**How it works:**
1. Children (often abroad) book a slot for their parents, or the parents book for themselves.
2. A car brings them to a partner studio, where a make-up artist spends about 45 unhurried minutes on hair and make-up.
3. A partner photographer takes solo and couple portraits, and the parents pick their favourites on a screen.
4. You deliver a large framed print and the digital photos, and send the files to the children abroad.

**Who pays and how much:** Adult children (local and NRI) and older people themselves. About ₹6,999 for one person and about ₹9,999 for a couple, framed print included.
**Proof it works abroad:** Egao Shashinkan, Japan: a Tokyo studio only for people over 60 photographs 100–120 people a month and has served more than 5,000 clients, starting each session with 40–60 minutes of hair and make-up. ([source](https://intojapanwaraku.com/culture/63787/))
**Who does this in India now:** No dedicated studio for older people found in a quick search. General portrait studios (such as MemoryCraft Studio in Pune) do one-off shoots.
**To start:** About ₹50,000 (hiring a studio for test days, a sample shoot with 5 older models, frames, ads). About 2–3 weeks. Main risk: the idea can feel sad, so sell it as a celebration ("Look your best at 70"), never as a funeral photo.

### "What next" retreats for people who have just retired
**In one line:** A 3-day stay at a resort where newly retired people plan the next 20 years of their life.
**What's missing in India today:** People who retire at 58–60 often feel lost: no office, no title, and children far away. A quick search found no place in India where they can pause with others in the same situation and plan purpose, health, money and relationships for the next stage of life.
**How it works:**
1. Retirees, or their children as a gift, book a seat in a 3-day retreat for 20 people at a partner resort near the city.
2. You build the programme with partners: a life coach, a money planner (talks only), a fitness trainer, and older guests who built a happy "second innings".
3. Everyone leaves with a written personal plan and a WhatsApp group of new friends.
4. You run a paid monthly online meet-up afterwards.

**Who pays and how much:** Retiring professionals, government officers and their children. About ₹35,000–45,000 per person including the stay; the resort costs about ₹12,000–15,000 per person. Big employers can also buy seats for staff who are about to retire.
**Proof it works abroad:** Modern Elder Academy, Mexico: charges $5,500 for a week-long course and has 3,000 alumni with an average age of 54. ([source](https://monocle.com/business/entrepreneurialism/modern-elder-academy-mexico-older-entrepreneurs/))
**Who does this in India now:** No residential retreat like this found in a quick search; only one-day talks, such as a Brahma Kumaris session for officers about to retire.
**To start:** About ₹1,00,000 (resort advance, partner fees, ads). About 6–8 weeks to the first retreat. Main risk: filling the first 20 seats, so sell seats in advance to the staff (HR) teams at banks, government companies and large firms.

### Rooms in older people's homes for students, in return for help and company
**In one line:** You match older people who have a spare room with a checked student who pays low rent and helps.
**What's missing in India today:** Many older couples and widows live alone in big flats after their children move away, while students and young job-starters pay high rents. A quick search found no service in India that matches them safely, with checks and clear rules, so neither side dares to try it.
**How it works:**
1. Older homeowners (or their children abroad) sign up, and you visit to see the room and agree house rules.
2. Students and young professionals apply. You do police verification, college or office checks and an interview.
3. You introduce a match, run a trial week, and use a simple written agreement: low rent plus about 10 hours a week of help, such as shopping, phone help and evening company.
4. You check in every month and sort out problems; either side can end it with notice.

**Who pays and how much:** The young person pays you about ₹3,000–5,000 a month, plus a low rent to the homeowner. Children abroad pay a one-time setup fee of about ₹10,000.
**Proof it works abroad:** THE Homeshare, Ireland: the young person pays the company €195 a month in Dublin and gives the older homeowner 10 hours a week of their time. ([source](https://thejournal.ie/home-share-older-young-3577237-Sep2017/))
**Who does this in India now:** No home-sharing match service found in a quick search. Companion firms such as Samvedna Senior Care send visitors for a few hours, which is different.
**To start:** About ₹50,000 (verification costs, an agreement drafted by a lawyer, ads). About 6–8 weeks to the first match. Main risk: safety and trust; one bad incident can end the business, so checks must be strict.

### Robot cats and dogs for lonely elderly parents
**In one line:** Selling soft, lifelike robot pets that purr and respond to touch, as gifts for parents living alone.
**What's missing in India today:** Many older people living alone would love a pet but cannot walk a dog, clean up after it, or bear its death. Robot pets made for older people are popular in the US, but a quick search found no one selling them in India.
**How it works:**
1. You import a small batch of robot pets made for older adults: cats and dogs that purr, bark and move when stroked.
2. NRI children order on your website or WhatsApp, and you gift-wrap each pet with a printed card.
3. A delivery helper hands it over and shows the parents how to switch it on and change batteries.
4. You also sell to old-age homes and day centres for older people.

**Who pays and how much:** NRI and local children. About ₹14,999–17,999 per pet; each pet costs you about ₹10,000–12,000 including import duty and shipping.
**Proof it works abroad:** Ageless Innovation (Joy for All), US: had delivered 500,000 companion robot pets to older adults by November 2022. ([source](https://www.homecaremag.com/news/ageless-innovation-delivers-500000-companion-pets-seniors))
**Who does this in India now:** No Indian seller found in a quick search.
**To start:** About ₹1,20,000 (first 8–10 pets, import duty, shipping, demo pieces for Reels). About 6–8 weeks. Main risk: customs may treat them as toys that need India's safety certificate (BIS), so ask a customs agent before you order.

### Speech writers for weddings and family milestones
**In one line:** A writer helps you prepare a funny, moving speech for a sangeet, wedding, anniversary or retirement party.
**What's missing in India today:** Speeches by siblings, parents and friends are now a big part of sangeets, receptions and 50th anniversaries, and people panic about them. Most copy lines from the internet. A quick search found no Indian brand that writes these speeches and coaches people to deliver them.
**How it works:**
1. The customer fills a short form and has a 30-minute call with you about the couple or person: stories, jokes, family names.
2. Your partner writer (a copywriter or stand-up comic) drafts a 4–6 minute speech in English, Hindi or Hinglish within 3 days.
3. Two rounds of changes, plus an optional video session with a speaking coach to practise.
4. You also sell wedding vows for the couple and toasts for parents.

**Who pays and how much:** Brothers and sisters of the bride or groom, best friends, and children planning a parent's anniversary or retirement. About ₹3,999 for a speech and about ₹6,999 with coaching; the writer gets about ₹1,500–2,500.
**Proof it works abroad:** Speechy, UK: a team of BBC-trained comedy writers who have written wedding speeches since 2015, with prices from £40. ([source](https://bridebook.com/uk/wedding-planners-toastmasters-celebrants/speechy-clifton-bristol-csBXdAqPKg))
**Who does this in India now:** No Indian speech-writing brand found in a quick search; only individual freelancers on sites like Fiverr.
**To start:** About ₹40,000 (3–4 trained writers, sample speeches, Reels, ads). About 2 weeks; the wedding season runs to February. Main risk: it is easy to copy, so build the brand fast with funny "before and after" speech Reels.

### Hosts for non-religious and mixed-culture wedding ceremonies
**In one line:** A trained host leads a personal vow ceremony for couples who want something beyond, or alongside, religious rituals.
**What's missing in India today:** More couples now marry across religions, cultures or countries, or want a ceremony about their own story instead of rituals they don't understand. The legal part is a registration at an office, so the celebration itself has no one to lead it. Abroad, this host is called a "celebrant".
**How it works:**
1. The couple books, and you match them with a host from your team (trained anchors, theatre artists or speakers).
2. The host meets the couple twice, learns their story, and writes a 20–30 minute script with vows, readings by family members, and chosen customs from both sides.
3. The host leads the ceremony at the venue, and you provide a printed vow booklet as a keepsake.
4. You sell the same service for vow renewals at 25th and 50th anniversaries.

**Who pays and how much:** Interfaith and intercultural couples, Indians marrying foreigners, and destination weddings. About ₹25,000–50,000 per ceremony; the host gets about ₹12,000–20,000.
**Proof it works abroad:** Humanists UK: its celebrants lead more than 1,050 non-religious weddings a year in England and Wales, up 266% since 2004. ([source](https://humanists.uk/2019/05/16/humanists-uk-wedding-numbers-continue-to-grow-show-new-figures/))
**Who does this in India now:** One found: Ceremonies with Jack ("The Wedding Guy", Bengaluru), listed on WeddingWire India at ₹20,000 per event.
**To start:** About ₹60,000 (training 3 hosts, a sample ceremony video, listings on wedding websites, ads). About 4–6 weeks. Main risk: families may object to "no rituals", so offer a mix where a priest does the key rites and your host does the rest.

### Rent a real Christmas tree, delivered and collected
**In one line:** A real potted Christmas tree arrives decorated in December and is picked up in January.
**What's missing in India today:** More city families now put up a Christmas tree, but plastic trees look cheap and cut trees die. Nurseries sell potted trees, but a quick search found no one who delivers and decorates them, then takes them back to look after until next year.
**How it works:**

2. Your nursery partner supplies a healthy potted araucaria, the plant most Indians use as a Christmas tree.

4. In early January you collect it and return it to the nursery. The deposit comes back if the tree is healthy, and the same tree can come back next year.

**Who pays and how much:** Families in Mumbai, Bengaluru, Kolkata, Goa, Delhi and Chennai, plus cafés and offices. About ₹3,499–6,999 per season, plus a refundable deposit of about ₹2,000.
**Proof it works abroad:** London Christmas Tree Rental and others, UK: Gardens Illustrated lists eight UK companies that rent out potted Christmas trees and take them back after the festival. ([source](https://www.gardensillustrated.com/christmas/how-to-rent-a-christmas-tree))
**Who does this in India now:** No rental service found in a quick search. Shops sell trees; for example, Trinity Christmas in Mumbai sells ready-decorated trees.
**To start:** About ₹80,000 (nursery deposits, decorations, transport, ads). About 4–6 weeks, which is tight but possible for Christmas 2026. Main risk: trees getting sick in hot, dark flats.

### Flying your parents in business class with your card points
**In one line:** An expert turns NRI children's credit-card and airline points into business-class tickets for their parents.
**What's missing in India today:** Long flights are hard on older parents, and business class can cost several lakh rupees. Many NRIs, and Indians with premium credit cards, hold lots of points but don't know how to move them to airlines and find seats that can be bought with points.
**How it works:**
1. The customer fills a form with travel dates, cities and the points they hold.
2. Your partner, a points expert from Indian points communities, finds seats that can be booked with points and the best way to move the points.
3. The customer moves the points and books, or the expert books with their permission.
4. You charge a fixed fee per passenger and make Reels like "Flew my parents business class to Toronto for almost no cash".

**Who pays and how much:** NRIs flying parents over or home, and Indians with premium cards. About ₹4,000–8,000 per passenger.
**Proof it works abroad:** point.me, US: raised a $15 million funding round in September 2024, co-led by Citi Ventures, for its points search and full-service points booking. ([source](https://www.citi.com/ventures/perspectives/pressrelease/investing-in-point-me.html))
**Who does this in India now:** Three found: LiveFromALounge's points-booking service, Book1A and SaveSage.
**To start:** About ₹60,000 (booking form, Reels, ads). About 2–3 weeks. Main risk: you depend on one expert's skill, and banks often change their points rules.

### Checked drivers to take children to classes and activities
**In one line:** Checked, trained drivers take children to tuition, sports and hobby classes while parents track every ride.
**What's missing in India today:** Working parents juggle office hours with children's evening classes across town. School vans only go to school, and parents don't trust ordinary cab drivers with a 9-year-old on their own.
**How it works:**
1. Parents book fixed weekly rides on WhatsApp or a simple form.
2. You partner with a cab company or a few owner-drivers, and do police verification, background checks and child-safety training for every driver.
3. A woman attendant rides along with younger children, and parents get live location plus pick-up and drop-off photos.
4. You sell monthly packages through housing societies and activity academies.

**Who pays and how much:** Working parents in large gated societies. About ₹6,000–10,000 a month for 3 fixed rides a week.
**Proof it works abroad:** HopSkipDrive, US: its checked "CareDrivers" have completed more than 5 million rides for more than 14,000 schools. ([source](https://www.hopskipdrive.com/))
**Who does this in India now:** No Indian app found. Informal school vans and private drivers are the substitute. The US model is HopSkipDrive ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/01/26/hopskipdrive-the-ridesharing-startup-for-kids-grabs-10-2m-in-series-a-funding)).
**To start:** About ₹1,00,000 (verification, training, insurance, ads). About 4–6 weeks. Main risk: any incident with a child is very serious, so strict checks, attendants and live tracking are a must.

### Fair sharing of parents' jewellery and keepsakes among siblings
**In one line:** A neutral helper lists a parent's belongings so siblings, including those abroad, can share them fairly by turns.
**What's missing in India today:** When parents move to a smaller home or pass away, brothers and sisters, often living in different countries, must divide jewellery, sarees, furniture and photos. It causes hurt feelings and long fights, and families rarely have a neutral person to run the process.
**How it works:**
1. The family books, and your partner visits the home to photograph and list every item, with a trusted valuer for gold.
2. You put the list, with photos, in a shared online sheet.
3. Siblings choose items by taking turns, or by "spending" an equal number of points, on a video call that you run, and you record every choice.
4. You arrange packing and shipping of each sibling's share. Parents can lead this while they are alive.

**Who pays and how much:** Siblings, especially NRIs. About ₹15,000–40,000 depending on the size of the home.
**Proof it works abroad:** FairSplit, US: an online service that lists a home's belongings with photos and lets family members divide them by taking turns or bidding; it quotes an estate expert saying most families expect no conflict and most of them end up in one. ([source](https://www.fairsplit.com/))
**Who does this in India now:** No friendly item-sharing service found. Lawyers and family mediators handle disputes after they start ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/supplements/law/mediation-to-resolve-inheritance-1503327356.html)).
**To start:** About ₹50,000 (valuer tie-up, templates, ads). About 4–6 weeks. Main risk: property and inheritance disputes, so handle only movable items and never give legal advice.

### Letters from Santa posted to children
**In one line:** Children receive a personalised letter from Santa in the post, mentioning their name, friends and good deeds.
**What's missing in India today:** Many city families now celebrate Christmas with their children, but the Santa magic is mostly a quick photo at a mall. Abroad, personalised Santa letters that arrive by post are a big Christmas business.
**How it works:**
1. Parents fill an online form with the child's name, age, city, one good deed and one wish.
2. Your designer prints a letter on special paper with a "North Pole" stamp, a "Nice List" certificate and a small gift such as a sticker sheet.
3. You post or courier it to arrive between 10 and 24 December.
4. Extra option: a personalised video message from Santa, recorded by an actor partner.

**Who pays and how much:** Parents of children aged 3–9 in the metros, Goa, Kerala and the North-East. About ₹499–1,499 per pack.
**Proof it works abroad:** Package From Santa, US: a family-owned company running for 20 years that sells Santa letter packs from $9.95 to $89.95 and says it has served millions of families. ([source](https://www.packagefromsanta.com/))
**Who does this in India now:** No Indian service found; overseas sellers on Etsy are the substitute.
**To start:** About ₹40,000 (design, paper, first 300 packs, ads). About 2–3 weeks, just in time for Christmas 2026. Main risk: it sells only in December, so plan a second product, such as birthday letters "from the Tooth Fairy".
