# Home, travel, hobbies and lifestyle

> Note on checks: for 15 of these ideas I ran a web search for Indian businesses doing the same thing. The session's web-search limit ran out before I could search for the last 7 ideas (private gardens, fishing trips, instrument rental, used sports gear, Santa video calls, bicycle repair van, learn-to-ride camps). Their "Who does this in India now" line says so. Check them before relying on them. All prices in ₹ marked "about" are my own estimates.

### Live Christmas trees you rent and give back

**In one line:** A real, living Christmas tree in a pot is delivered in December and collected in January.

**What's missing in India today:** Families, cafés and offices that celebrate Christmas either buy a plastic tree or cut branches that dry out in days. No one we found rents a living tree that goes back to a nursery after the season.

**How it works:**
1. A customer picks a tree size (about 3, 5 or 7 feet) and a delivery date on your Instagram or website.
2. You book potted pine-type plants from a large nursery partner and plan the delivery routes.
3. The nursery's staff deliver and set up the tree, then collect it in the first week of January and look after it until next year.
4. You also rent lights and ornaments, and message the same customers again next November.

**Who pays and how much:** Families, cafés, offices, schools and small hotels. About ₹2,500 for a small tree and about ₹8,000 for a tall one, for about 4 weeks, with delivery and pickup.

**Proof it works abroad:** Rent A Living Christmas Tree, USA (California): delivers a potted Christmas tree to your door and picks it up after the holidays ([Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/san-francisco/shopping/rent-a-living-christmas-tree))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search.

**To start:** About ₹60,000–₹1,00,000 (nursery advance for about 50 trees, plus Reels ads). About 2–3 weeks to the first paying customer, as bookings begin in late November. Main risk: it is one season a year, and some trees may die in dark flats.

### Hot tubs delivered for winter house parties

**In one line:** A warm outdoor hot tub delivered, filled and taken away for a weekend party.

**What's missing in India today:** North Indian winters are cold, and people hosting farmhouse or rooftop parties want something special. Today the only way to get a hot tub is to book a villa that already has one.

**How it works:**
1. A customer books a tub for a weekend at a farmhouse, rooftop or bungalow garden.
2. You buy 2–3 blow-up (inflatable) hot tubs; a partner swimming-pool technician delivers, fills and heats one the day before.
3. After the party he drains, cleans and packs it.
4. You also rent towels, robes, fairy lights and floating drink trays.

**Who pays and how much:** People hosting winter birthdays, bachelorette parties, wedding after-parties, and farmhouse owners who rent to guests. About ₹8,000–₹15,000 for a weekend.

**Proof it works abroad:** Party Hire Stuff, UK: rents a 5-seater hot tub for £199 from Thursday to Monday; the Add to Event site lists 30+ UK hot-tub hire suppliers ([Add to Event](https://www.addtoevent.co.uk/near-me/hot-tub-hire))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search. Only villas on Airbnb that already have a hot tub came up.

**To start:** About ₹1,50,000 for 2 tubs, pumps, covers and ads. About 3–4 weeks to the first paying customer (Delhi NCR winter). Main risk: the water takes about a day to heat and needs power; hygiene; it only sells in winter.

### A monthly printed family newspaper for grandparents

**In one line:** The family sends photos during the month, and grandparents get them as a printed newspaper by post.

**What's missing in India today:** Many grandparents struggle with smartphones, so they miss the photos shared in family WhatsApp groups. Nothing in India turns those photos into something printed they can hold and show their friends.

**How it works:**
1. A son, daughter or grandchild signs up and invites the family to send photos to one WhatsApp number or a simple upload form.
2. Through the month, everyone sends photos with a line of news.
3. At month end, a freelance designer drops them into a ready 8–12 page template, and a print partner prints it and posts it.
4. You sell yearly plans and special birthday or Diwali editions.

**Who pays and how much:** Adult children, including NRIs, for their parents. About ₹499–₹699 a month, or about ₹4,999 a year.

**Proof it works abroad:** Famileo, France: over 260,000 families use it, at £5.99 a month including postage ([Honest Mum](https://honestmum.com/famileo-a-personalised-family-newspaper-and-app/))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search.

**To start:** About ₹40,000 (templates, sample prints, ads). About 3 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: design work grows with every customer, and some families may stop after a few months.

### Paid visits to beautiful private gardens

**In one line:** Owners of lovely private gardens open them a few days a year, and visitors buy tickets.

**What's missing in India today:** Cities have beautiful private gardens, farmhouses and old bungalows that the public never sees. Garden lovers and families have few calm, green places to visit on winter weekends.

**How it works:**
1. You sign up garden owners (farmhouses, bungalows, estates near the city) to open 2–4 days a season.
2. Visitors book timed tickets online; a partner café runs a tea stall and a gardener gives a short walk.
3. You handle tickets, crowd limits, parking and a person at the gate.
4. You split the ticket money with the owner, or give their share to a charity they choose.

**Who pays and how much:** Garden lovers, families, retired people and photographers. About ₹300–₹600 a ticket.

**Proof it works abroad:** National Garden Scheme, UK: opens over 3,300 private gardens to paying visitors and has given over £77.8 million to nursing charities since 1927 ([NGS](https://ngs.org.uk/about-us/))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found. The UK's National Garden Scheme opens about 3,700 private gardens a year ([Country & Town House](https://www.countryandtownhouse.com/culture/the-national-open-garden-scheme-what-is-it)).

**To start:** About ₹30,000–₹50,000. About 4–6 weeks to the first paying visitors (winter is garden season). Main risk: owners worry about privacy and damage.

### Big suitcases on rent for one trip

**In one line:** Rent a large, good suitcase for one trip; it is delivered home and collected after.

**What's missing in India today:** Many families need a huge suitcase once a year (a foreign holiday, a wedding, a student moving abroad), then it sits under the bed. Shops only sell suitcases; we found luggage storage services, but no one renting suitcases.

**How it works:**
1. A customer picks a size and dates on your website or WhatsApp.
2. You buy a stock of strong suitcases in bulk from a luggage wholesaler.
3. A delivery partner drops the suitcase home before the trip and collects it after; a cleaning partner wipes and checks it.
4. You also sell luggage scales, covers and packing cubes, and offer "keep it" at a discount.

**Who pays and how much:** Families going abroad, students and wedding travellers. About ₹800–₹1,500 for up to 2 weeks, plus a refundable deposit.

**Proof it works abroad:** R&Y Rental, Japan: has more than 7,000 suitcases for rent and ships over 20,000 a month ([Japan Post case study](https://www.post.japanpost.jp/bizpost/column/logistics12/rental-business/))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search. Lugsto and SafeStorage store bags; they do not rent suitcases.

**To start:** About ₹1,20,000 for about 30–40 suitcases plus ads. About 2–3 weeks to the first paying customer (December holidays). Main risk: airline damage and slow payback.

### Send your bags ahead so you travel hands-free

**In one line:** Your suitcases are picked up at home and delivered to your hotel, wedding venue or airport.

**What's missing in India today:** Families going to weddings or on holiday drag heavy bags through stations, airports and cabs. In Japan, sending bags ahead is normal; in India very few people know it is possible.

**How it works:**
1. A customer books a pickup online: from where, to where, how many bags.
2. You give the job to a courier partner (other city) or a local van partner (same city).
3. The bags are sealed, tracked and delivered before the guest arrives.
4. You sell group deals to wedding planners and resorts for all their guests.

**Who pays and how much:** Wedding guests, holiday families and older travellers. About ₹600–₹1,200 a bag within a city; more between cities.

**Proof it works abroad:** Yamato Transport, Japan: airport-to-city bag delivery costs about ¥1,700 a bag and usually arrives the same day by 5pm ([Japlanning](https://www.japlanning.com/blog/japlanning-tips-shipping-your-luggage-in-japan))

**Who does this in India now:** CarterX (Bengaluru; moves bags to and from airports) and Baggage Free (Mumbai; ships bags ahead).

**To start:** About ₹30,000 (website, partner deals, ads). About 2–3 weeks to the first paying customer (wedding season). Main risk: lost or late bags; partners letting you down.

### Fruit-picking day trips from the city

**In one line:** A bus trip to a farm where families pick strawberries, grapes or mangoes and take them home.

**What's missing in India today:** City children rarely see where fruit grows. A few farms allow picking, but families must find them, drive for hours and hope they are open.

**How it works:**
1. You partner with fruit farms that allow picking for a fixed fee (for example, strawberries near Mahabaleshwar for Pune and Mumbai; crops differ by city).
2. Families book seats online, and a bus partner picks them up early in the morning.
3. The farmer's team guides the picking, and a local caterer serves lunch.
4. Each family takes home a basket (about 1 kg included) and can pay for more.

**Who pays and how much:** Families with children, school groups and office teams. About ₹2,000–₹3,000 a person, including bus, picking and lunch.

**Proof it works abroad:** Pick-your-own farms, UK and USA (many small farms, not one company): pick-your-own strawberries started in the UK in 1961, and U-pick farms run in all 50 US states ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pick_your_own))

**Who does this in India now:** Some Mahabaleshwar farms (Laxmi Strawberry Farm, Green Valley Farm, Dhanashree Garden) let visitors pick, and Nine Tours sells a Pune day trip with optional strawberry picking. No city brand running regular picking trips was found.

**To start:** About ₹40,000–₹60,000 (bus advance, farm fees, ads). About 3–4 weeks to the first paying customer (strawberry season starts in winter). Main risk: weather and crop timing; long drives.

### Overnight museum sleepovers for kids

**In one line:** Children spend a night inside a museum or science centre with torch-lit tours, a show and breakfast.

**What's missing in India today:** Indian museums close in the evening, and children's visits are short school trips. Parents looking for a special weekend treat have no "night at the museum" option.

**How it works:**
1. You sign up a private museum, science centre or planetarium that is empty at night and wants extra income.
2. With a partner events team you plan the night: torch tour, a science or history show, a craft, a film, sleeping mats and breakfast.
3. Parents book tickets online for a child plus one adult; the museum's staff and hired security run the night.
4. You fill places through Reels, schools and parents' WhatsApp groups, and share ticket money with the museum.

**Who pays and how much:** Parents of 7–12-year-olds. About ₹3,000–₹4,500 for a child and parent together.

**Proof it works abroad:** Natural History Museum, London, UK: its "Dino Snores" sleepovers for children aged 7 to 11 cost from £87 a person ([NHM](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/events/dino-snores-for-kids.html))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search.

**To start:** About ₹50,000–₹80,000 (mats, food partner, insurance, ads). About 4–6 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: getting a museum to say yes; child safety.

### Fishing day trips with local boat owners

**In one line:** Book a half-day fishing trip with a local boat owner, with rods, bait and guidance included.

**What's missing in India today:** Fishing videos are popular, but beginners don't know where to go, whom to trust or what to bring. Local fishermen and lake boat owners have no easy way to take bookings online.

**How it works:**
1. You sign up boat owners and experienced anglers on the coast or at lakes near the city where fishing is allowed.
2. Customers book a trip online; you take payment and send directions, timing and a kit list.
3. The boat owner runs the trip and brings rods and bait.
4. You build a group: a monthly "catch and release" outing and a beginner kit to buy.

**Who pays and how much:** Hobbyists, groups of friends, parents with children, and office groups. About ₹2,000–₹4,000 a person for a half-day.

**Proof it works abroad:** FishingBooker, USA and Serbia: lists 62,909 fishing trips in 128 countries and has had over 1,000,000 trips completed, without outside investors ([FishingBooker](https://fishingbooker.com/about))

**Who does this in India now:** A full search was not possible because the search limit ran out. FishingBooker itself lists only 9 trips in India, in the Andamans and the Himalayas, and none near the big cities ([FishingBooker India](https://fishingbooker.com/destinations/country/in)).

**To start:** About ₹30,000. About 3–4 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: fishing rules differ by place; safety on boats.

### Rent a guitar or keyboard, and the rent counts toward buying it

**In one line:** Start lessons on a rented instrument; if you keep it, all the rent you paid counts toward the price.

**What's missing in India today:** Parents and adults hesitate to spend about ₹10,000–₹40,000 on an instrument for a hobby that may not last. Most music shops only sell.

**How it works:**
1. A customer picks an instrument (guitar, keyboard, ukulele or violin) and rents it monthly.
2. A partner music shop supplies the instruments and repairs them.
3. A delivery partner drops it home, and you connect the customer with a partner teacher for online or home lessons.
4. After 6–12 months the customer can return it or buy it, with all rent paid counted toward the price.

**Who pays and how much:** Parents of beginners and adult learners. About ₹500–₹1,500 a month.

**Proof it works abroad:** Music & Arts, USA: counts 100% of rental payments toward buying the instrument and serves 300,000+ parents through about 250 locations ([Music & Arts](https://www.musicarts.com/rentals))

**Who does this in India now:** 2: Rentock (Mumbai) rents instruments online ([Gust](https://gust.com/companies/rentock)), and local shops such as OMR Musical (Chennai) rent and sell. No rent-to-own plan found.

**To start:** About ₹1,00,000, or much less if the music shop owns the stock and you split the money. About 3–4 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: damaged instruments; people quitting after two months.

### Video calls from Santa for your child

**In one line:** Your child gets a personal video or live video call from Santa, who knows their name and wishes.

**What's missing in India today:** Christmas is a big event for children in Indian cities and schools, but Santa only appears at mall events. Parents have no way to give their child a personal "Santa knows me" moment.

**How it works:**
1. Parents fill a form: the child's name, age, friends, something they did well, and a wish.

3. A freelance editor adds music and the child's photos.
4. You sell class packages to schools and play schools.

**Who pays and how much:** Parents of 3–9-year-olds. About ₹499 for a recorded video and about ₹1,499 for a live call.

**Proof it works abroad:** Portable North Pole (home country not checked): has made personalised Santa videos and calls since 2008, with over 354 million video views ([Portable North Pole](https://www.portablenorthpole.com/))

**Who does this in India now:** No Indian live-Santa service found. Global apps like Portable North Pole are the substitute ([PNP](https://www.portablenorthpole.com/santa-village/blog/how-to-call-Santa-Claus)).

**To start:** About ₹25,000 (costume, simple set, actor fees, ads). About 2 weeks to the first paying customer (December). Main risk: the season is only about 4–5 weeks; the actor must be good.

### Kids' bicycles on a monthly plan that grow with your child

**In one line:** Rent your child a bicycle by the month, and swap it for a bigger one when they grow.

**What's missing in India today:** Children outgrow a cycle every year or two, so parents keep buying new ones while the old ones rust in the parking. Only one company does this, and only in Bengaluru.

**How it works:**
1. A parent picks a cycle size online and pays a monthly fee plus a small deposit.
2. You buy a small stock of good cycles at a bulk price through a local cycle-shop partner.
3. The cycle shop delivers, services the cycle every few months, and swaps it for the next size when asked.
4. Returned cycles are cleaned, fixed by the shop and rented out again.

**Who pays and how much:** Parents of 3–12-year-olds in housing societies. About ₹300–₹600 a month, plus a refundable deposit of about ₹1,500.

**Proof it works abroad:** The Bike Club, UK: had a fleet of over 20,000 kids' bikes in January 2021, from £5 a month ([BikeBiz](https://www.bikebiz.com/the-bike-club-on-offering-an-affordable-and-sustainable-way-to-get-families-cycling/))

**Who does this in India now:** 1: Gro Club in Bengaluru rents bicycles to kids and adults with doorstep delivery and repairs ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/gro_club)).

**To start:** About ₹1,50,000 for about 25 cycles plus ads. About 3–4 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: money is tied up in cycles and comes back slowly; damage and theft.

**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search.

**To start:** About ₹50,000 (10 balance bikes, helmets, coach fees, ads). About 2–3 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: child injuries; getting free ground space.

### Buy, sell and trade used sports gear

**In one line:** A shop and website where you sell your old sports gear and buy good used gear cheaply.

**What's missing in India today:** Children outgrow cricket kits, skates, football boots and rackets fast, and adults give up gym or golf. Good gear sits at home because selling it piece by piece online is a hassle.

**To start:** About ₹60,000–₹1,00,000 (first stock and stall costs). About 2–3 weeks to the first sale. Main risk: stock that doesn't sell; hygiene worries about pads and helmets.

### Track days for everyday car and bike owners

**In one line:** Ordinary people drive their own car or ride their own bike on a real racetrack, with coaching.

**What's missing in India today:** Many people own fast cars and bikes but have nowhere legal to drive fast, so some race on highways. Circuits do sell track time, but few people know how to book it, what to bring or where to get coaching.

**How it works:**
1. You book places at a circuit's open track day (for example, the Buddh circuit near Delhi).
2. Customers buy a package: track time, a session with a partner racing instructor, helmet hire, photos and video, and lunch.
3. You run the group on the day: safety briefing, timings, a WhatsApp group and a lap-time leaderboard.
4. You grow it into a club with regular events and Reels from each day.

**Who pays and how much:** Car and bike fans aged about 22–45. About ₹8,000–₹15,000 for a car package and about ₹5,000–₹8,000 for a bike.

**Proof it works abroad:** Hooked On Driving, USA: runs track days for ordinary car owners in five US regions, with coaches, and welcomes first-timers ([Hooked On Driving](https://www.hookedondriving.com/))

**Who does this in India now:** Buddh International Circuit runs its own open track days (₹6,500 for a 30-minute car session in 2019, per [evo India](http://www.evoindia.com/buddh-international-circuit-time-trials/)). No separate organiser was found in a quick search.

**To start:** About ₹50,000–₹80,000 (track booking advance, coach fees, ads). About 4–6 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: crashes and insurance; few circuits outside Delhi NCR and Tamil Nadu.

### Battery health check before you buy a used electric car or scooter

**In one line:** A technician plugs a reader into a used electric vehicle and gives you a report on its battery health.

**What's missing in India today:** The battery is the most expensive part of an electric car, and a test drive cannot show how worn it is. India has no common standard for this, so buyers mostly trust the seller's word.

**How it works:**
1. A buyer (or a seller who wants to prove the car is good) books a check online.
2. You send a partner EV technician with a tool that plugs into the vehicle's diagnostic socket.
3. The tool reads how much charge the battery can still hold; you send a simple one-page report with a "fair price" note.
4. You also sell checks to small used-car dealers.

**Who pays and how much:** Used EV buyers, sellers and small dealers. About ₹1,500 for a scooter and about ₹3,000–₹4,000 for a car.

**Proof it works abroad:** Aviloo, Austria: its three-minute battery test covers 96% of EV models on the road, and in 2026 it extended a used-EV battery warranty based on this test to 10 European countries ([Bestmag](https://www.bestmag.co.uk/aviloo-expands-ev-battery-warranty-europe/))

**Who does this in India now:** ev.care (active in Delhi) and VahanBazaar's AI Vahan Inspection offer doorstep battery checks ([ev.care](https://ev.care/blog/certified-pre-owned-ev-india)). Spinny and Cars24 check only the cars they sell.

**To start:** About ₹40,000–₹60,000 (a good reading tool, technician fees, listings). About 3–4 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: the tool may not read every brand; few used EVs in some cities.

### Car-boot sale markets

**In one line:** A Sunday-morning market where families sell their old things straight from their car boots.

**What's missing in India today:** Homes are full of good unused things (books, toys, kitchenware, gadgets), but selling online means endless chats, bargaining and no-shows. India has flea markets for traders, but we found no regular market where ordinary families sell from their own cars.

**How it works:**

4. You fill the market through Reels and society WhatsApp groups, and sell banners to local brands.

**Who pays and how much:** Families clearing out their homes (about ₹500–₹1,000 a car spot), food stalls (about ₹1,500–₹3,000) and local sponsors.

**Proof it works abroad:** Global Village, Dubai, UAE: ran a weekly car-boot sale where sellers paid Dh150 for a spot and buyers entered free ([Gulf News, 2018](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/uae%2Fglobal-village-hosts-largest-car-boot-sale-every-friday-1.2171666))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search.

**To start:** About ₹40,000–₹70,000 for the first market (ground rent, permissions, signs, ads). About 3–4 weeks to the first paying sellers. Main risk: getting a cheap ground and police or venue permission; too few sellers at the first event.

### Checked second-hand premium cycles and e-cycles with a warranty

**In one line:** Buy a used premium bicycle or e-cycle that has been checked, fixed and comes with a 6-month warranty.

**What's missing in India today:** Many people buy costly cycles and e-cycles, ride them for a few months, then leave them unused. Buyers on OLX fear hidden faults and stolen cycles, so good used cycles don't sell easily.

**How it works:**

4. Later you offer trade-ins: bring your old cycle and pay the difference for a better one.

**Who pays and how much:** Office commuters, fitness riders and students. About ₹8,000–₹40,000 a cycle; you keep about ₹2,000–₹6,000 on each.

**Proof it works abroad:** Upway, France and USA: has refurbished and sold over 100,000 e-bikes, usually about 45% below the new price, with a one-year warranty ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/11/07/upway-raises-60m-to-refurbish-1-million-e-bikes-by-2030/))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found for bicycles in a quick search. CredR sells checked second-hand motorbikes and scooters, not cycles.

**To start:** About ₹1,00,000–₹1,50,000 for the first 10–15 cycles, or close to nothing if you start by listing cycles for owners. About 3–4 weeks to the first sale. Main risk: being sold stolen cycles; slow sales.

### A bicycle repair van that comes to your home or office

**What's missing in India today:** Cycle shops are often far away and you need a car to carry the cycle there, so cycles sit with flat tyres and rusty chains. E-cycle owners find it especially hard to get someone who understands their cycle.

**How it works:**

3. You sell yearly service plans and spare parts.
4. You set up "cycle service days" with housing societies and offices.

**Who pays and how much:** Cycle owners, parents of young riders, office cyclists and e-cycle owners. About ₹499–₹999 a service.

**Proof it works abroad:** Velofix, Canada and USA: mobile bike shops with 130+ certified technicians and over 220,000 services done ([Velofix](https://www.velofix.com/))

**Who does this in India now:** 1: Fix My Cycle (Chennai) books doorstep bicycle repairs in about 20 cities ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/01/chennai-startup-fix-my-cycle-doorstep-service)).

**To start:** About ₹50,000 (tools, parts, ads; the mechanic uses his own vehicle at first). About 2–3 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: low money per job; finding a reliable mechanic.

### Washable cups for events instead of plastic

**In one line:** You bring washable cups to events, collect them afterwards and get them washed, so no plastic cups are used.

**What's missing in India today:** Concerts, marathons, festivals and society events throw away thousands of plastic and paper cups. Organisers want to look green but don't have time to manage reusable cups themselves.

**How it works:**
1. An event organiser books cups for a date; you bring branded hard cups in crates.
2. Guests pay a small deposit with their first drink and get it back when they return the cup, or keep the cup.
3. A commercial dishwashing partner collects the dirty crates and washes them for the next event.
4. You sell the event's sponsor a logo printed on the cups.

**Who pays and how much:** Event organisers, breweries, marathon organisers and companies holding events. About ₹8–₹15 a cup per use, plus guest deposits.

**Proof it works abroad:** RECUP, Germany: its deposit cups are used at around 20,000 places across Germany, with a €1 deposit a cup ([RECUP](https://recup.de/en/))

**Who does this in India now:** Recube, Mumbai (brand Cupable): supplies reusable cups to 500+ food outlets and has been used at 150+ events ([Eco-Business](https://www.eco-business.com/ms/news/reuse-and-refill-a-business-model-turning-the-tide-on-single-use-plastic/)).

**To start:** About ₹1,00,000 (about 3,000 cups, crates, ads). About 4 weeks to the first paying customer (December events). Main risk: cups going missing; organisers choosing cheap plastic instead.

### Rent a whole fruit tree and get all its fruit

**In one line:** Book one apple, orange or litchi tree on a farm for a season and get all its fruit delivered.

**What's missing in India today:** People want fresh, naturally ripened fruit and to know who grew it. One startup does this for mangoes, but other fruits have no such option.

**How it works:**
1. You sign up a few orchards (for example, apples in Himachal or oranges near Nagpur), and the farmer tags and photographs each tree.
2. Customers choose a tree online, pay for the season, and get photo and video updates from the farm.
3. At harvest the farmer picks and packs the fruit, and a courier partner delivers it in 2–3 boxes.
4. You sell "a tree in your name" as a birthday or Diwali gift.

**Who pays and how much:** City families and gift buyers. About ₹6,000–₹12,000 a tree for a season (about 20–40 kg of fruit).

**Proof it works abroad:** Nudo Adopt, Italy: people adopt an olive tree from small farmers and receive its olive oil by post every few months; it marked its 20th anniversary in 2025 ([Nudo Adopt](https://www.nudoadopt.com/))

**Who does this in India now:** Rent a Tree (Kochi) rents Alphonso mango trees from about ₹10,300 a season and had about 160 customers in March 2026 ([FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/9816016/why-buy-mangoes-when-you-can-rent-the-whole-tree-in-india/)). It only does mangoes.

**To start:** About ₹50,000 (farm visits, tagging, photos, website, ads). About 4–6 weeks to the first paying customer (sell before each harvest). Main risk: bad weather cuts the crop; fruit damaged in transit.
