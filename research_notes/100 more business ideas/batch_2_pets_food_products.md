# Pets, food, drinks and products

### Real-grass toilet patches for dogs in flats
**In one line:** A tray of real, living grass, delivered every week or two, for flat dogs to pee on.
**What's missing in India today:** Dogs in tall buildings often need to pee at night, in heavy rain or while their owners are at work, so owners use plastic pee pads or fake-grass mats that start to smell. Nobody in India delivers fresh, real grass on a regular schedule and takes the old patch away.
**How it works:** 1. The owner picks a patch size and how often they want a fresh one (every week or every two weeks). 2. A partner plant nursery grows grass in light coconut-fibre trays, with no soil mess. 3. You deliver each fresh patch by local courier and take the used one back for composting. 4. You find customers through Reels, vets, pet shops and building WhatsApp groups.
**Who pays and how much:** Owners of puppies, small dogs and old dogs in flats. About ₹699–999 per patch, or about ₹2,500 a month for a fresh patch every week.
**Proof it works abroad:** Fresh Patch, USA: sells real-grass patches (a standard patch costs $34.95) on a plan that sends a fresh one every 1–4 weeks. ([source](https://freshpatch.com/blogs/news/how-the-fresh-patch-grass-subscription-works))
**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search. Only imported fake-grass mats are sold online (for example on Ubuy).
**To start:** About ₹1,00,000 (trial batches with a nursery, trays, delivery, first ads, reserve). About 4–5 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: grass browning or smelling in hot weather, and delivery costs eating the profit.

### Borrow-a-dog club for people who can't own one
**In one line:** A club that matches busy dog owners with nearby dog lovers who walk their dogs for free.
**What's missing in India today:** Many young people in rented flats or hostels love dogs but are not allowed to keep one, while many owners want extra walks and company for their dog without paying a walker every day. Paid dog-walking services exist, but no trusted club links these two groups.
**How it works:** 1. Owners and dog lovers in one neighbourhood join through a simple form, with ID checks. 2. You match them and set up a first "meet the dog" visit in a park. 3. The borrower walks the dog or spends an afternoon with it; no money passes between them. 4. Both sides pay you a yearly fee, and you run monthly park meet-ups to keep the club lively.
**Who pays and how much:** Dog owners pay about ₹1,999 a year; borrowers pay about ₹499 a year. Later, pet brands can pay to sponsor meet-ups.
**Proof it works abroad:** BorrowMyDoggy, UK: dog owners pay £49 a year and borrowers £10 a year to join and find each other. ([source](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/borrowmydoggy/id860721134))
**Who does this in India now:** No dog-borrowing club found. Sploot and others sell paid dog walking, which is a different service.
**To start:** About ₹60,000 (ID checks, a simple website, meet-up costs, ads). About 3–4 weeks. Main risk: safety and trust, such as a dog getting lost or biting someone, so you need strict checks and clear rules.

### First-aid classes for pet owners
**In one line:** Half-day classes, taught by vets, where pet owners learn what to do if their pet chokes, bleeds or overheats.
**What's missing in India today:** When a dog gets heatstroke in May, swallows a bone or panics during Diwali crackers, most owners freeze and lose precious minutes. First-aid courses for humans are common, but classes for ordinary pet owners are very rare.
**How it works:** 1. You book a vet or vet nurse as the trainer, and a pet-friendly café or clinic hall as the venue. 2. Owners book a 3–4 hour Saturday class and practise on dog-shaped training dummies. 3. Everyone goes home with a printed emergency card and a pet first-aid kit that you buy from suppliers. 4. You also sell private classes to housing societies, pet hostels, groomers and dog-walking firms for their staff.
**Who pays and how much:** Pet owners pay about ₹1,499–2,499 a seat. Pet businesses pay about ₹15,000–25,000 to train a batch of staff.
**Proof it works abroad:** IndyVet, USA: runs a 4-hour animal first-aid and CPR class for pet owners at $50 a person, with a manual and hands-on practice. ([source](https://www.wfyi.org/news/articles/indyvet-offers-pet-first-aid-cpr-training-oct-4))
**Who does this in India now:** VOSD in Bengaluru ran a one-day workshop at ₹2,999 for 15 people, aimed mainly at rescuers and street-dog feeders. No regular classes for pet owners were found in other cities.
**To start:** About ₹70,000 (two training dummies, kits, vet fees for the first classes, venue, ads). About 3–4 weeks. Main risk: finding vets who teach well and can give up their Saturdays.

### Dog handlers for weddings
**In one line:** A trained handler brings your dog to your wedding, keeps it calm and safe, then takes it home.
**What's missing in India today:** More couples want their dog in the pre-wedding shoot or the varmala photos, but weddings are loud, with dhol, crackers and crowds, and every relative is busy. No one offers a booked service that looks after the dog for the day.
**How it works:** 1. The couple books a package: a visit to meet the dog beforehand, pick-up, 4–6 hours of care at the venue, and drop-off. 2. Your partner, a trained pet sitter or dog trainer, keeps the dog in a quiet room, walks it and brings it out for photos and the big moment. 3. You find couples through wedding planners, photographers and Reels of dogs at weddings. 4. Add-ons include a second dog, outfits from a partner tailor, or overnight care during a three-day wedding.
**Who pays and how much:** Couples with dogs, mostly in big cities. About ₹7,500–15,000 per wedding day.
**Proof it works abroad:** All Paws Weddings, UK: full-day dog care at weddings starts at £379, with £100 for each extra dog. ([source](https://bridebook.com/uk/wedding-planners-toastmasters-celebrants/all-paws-weddings-smallfield-surrey-cs0Y1ybJ6Y))
**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search. Some wedding planners arrange pet grooming or transport as a side favour.
**To start:** About ₹50,000 (paying and briefing 2–3 handlers, travel crates, first-aid kits, ads). About 3–5 weeks, and the wedding season is already on. Main risk: a frightened dog getting hurt or running off at a crowded venue.

### Comfort products for old dogs and cats
**In one line:** An online shop and home set-up service for ageing pets: soft beds, ramps, non-slip mats, lifting belts.
**What's missing in India today:** Pets now live longer, and many dogs over 10 struggle with stairs, slippery floors and weak back legs. Owners find odd items on Amazon, but no shop explains what an old pet needs or comes to set up the home.
**How it works:** 1. The owner fills a short form or does a video call about the pet's age and problems. 2. You suggest a kit (bed, ramp, mats, harness) sourced from Indian makers of foam beds, mats and harnesses. 3. A delivery partner drops it off, and for bigger homes a helper lays the mats and fixes the ramp. 4. Vets refer old pets to you, and you post "golden oldies" Reels.
**Who pays and how much:** Owners of dogs and cats aged 8 or more. Kits about ₹2,499–5,999; single items about ₹499–2,999.
**Proof it works abroad:** Walkin' Pets, USA: has sold wheelchairs, harnesses and support products for old and disabled pets since 2001. ([source](https://www.walkinpets.com/about-us))
**Who does this in India now:** Earlier research for this project found no shop focused on old pets, only charity homes for old dogs (CUPA and VOSD). Big pet stores such as HUFT and Supertails may sell some items (not checked).
**To start:** About ₹1,20,000 (first stock, photos, ads, reserve). About 4–5 weeks. Main risk: unsold stock, and a smaller audience than puppy owners.

### Frozen croissants and breads you bake at home
**In one line:** A box of frozen croissants and breads from good bakeries that you bake fresh at home in minutes.
**What's missing in India today:** Good croissants and sourdough are sold in only a few cafés, and they go stale within a day. Many homes now have an oven (OTG) or air fryer, but you cannot buy café-quality frozen pastries to bake at home.
**How it works:** 1. You partner with 1–2 good bakeries that already make frozen dough for cafés and hotels. 2. Customers order a box (for example 12 croissants, 6 chocolate croissants and 2 sourdough loaves) on WhatsApp or a simple website. 3. You deliver it frozen within the city in insulated boxes with ice packs. 4. Customers keep it in the freezer and bake a few on a lazy Sunday morning.
**Who pays and how much:** Families and couples who love café breakfasts. About ₹1,499–2,499 a box, with a monthly plan.
**Proof it works abroad:** Wildgrain, USA: a bake-from-frozen box of breads and pastries made by about 50 small bakeries, with over 100,000 subscribers and over $31 million a year in revenue. ([source](https://thehustle.co/news/the-viral-online-bakery-making-serious-dough))
**Who does this in India now:** No home box found. Frozen croissants are sold only in bulk to cafés and hotels, for example by Delta Nutrition and Hot Cakes on IndiaMART.
**To start:** About ₹1,20,000 (a deep freezer, insulated boxes, ice packs, the ₹100 FSSAI food registration, first ads, reserve). About 4–6 weeks. Main risk: keeping everything frozen on the way in a hot city.

### A box of snacks from a different Indian state every month
**In one line:** Each month, a box of famous snacks and sweets from one Indian state, with their stories.
**What's missing in India today:** Someone in Bengaluru rarely tastes real Bikaneri bhujia, Goan bebinca or Assamese pitha unless a friend brings some back from a trip. Snacks are sold everywhere, but nobody puts together a fun "travel India by snack" box each month.
**How it works:** 1. Each month you choose one state and buy 8–10 packed snacks from well-known local makers who already hold food licences. 2. You pack them with a printed card telling the story of each snack. 3. Customers sign up monthly, or gift 3 or 6 months, and you ship by courier. 4. You sell with "unboxing Nagaland" style Reels to snack lovers, students and gift buyers, and later to NRI families.
**Who pays and how much:** Snack lovers and people buying gifts. About ₹999–1,499 a box.
**Proof it works abroad:** Universal Yums, USA: sends snacks from a different country each month, and has over 100,000 active subscribers and over 10 million boxes sold. ([source](https://woocommerce.com/posts/universal-yums-subscriptions-woocommerce/))
**Who does this in India now:** Only older general snack boxes were found: The Namkeen Club (Mumbai, 2018) and TopMunch (2017). Whether they still run is unknown.
**To start:** About ₹1,00,000 (the first 100 boxes, packaging, printing, ads). About 3–4 weeks. Main risk: short shelf life and breakage, and people cancelling after 2–3 months.

### Freeze-dried sweets
**In one line:** Sweets and gummies freeze-dried so they puff up, turn crunchy and look amazing on camera.
**What's missing in India today:** Freeze-dried sweets are a huge social-media trend abroad, but Indian shops do not sell them. Indian factories already freeze-dry fruit, so making sweets this way is possible here.
**How it works:** 1. You find an Indian factory that already freeze-dries fruit and ask it to do small batches of gummies, marshmallows and a few Indian sweets. 2. The factory packs them in sealed pouches and jars under your brand name. 3. You sell online and at flea markets, college fests and party shops, with Reels of the loud crunch. 4. Later you add gift jars for birthdays and return gifts.
**Who pays and how much:** Teenagers, young adults, and parents buying party favours. About ₹249–399 a pouch.
**Proof it works abroad:** Sow Good, USA: a freeze-dried sweets brand that made over 7.5 million units in one quarter of 2024 and was heading for 10,000+ stores. ([source](https://www.snackandbakery.com/articles/112264-sow-good-on-taking-a-tiktok-trend-to-market))
**Who does this in India now:** No freeze-dried sweets brand found. Freeze Fusion Foods (Mumbai) and IndiaMART sellers offer freeze-dried fruit and meals, not sweets.
**To start:** About ₹1,50,000 (trial batches, packaging, food registration, ads, reserve). About 5–7 weeks. Main risk: finding a factory that will do small batches, and damp air making the sweets go soft.

### Fortune cookies with your own messages
**In one line:** Crisp fortune cookies with your own messages inside, for weddings, proposals, birthdays and parties.
**What's missing in India today:** People want small personal touches at weddings and parties, yet guests get the same chocolates and dry-fruit boxes everywhere. Fortune cookies with custom messages are almost impossible to order in India.
**How it works:** 1. A partner bakery bakes the cookies and you supply the printed message slips. 2. Customers order 50 or more online and type up to five messages, such as "Will you marry me?", a wedding hashtag or a baby announcement. 3. You wrap each cookie, pack them in branded boxes and deliver. 4. You sell through wedding planners and gift shops, with Reels of proposal reveals.
**Who pays and how much:** Couples, party hosts and companies planning events. About ₹40–70 a cookie, with a minimum order of 50.
**Proof it works abroad:** Fancy Fortune Cookies, USA: has made gourmet fortune cookies since at least 1988, and custom-message cookies start at $0.70 each for 50. ([source](https://fancyfortunecookies.com/Personalized-Vanilla-Fortune-Cookies-p/van1020.htm))
**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search. Some home bakers may make them on request.
**To start:** About ₹60,000 (trial batches with a bakery, a printer for slips, packaging, ads). About 3–4 weeks. Main risk: cookies breaking on the way, and the bakery's quality changing from batch to batch.

### Clear party ice delivered to homes
**In one line:** Big, crystal-clear ice cubes and balls, like those in fancy bars, delivered for house parties.
**What's missing in India today:** Ice made at home is cloudy, melts fast and is never enough for a party. Good bars now use clear ice, but people hosting at home still buy small bags of cloudy cubes.
**How it works:** 1. You partner with a maker of clear ice, which freezes big blocks and cuts them into large cubes and balls. 2. Hosts order on WhatsApp a day ahead: party packs of clear cubes, balls and crushed ice, with a cool box on loan. 3. You deliver on Friday and Saturday evenings in insulated boxes. 4. Later you add ice stamped with a name or logo for birthdays and weddings.
**Who pays and how much:** People who host house parties or have a home bar, and small event hosts. About ₹499–1,499 a party pack.
**Proof it works abroad:** Clear-ice makers that sell big cubes to bars and home drinkers are common in the USA, such as Hundredweight Ice (not checked).
**Who does this in India now:** JUSTice sells clear ice to bars, weddings and events. Fresh Ice sells small packs of clear cubes on Swiggy Instamart at ₹75 a kilo. No one was found selling party packs of big cubes and balls to homes.
**To start:** About ₹80,000 (a deep freezer, cool boxes, packaging, ads). About 3 weeks. Main risk: melting on the way and thin profit, because the ice maker controls your cost.

### Café passport: one drink at 15 independent cafés
**In one line:** A printed passport, bought once, that includes one drink at each of 10–20 independent cafés.
**What's missing in India today:** Every big city has hundreds of new small cafés, but people keep going back to the same few. Small cafés cannot afford big ads to bring in new customers.
**How it works:** 1. You sign up 10–20 independent cafés in one city, and each agrees to serve one drink from a short list to every passport holder. 2. You print a good-looking passport with one page per café. 3. People buy it online or at partner cafés, and each café stamps its page when it serves the drink. 4. Cafés are usually not paid, because they gain new regular customers; you keep the sales and print a new edition every 6–12 months.
**Who pays and how much:** Coffee lovers, students, people new to the city, and gift buyers. About ₹999–1,499 a passport, which holds about ₹3,000 or more of drinks.
**Proof it works abroad:** Indie Coffee Passport, Canada and USA: it started in Toronto and spread to Ottawa, Montréal and Vermont; in Burlington, $12 bought one drink at each of 10 cafés. ([source](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/food-drink/indie-coffee-passport-offers-burlington-ground-tour-2380259/))
**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search. Cafe BLR lists Bengaluru cafés online but does not include drinks.
**To start:** About ₹50,000 (a freelance designer, printing 1,000 passports, ads). About 4–5 weeks. Main risk: cafés dropping out halfway, or complaining that passport holders come only once.

### Roasted barley tea, the caffeine-free drink of Japan and Korea
**In one line:** Tea bags of roasted barley: nutty, caffeine-free, drunk hot in winter and iced in summer.
**What's missing in India today:** Many people want less tea and coffee at night or during pregnancy but find herbal teas strange. Japanese and Korean homes drink roasted barley tea every day, but in India it is only sold as a costly import.
**How it works:** 1. A tea-packing factory roasts Indian barley (jau) and packs it in tea bags under your brand. 2. You sell it now as a warm winter drink, and in summer as a jug of iced tea for the fridge. 3. You sell online, in Korean and Japanese grocery shops, in gyms and cafés, and through K-drama and Japan-travel fan pages. 4. You hand out free tasting cups at markets, because taste is what sells it.
**Who pays and how much:** Health-minded young adults, K-culture fans, pregnant women, and parents who want a sugar-free drink for children. About ₹299–399 for 30 tea bags.
**Proof it works abroad:** Barley tea makers, Japan: production of barley tea drinks hit a record 1.5 million kilolitres in 2025, more than seven times the 2005 level. ([source](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h02855/))
**Who does this in India now:** No Indian brand found. Japanese Ito En barley tea is sold as an import (₹1,400 for 54 bags on Wow Nippon).
**To start:** About ₹1,00,000 (trial roasts, 2,000–3,000 packs, food registration, ads). About 5–6 weeks. Main risk: most Indians have never tasted it, so you must give away a lot of samples.

### Good decaf coffee for evenings and pregnancy
**In one line:** A coffee brand that sells only good decaf, roasted fresh by an Indian partner roaster.
**What's missing in India today:** Many people love coffee but cannot drink it after 4 pm, while pregnant, or because of anxiety or heart problems. Indian coffee brands focus on strong coffee, and good decaf (coffee with the caffeine removed) is hard to find.
**How it works:** 1. You partner with an Indian roaster that can buy decaf beans and roast them under your brand. 2. You sell ground coffee, single-cup drip bags and cold-brew bags. 3. You reach pregnant women, new mothers, night-shift workers and "evening coffee" lovers through Reels and pregnancy pages on Instagram. 4. You offer a monthly plan so customers never run out.
**Who pays and how much:** Coffee lovers who need to cut caffeine. About ₹599–799 for 250 grams.
**Proof it works abroad:** Decadent Decaf, UK: a coffee company that has sold only decaf since 2014, with bags from £8.99. ([source](https://www.decadentdecaf.com/))
**Who does this in India now:** Earlier research for this project found no decaf-only brand. Lavazza launched one decaf in India in 2018, and some big roasters may sell a decaf option (not checked).
**To start:** About ₹1,20,000 (first roasted batches, packaging, food registration, ads). About 5–7 weeks. Main risk: decaf beans are mostly imported, so your supply and price depend on the roaster.

### Birthday cards handwritten and posted for you
**In one line:** Tell us your family's birthdays once; we post each person a handwritten card on time, every year.
**What's missing in India today:** People forget birthdays or send a quick WhatsApp message instead. A real handwritten card feels special, but buying, writing and posting one on time is too much effort for most people.
**How it works:** 1. The customer fills a form with names, addresses, dates and a few lines about each person. 2. Ten days before each birthday, you send a draft message on WhatsApp for them to approve or change. 3. Freelance writers with beautiful handwriting write the card, and you post it by Speed Post or courier. 4. Later you sell the same service to doctors, accountants and shops that want to send cards to their clients.
**Who pays and how much:** Busy professionals, NRIs writing to family in India, and small businesses. About ₹199–299 a card, or about ₹1,999 a year for 10 cards.
**Proof it works abroad:** Handwrytten, USA: uses robots holding real pens to write and post cards, and one business customer says it sends over 15,000 cards a month. ([source](https://www.handwrytten.com/))
**Who does this in India now:** The Indian Handwritten Letter Co. (Bengaluru, older article) and Yours Letterly write letters on request, and freelancers on Fiverr do too. No yearly birthday-card plan was found.
**To start:** About ₹40,000 (cards, postage, writers' pay, a simple form, ads). About 2–3 weeks. Main risk: people not renewing after the first year, and postal delays.

### Exam-week care boxes for students in hostels
**In one line:** Parents order a box of snacks, treats and a note that reaches their child's hostel in exam week.
**What's missing in India today:** Lakhs of students live in hostels and paying-guest rooms in Kota, Delhi, Pune, Manipal and other study towns. Parents want to show love during exams but can only send money, or home-made laddoos that arrive late or broken.
**How it works:** 1. You put together three boxes (healthy snacks, comfort treats, and an "all-nighter" box with coffee and instant noodles) from packed brands. 2. Parents order online and add a personal note, which you print on a card. 3. You deliver to the hostel gate just before exams, timed to each college's exam dates. 4. College student clubs share your link with parents and earn a small cut, the same way US colleges run it.
**Who pays and how much:** Parents of college and coaching-centre students. About ₹799–1,999 a box.
**Proof it works abroad:** University of Wisconsin–Madison alumni association, USA: sold parents exam-week care packages at $15.45–30.95 and expected 400–500 orders in one term (older article). ([source](https://news.wisc.edu/waa-helps-parents-give-students-an-exam-week-boost))
**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search.
**To start:** About ₹70,000 (stock for the first 100 boxes, packaging, ads aimed at parents). About 3–4 weeks, and end-of-term exams fall in November–December. Main risk: hostels refusing deliveries, and exam dates differing from college to college.

### Film cameras on every table at weddings
**In one line:** Disposable film cameras on wedding tables; we develop the film and send the couple every guest photo.
**What's missing in India today:** Couples pay a lot for photographers but miss the fun, candid shots that guests take. Film cameras are back in fashion with young people, but no one in India offers a ready service that includes developing and scanning.
**How it works:** 1. The couple books a pack of 10, 20 or 40 cameras with stickers showing their names and date. 2. You deliver the cameras with small table signs explaining how to use them. 3. After the wedding you collect the cameras, a partner film lab develops and scans the photos, and you send a private online album within 2–3 weeks. 4. Add-ons include a printed photo book and the same service for birthdays and office parties.
**Who pays and how much:** Young couples, especially for mehendi, sangeet and haldi. About ₹2,500–3,000 a camera, including developing and scans.
**Proof it works abroad:** Joy, USA: a wedding-planning website that sells packs of 3, 5 or 10 disposable cameras with professional developing, digital scans and free return postage included. ([source](https://weddingshop.withjoy.com/products/bulk-disposable-cameras-for-weddings-and-events-with-film-development))
**Who does this in India now:** No one found offering it as a full service. WedMeGood suggests couples do it themselves.
**To start:** About ₹90,000 (40 cameras, a lab tie-up, stickers, ads). About 3–4 weeks. Main risk: cameras and film are costly imports that can run short, and some indoor shots come out dark.

### Detective mystery boxes by post
**In one line:** A box of letters, photos, maps and clues arrives; you and friends solve the case at home.
**What's missing in India today:** Young couples and groups of friends want fun, screen-free things to do at home. Escape rooms cost a lot and are far away, and there is no Indian "solve the case at home" game set in Indian places.
**How it works:** 1. A freelance writer creates a mystery set in India, such as a missing painting in an old Kolkata mansion. 2. A printer makes the clue letters, photos, maps and small props, and you pack them into boxes. 3. Customers buy one box, or a "season" of 3–6 boxes posted monthly, and check their final answer on WhatsApp. 4. You sell it as a gift for couples and birthdays, and to offices for team fun.

**Proof it works abroad:** Hunt A Killer, USA: each mystery is a "season" of six monthly boxes of clues, at about $25 a month. ([source](https://inverse.com/article/58123-hunt-a-killer-subscription-box))
**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search. The only close product is a children's detective board game (Bloomingo's "Mumbai Files").
**To start:** About ₹1,20,000 (writer, freelance designer, printing 200 boxes, ads). About 6–8 weeks. Main risk: the first story must be truly gripping, and each new story costs money to write.

### Real Christmas trees on rent
**In one line:** A living Christmas tree in a pot, delivered in December and taken back after New Year.
**What's missing in India today:** Families, offices, cafés and hotels want a real tree for Christmas, not a plastic one, and not a tree that dies by January. Nurseries sell potted trees, but almost no one rents them out and takes them back.
**How it works:** 1. You partner with a nursery that grows the pine-like potted "Christmas tree" plant in sizes from 2 to 8 feet. 2. Customers choose a size online, and you deliver in mid-December, with lights and decorations if they want. 3. In the first week of January you collect the tree, and the nursery looks after it until next year. 4. Offices, cafés and malls give the biggest orders, and the same trees can be rented again every year.
**Who pays and how much:** Families (especially Christian and expat homes), offices, cafés and hotels. About ₹1,999–7,999 a tree for the season.
**Proof it works abroad:** Rent A Living Christmas Tree, USA (California): delivers a potted tree to your door and picks it up after the holidays. ([source](https://www.timeout.com/san-francisco/shopping/rent-a-living-christmas-tree))
**Who does this in India now:** Greenlands Nursery in Mumbai hires out plants and sells Christmas trees; other nurseries only sell them. No dedicated tree-rental service was found.
**To start:** About ₹80,000 (rent per tree paid to the nursery, pots, a hired delivery van, ads). About 3–6 weeks, and you must sell by early December. Main risk: it earns money for only one month a year, so it works best alongside plant rentals for Diwali and weddings.

### New Year lucky bags
**In one line:** Sealed mystery bags from good brands, sold around 1 January, always worth more than the price.
**What's missing in India today:** Indian brands end the year with leftover stock and run dull sales. Shoppers love surprises and unboxing videos, but India has no New Year "lucky bag" tradition.
**How it works:** 1. You sign up 15–30 small Indian brands (skincare, stationery, snacks, clothes, home decor) to sell you leftover stock and samples cheaply. 2. You fill sealed bags at three price levels, each holding goods worth about 2–3 times the price. 3. You take online pre-bookings in December and run a 1–3 day pop-up stall at a mall or café around 1 January. 4. Brands gain new customers, you keep the profit, and buyers' unboxing Reels advertise the next year's bags.
**Who pays and how much:** Shoppers aged 18–35 and gift buyers. Bags at about ₹999, ₹1,999 and ₹4,999.
**Proof it works abroad:** Disney Store Japan, Japan: one of many shops selling New Year lucky bags (fukubukuro); its ¥3,500 bag holds goods worth about ¥10,500. ([source](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/japan-life/japanese-lucky-bag-fukubukuro-shopping-guide-2026/))
**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search.
**To start:** About ₹1,50,000 (stock bought from brands, bags, pop-up stall rent, ads). About 8–10 weeks, with pre-bookings from December. Main risk: buyers feeling cheated if bags look like junk, so choose brands carefully.

### Stylish earplugs for weddings, concerts and noisy nights
**In one line:** Reusable, good-looking earplugs that turn down loud music without muffling it, for weddings, concerts and sleep.
**What's missing in India today:** Indian weddings, baraats, clubs and concerts are extremely loud, and city nights are noisy. People put up with ringing ears because only cheap foam plugs are easy to find in shops.
**How it works:** 1. A factory that already makes silicone earplugs with a small sound filter makes them in your colours and case. 2. You sell online and at concerts, wedding shopping fairs and gyms. 3. You sell bulk packs to wedding planners (for the family on baraat day), DJs, music schools and noisy workplaces. 4. Your Reels show sound-meter readings at a baraat or a concert.
**Who pays and how much:** Concert-goers, wedding guests, light sleepers and musicians. About ₹999–1,999 a pair.
**Proof it works abroad:** Loop Earplugs, Belgium: founded in 2016, it has sold over 20 million pairs in more than 150 countries. ([source](https://www.musicconnection.com/?p=153135))

### Stick-on body warmers for North Indian winters
**In one line:** Thin stick-on pads that stay warm for hours inside your clothes, for cold North Indian winters.
**What's missing in India today:** North Indian winters are cold, and many homes, offices, buses and wedding lawns have no heating. In Japan people stick small warming pads inside their clothes, but in India they are hard to find and sold only without a brand.
**How it works:** 1. You buy ready-made warming pads (they heat up when the pack is opened and air reaches them) from a factory that already makes them. 2. You pack them under your brand for the body, hands and feet. 3. You sell online, through chemists, to bike-taxi and delivery fleets, to planners of outdoor night weddings, and to trekking companies. 4. Your Reels show winter hacks for Delhi mornings, night weddings and Himalayan trips.
**Who pays and how much:** Office-goers, two-wheeler riders, outdoor wedding guests and trekkers. About ₹299 for 10 pads.
**Proof it works abroad:** Kobayashi Pharmaceutical, Japan: sells Kiribai Kairo warming pads for hands, body and feet, some lasting up to 24 hours. ([source](https://int.japanesetaste.com/collections/kiribai))
**Who does this in India now:** Only unbranded sellers were found on IndiaMART, for example Warmzy hand warmers at ₹350 for two pairs.
**To start:** About ₹1,20,000 (first stock, packaging, ads, reserve). About 5–7 weeks, and the cold peaks in December–January. Main risk: a short season, import delays, and skin burns if instructions are not clear.

### Laundry detergent sheets for hostels, PGs and travel
**In one line:** Thin sheets of detergent: tear one off, drop it in the wash. No spills, no heavy packs.
**What's missing in India today:** Students in hostels and paying-guest rooms, people living alone, and travellers carry heavy, leaky detergent packs. Light, mess-free detergent sheets are popular abroad, but in India only a couple of brands sell them, one mainly for baby clothes.
**How it works:** 1. A factory that already makes detergent sheets packs them under your brand (a travel pack of 10 and a monthly pack of 30). 2. You sell online and through hostel shops, college fests, paying-guest owners and travel stores. 3. You offer a "hostel laundry kit" with sheets, a mesh laundry bag and a stain pen. 4. Your Reels compare a neat pack of sheets with a backpack soaked in spilt liquid.
**Who pays and how much:** Students, young people living alone and frequent travellers. About ₹199 for 30 sheets.
**Proof it works abroad:** Earth Breeze, USA: calls itself the top laundry-sheet brand and says over 3 million people use it. ([source](https://www.earthbreeze.com/pages/about-us))
**Who does this in India now:** SuperBottoms (sheets for baby clothes and cloth nappies) and Lavella (a seed-funded detergent-sheet startup).
**To start:** About ₹1,20,000 (samples, first 3,000 packs, packaging, ads). About 6–8 weeks. Main risk: cheap washing powder is hard to beat on price, so the selling point must be convenience.
