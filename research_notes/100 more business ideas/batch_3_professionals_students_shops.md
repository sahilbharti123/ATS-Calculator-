# Professionals, students and small businesses

### Someone who quits your job for you

**In one line:** A service that resigns from your job for you and handles the awkward calls and final paperwork.

**What's missing in India today:** Many young employees dread telling a pushy boss they are leaving, and then spend weeks chasing their last salary, relieving letter (the letter that proves you left properly) and PF (provident fund) transfer. We found no Indian service that does this for you.

**How it works:**
1. The customer fills a short form: company, last working day they want, and what they are still owed.
2. You send a polite, firm resignation email on their behalf and track every step on a simple checklist.
3. A partner HR professional (a freelancer who has handled many exits) talks to the company's HR team, agrees the notice period and chases the last salary, relieving letter and PF transfer.
4. If there is a legal fight, you pass the customer to a partner lawyer, who charges separately.

**Who pays and how much:** Salaried people aged about 22–35 leaving a stressful job. About ₹2,999 for the resignation and handover talks, or about ₹5,999 including chasing the last salary, relieving letter and PF.

**Proof it works abroad:** Momuri, Japan: charges ¥22,000 for full-time workers and says it has helped 20,000 people quit their jobs. ([source](https://thepost.ph/money/japan-resignation-agencies/))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search.

**To start:** About ₹40,000 (simple website, form, email templates checked by a lawyer, first Reels ads). About 3–4 weeks to the first paying customer. Main risk: some companies will refuse to talk to an outsider, and the customer still has to serve the notice period.

### Job-offer negotiation help, paid only if your offer goes up

**In one line:** Experts coach you through negotiating a job offer and take a small cut only if your pay rises.

**What's missing in India today:** Many people accept the first number a company offers, even when recruiters expect some bargaining. Free tips exist online, but no one sits with you, writes your replies and gets paid only when it works.

**How it works:**
1. The customer shares the offer letter and any other offers they have.
2. You find out the usual pay range for that role with your partner and send the customer a simple plan.
3. A partner (an experienced recruiter or HR manager working freelance) writes the emails and call scripts and coaches the customer before each call.
4. If the final offer is higher, the customer pays a share of the increase.

**Who pays and how much:** Mid-level people in tech, finance and sales who are switching jobs. About 8% of the first year's increase (for example, about ₹16,000 on a ₹2 lakh raise), and nothing if the offer does not go up.

**Proof it works abroad:** Rora, USA: charges a percentage of the extra pay it negotiates ("No increase, no fee") and says it has helped more than 1,200 people since 2017. ([source](https://www.teamrora.com/))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search; only free articles and tips.

**To start:** About ₹50,000 (website, simple contract, LinkedIn and Reels ads). About 3–5 weeks. Main risk: customers may hide the final number, so ask for the signed offer letter before you start.

### Someone who bargains your new car price for you

**In one line:** An expert gets quotes from several dealers and cuts hidden charges before you buy a new car.

**What's missing in India today:** First-time car buyers face "handling charges", forced accessories and costly dealer insurance, and they don't know what discount is possible. Few people have the time or nerve to bargain with five dealers.

**How it works:**
1. The customer tells you the car model, version and city.
2. A partner (a former car salesman working freelance) asks several dealers for written quotes by phone and WhatsApp.
3. The partner pushes for the best price, removes unfair add-ons and checks the car before delivery.
4. The customer pays a fixed fee, and you refund it if you can't beat their own best quote.

**Who pays and how much:** Young professionals buying their first or second car. About ₹4,999 per car, or about ₹7,999 with a check of the car before delivery.

**Proof it works abroad:** Delivrd, USA: charges $1,000 per deal, has five negotiators and brings in about $200,000 a month. ([source](https://thehustle.co/news/this-guy-fights-car-dealers-for-1k))

**Who does this in India now:** RowthAutos (a website that bargained with car dealers for buyers in north India; whether it still runs was not checked).

**To start:** About ₹40,000 (partner fees for the first cases, Reels, ads). About 2–3 weeks; year-end discounts in December help. Main risk: some dealers refuse to deal with a middleman, so the customer may need to do the final visit.

### Speeches written for you: weddings, farewells and anniversaries

**In one line:** A writer turns your stories into a short, warm and funny speech, and can coach you to deliver it.

**What's missing in India today:** Sangeets, office farewells and parents' anniversaries often need someone to stand up and speak, and most people freeze or read a dull list. We found no Indian company that writes personal speeches; people use AI tools or random freelancers.

**How it works:**
1. The customer fills a form about the person, a few stories and the event, then does a 20-minute call.
2. A partner writer (a stand-up comic or copywriter) writes a 3–5 minute speech in English, Hindi or a mix.
3. The customer gets two rounds of changes.
4. If they want, a partner coach runs a 30-minute video practice session.

**Who pays and how much:** Siblings and friends at weddings, managers giving farewell speeches, and children speaking at parents' anniversaries. About ₹2,999 for a speech, or about ₹4,999 with coaching.

**Proof it works abroad:** All Speeches Great & Small, UK and USA: charges a flat $485 for a personal wedding speech and shows 421 verified reviews. ([source](https://www.allspeechesgreatandsmall.com/))

**Who does this in India now:** No Indian company found; some Indian freelancers sell speeches on Fiverr, and AI speech tools exist.

**To start:** About ₹25,000 (sample speeches, Reels, tie-ups with wedding planners). About 2 weeks; the December wedding season is a good start. Main risk: AI tools make people think a speech should be free.

### Daytime workspace in empty bars and restaurants

**In one line:** Bars and restaurants that are empty until evening become paid daytime workspaces for remote workers.

**What's missing in India today:** Many bars and dinner restaurants sit empty until 6 pm, while remote workers crowd cafés and feel bad holding a table all day. Only a few Indian players do this, and mostly with lounges and cafés.

**How it works:**
1. You sign up 4–6 bars and restaurants in one area that are empty from 10 am to 5 pm.
2. Members buy a day pass or monthly pass and book a seat on WhatsApp.
3. The venue gives Wi-Fi, charging points and unlimited tea or coffee, and earns extra from food orders.
4. You share the pass money with the venue and run a small weekly event to build a community.

**Who pays and how much:** Remote and hybrid workers, freelancers and students preparing for exams. About ₹249 a day or about ₹2,999 a month.

**Proof it works abroad:** KettleSpace, USA: turned underused restaurants and hotels in New York into daytime workspaces for just under $100 a month, with 6 spaces at the time. ([source](https://allwork.space/2018/02/classic-lightbulb-moment-brings-low-cost-coworking-to-nyc/))

**Who does this in India now:** myHQ (has turned 60+ lounges and cafés into workspaces) and SOHO Space (Mumbai; whether it still runs was not checked). Friyey tried workspaces in bars but shut down in 2023.

**To start:** About ₹40,000 (venue tie-ups, signs, Wi-Fi boosters, Reels). About 2–3 weeks. Main risk: myHQ could move into your area, so lock in the best venues first.

### Small monthly support groups for new managers

**In one line:** About eight new managers from different companies meet monthly with a guide to solve real team problems.

**What's missing in India today:** People promoted to manager often get little training, and they can't talk openly with colleagues about a difficult team member or boss. There is no simple, private group where new managers can learn from each other.

**How it works:**
1. People apply, and you group about 8 managers at a similar level from different companies (never two from the same company).
2. A partner guide (an experienced HR leader or coach) runs a 2-hour session each month, in person or online.
3. In each session, members bring a real problem and the group helps solve it; a WhatsApp group keeps going between sessions.
4. You sell seats to individuals, and to companies as a learning benefit for their staff.

**Who pays and how much:** First-time managers with about 3–8 years of work experience, or their companies. About ₹2,500 a month per person, or about ₹25,000 a year.

**Proof it works abroad:** Chief, USA: a paid network of more than 10,000 senior women leaders who meet in small "Core Groups". ([source](https://chief.com/))

**Who does this in India now:** No paid peer group found. Only formal courses exist, such as IIM Indore's programme for first-time managers ([IIM Indore](https://iimidr.ac.in/mdp-calendar/leadership-development-program-for-first-time-managers/)).

**To start:** About ₹30,000 (guide fees for the first groups, venue, LinkedIn and Reels ads). About 4 weeks. Main risk: members drop out after 2–3 months, so sell 6-month seats.

### Bicycle on a monthly plan, repairs included

**In one line:** Pay a monthly fee for your own bicycle; if it breaks, a mechanic fixes or swaps it free.

**What's missing in India today:** Many office-goers and students live a short ride from the metro, office or college, but buying a good cycle and paying for repairs puts them off. There is no simple "your own cycle, fixed for free" monthly plan.

**How it works:**
1. The customer picks a cycle and a monthly plan, and pays a deposit.
2. A partner cycle shop supplies the cycles (or you buy a small first batch) and delivers them with a lock.
3. The partner shop's mechanic fixes any problem within 48 hours, or swaps the cycle.
4. You sell to people living near metro stations, tech parks and colleges.

**Who pays and how much:** Office-goers, students and metro commuters. About ₹699–999 a month, plus a refundable deposit of about ₹2,000.

**Proof it works abroad:** Swapfiets, Netherlands: its monthly fee includes the bicycle, maintenance and repairs, and it had more than 220,000 bicycles in 64 cities in 2021. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swapfiets))

**Who does this in India now:** 1: Gro Club in Bengaluru rents bicycles to kids and adults with doorstep delivery and repairs ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/gro_club)).

**To start:** About ₹1,50,000 if you buy 20 cycles yourself, or about ₹40,000 if a partner shop owns them. About 4 weeks. Main risk: theft and damage, so take deposits and hand out strong locks.

### Interview suits on rent for placement season

**In one line:** Rent a fitted formal suit, shirt and tie for campus placements or job interviews, then return it.

**What's missing in India today:** Final-year students need a suit for a few interview days and may never wear it again, yet buying one costs thousands. We found no one in India who rents plain interview suits to students.

**How it works:**
1. Students book a size and a pick-up slot on WhatsApp or through their college placement team.
2. You keep a stock of plain navy, grey and black suits, shirts and ties at a partner tailor's shop near the colleges.
3. The partner tailor does quick fittings and small changes, and a partner dry cleaner cleans each set after it comes back.
4. Students keep the suit for 3–4 days and return it; you hold a refundable deposit until then.

**Who pays and how much:** Final-year engineering and MBA students and freshers going to interviews. About ₹799 for 3 days (suit, shirt and tie), plus a refundable deposit of about ₹2,000.

**Proof it works abroad:** Yofuku no Aoyama, Japan: this suit retailer rents plain "recruit suits" to job-hunting students at a lower cost than buying one. ([source](https://soranews24.com/2020/11/07/japanese-clothing-chain-offers-an-answer-to-job-hunting-suit-conundrum/amp/))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found in a quick search.

**To start:** About ₹1,50,000 (about 60 suit sets from wholesale markets, cleaning, a few college tie-ups). About 3–4 weeks, in time for winter placement drives. Main risk: suits sit unused outside placement season.

### Ready room boxes for hostel and PG move-in

**In one line:** Parents order one box with bedding, bucket, lamp and other basics, delivered to the hostel or PG room.

**What's missing in India today:** Every year, families make long lists and run around markets to buy the same bedsheets, pillow, bucket, extension board and hangers. Colleges publish these lists, but no one sells everything as one ready box delivered to the room.

**How it works:**
1. Parents pick a box (basic, comfort or premium) and give the hostel or PG address.
2. You buy in bulk from wholesale suppliers, and a partner packer packs the boxes.
3. A delivery partner drops the box at the hostel gate or PG on move-in day.
4. You tie up with student groups and PG owners, who get a small cut for each box sold.

**Who pays and how much:** Parents of first-year students, and young people joining a PG for a new job. About ₹3,499–6,999 per box.

**Proof it works abroad:** OCM (On Campus Marketing), USA: sells bedding, bath sets and care packages for students and serves over 900 campuses in North America. ([source](https://www.starrez.com/news/starrez-and-ocm-partnership-collaboration))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found for Indian hostels. AmberStudent and Discounto sell similar kits, but only for students going abroad.

**To start:** About ₹1,20,000 (stock for about 40 boxes, packing, college tie-ups). About 4–6 weeks; January semester starts and new PG joiners come first, and July–August is the big season. Main risk: unsold stock outside admission season.

### Student discount card for shops near campus

**In one line:** Students buy a yearly card that gets them discounts at cafés, salons and shops near their college.

**What's missing in India today:** Student discounts in India are mostly for big online brands. Small cafés, photocopy shops, gyms and salons near a college want students on slow weekdays, but have no simple way to reach them.

**How it works:**
1. You sign up 20–30 shops around one college area, and each agrees to a 10–20% student discount.
2. Students buy the card (on WhatsApp or as a plastic card) and show it with their college ID.
3. Shops pay you a small monthly fee to be listed and shown in your Reels.
4. You repeat this around the next group of colleges.

**Who pays and how much:** Students pay about ₹199 a year; shops pay about ₹1,000 a month.

**Proof it works abroad:** TOTUM, UK: sells a student discount card for £14.99 a year. ([source](https://www.cambridgesu.co.uk/aboutus/services/totum/))

**Who does this in India now:** RCMH Card, UNiDAYS and Student Beans offer student discounts, but mostly on national and online brands; no local campus card found.

**To start:** About ₹40,000 (cards, a freelance designer, Reels). About 3–4 weeks. Main risk: shops stop giving the discount, so check in with each shop every month.

### Flatmate speed-meeting nights

**In one line:** An evening at a bar or café where people with a spare room meet people who need one.

**What's missing in India today:** Young people find flatmates through crowded Facebook groups and apps, then meet strangers one by one. There is no friendly event where you can meet 30 possible flatmates in one evening.

**How it works:**
1. People sign up and say whether they have a room or need one, with their budget and area.
2. You host a weekly evening at a partner café or bar, with coloured name badges ("room to share" or "need a room").
3. The partner venue earns from food and drinks; you keep the ticket money.
4. After the event, you send a WhatsApp list of who came, so people can follow up.

**Who pays and how much:** Working people and students aged about 21–32 in cities like Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad, Gurugram or Mumbai. About ₹299 a ticket with one drink, and about ₹499 for people with a room to fill.

**Proof it works abroad:** SpareRoom, UK: by 2018 it had run SpeedFlatmating nights for almost ten years, every week in London and Manchester (free to attend, to bring people to its flat-share website). ([source](https://blog.spareroom.co.uk/our-guide-to-speedflatmating/))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found. Housr matches roommates only inside its own rental homes, and Flatchat (a flatmate app) has shut down.

**To start:** About ₹30,000 (badges, first venue deal, Reels ads). About 2–3 weeks. Main risk: too few people with rooms turn up, so recruit room-owners first.

### Someone checks your rental flat abroad before you pay

**In one line:** Before a student pays a deposit abroad, a local person visits the flat and sends a video report.

**What's missing in India today:** Indian students going to the UK, Canada, Australia or Germany often pay deposits for flats they have never seen, and rental scams are common. Booking sites list checked student halls, but no one visits a private flat you found yourself.

**Who pays and how much:** Students going abroad and their parents. About ₹3,999 per flat, or about ₹9,999 for three flats.

**Proof it works abroad:** Propzee, Australia: inspects rental homes for people who can't view them, such as students and people moving from overseas, for A$179 with a report within 8 hours. ([source](https://eliteagent.com/new-outsourcer-rental-inspections-helps-time-poor-consumers/))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found doing in-person checks. Amber, InstaDwell and Student.com list checked student housing, but they don't visit a private flat you found yourself.

**To start:** About ₹50,000 (partners in 5–6 cities found through alumni groups, website, Reels). About 3–4 weeks; the January intake is close. Main risk: finding reliable checkers in every city.

2. You measure the flat with a partner and order from partner furniture and appliance suppliers at trade prices.
3. Partners deliver and set up everything in about a week, and you take photos for the rental listing.
4. You earn a share of the pack price, plus an optional fee for finding the first tenant through a partner broker.

**Who pays and how much:** Owners of flats in cities like Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad and Gurugram who rent to professionals. About ₹1,50,000–3,00,000 per flat, of which you keep about 12–15%.

**Proof it works abroad:** InStyle Direct through Benham & Reeves, UK: sells landlord furniture packs from £3,395 + VAT to help landlords "maximise their asking rents". ([source](https://www.benhams.com/services/furnishing-packages/))

**Who does this in India now:** No one found selling packs to landlords. Flent furnishes and runs rental homes itself, and FlatFurnish rents furniture to tenants.

**To start:** About ₹50,000 (sample catalogue, supplier deals, broker tie-ups). About 4–6 weeks. Main risk: supplier delays or damage land on you, so take full payment upfront and pass on supplier warranties.

### Someone shows your flat to tenants for you

**In one line:** A trained local person shows your empty flat to would-be tenants, so you don't miss work.

**What's missing in India today:** Owners renting out a flat must take time off again and again to show it, often to people who don't turn up. Brokers will do it, but they charge a big commission.

**How it works:**
1. The owner shares the keys (or a smart-lock code), house rules and the asking rent.
2. You fix viewing slots and confirm each visitor by phone.
3. A partner "viewer" (a checked student or part-timer living nearby) opens the flat, shows it, answers questions and sends the owner notes and a short video of each visit.
4. The owner decides; you can add tenant document checks through a partner verification firm.

**Who pays and how much:** Busy flat owners. About ₹499 per viewing slot, or about ₹2,999 for up to 10 viewings.

**Proof it works abroad:** Viewber, UK: charges £30 for each viewing and pays local "Viewbers" £10–20 a viewing plus travel. ([source](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/revealed-viewber-pricing-agent-payments-work/))

**Who does this in India now:** Not checked: the web-search limit for this session ran out before this check. Search before using this idea.

**To start:** About ₹30,000 (viewer checks and training, a key-safe system, Reels). About 2–3 weeks. Main risk: trust with keys, so check every viewer and start with owners in gated societies.
