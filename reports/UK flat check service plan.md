# Check the UK flat before students pay

**Yes, you can build this business, but keep it small, careful and in England at first.** The service is simple. An Indian family pays you about **₹7,999 (about £63)**. A checked local partner in the UK city visits the flat or room with the landlord. The partner shows the student round on a **live video call** and checks the landlord's name against the official owner record. The partner also checks the key legal papers. Within **48 hours** you send a plain report that marks each test green, amber or red. The buyers are real but there are fewer each year. Indian study visas fell **16% to 82,523** in the year to June 2026. By my estimate, only about **10,000 to 24,000** Indian students a year pay for a private room before they have seen it. So plan for about **300 checks in your first year**, not thousands. The closest rival, Kinpany, charges £55 but works **only in London** and takes 2 to 5 working days. Nobody else does all of these together: covers every big UK student city, checks the owner and the papers, does a live call, reports in 48 hours, and sells in rupees through Reels and WhatsApp. After paying the partner, the fees and a referral reward, each check leaves you about **₹1,900**. That means about **8 checks a month** cover your running costs, and about **29 a month** would also pay you ₹40,000. Two legal points shape the whole plan. First, people on a **Student visa cannot be self-employed**, so your partners must hold a Graduate visa, be British or settled, or come from a network like Viewber. Second, **from 1 October 2026 you must check every freelancer's right to work in the UK**. Treat the January 2027 intake as a cheap test. Keep ₹1 lakh untouched. Push hard for the big June to August 2027 season only if your February review says go.

*A note on numbers: all money uses **£1 = ₹127** (the Wise rate on 3 October 2026) ([Wise](https://wise.com/gb/currency-converter/gbp-to-inr-rate)). Some research notes used ₹127.4 or about ₹115 to the pound. I have redone every conversion at ₹127. "(estimate)" means my own sum or guess, not a published fact.*

## A ₹7,999 "friend in the UK" who checks before the family pays

**Here is the service in plain words.** A student in India finds a private flat or a room in a shared house. They usually find it on SpareRoom, OpenRent, Facebook or a WhatsApp group. Before they pay a deposit or sign anything, they send you the advert link. You do some quick checks from India. Then a partner who lives in that UK city meets the landlord at the flat. The landlord opens the door with keys while the student and parents watch live on video. The partner walks through every room, tests the alarms and photographs the safety papers. The partner also sees the landlord's ID, so you can compare the name with the owner shown in the official land records. Within 48 hours the family gets a one-page report. Each test is marked green (fine), amber (be careful) or red (a problem). The family then decides whether to pay. Later, the same service can be sold to students from Nepal, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka, and to Indians already in the UK who are moving to a new city for a job.

**The UK police tell students to do exactly this.** Action Fraud, the UK's fraud reporting service, tells students: "do not pay any money until you, or a reliable contact, has visited the property". It adds that students arriving from overseas should get a friend or contact to check that the property exists ([Action Fraud](https://www.actionfraud.police.uk/news/students-urged-to-watch-out-for-rental-scams)). SpareRoom, the biggest UK flat-share website, says never pay a deposit without viewing the room in person ([SpareRoom](https://www.spareroom.co.uk/content/info-flatsharing/scams-to-look-out-for/)). A student sitting in Pune or Hyderabad cannot follow that advice alone. Your partner is the "reliable contact" the police are talking about.

**The money at risk is large for an Indian family.** Victims reported **£4.2 million (about ₹53 crore)** of rental fraud losses in 2023. The average victim lost **£1,720 (about ₹2.18 lakh)**. Rental fraud is one of the five most-reported frauds for people aged 11 to 29 ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/4-ways-to-spot-a-rental-scam-a206R0v7tjRF)). One London case shows the kind of trick your partner would catch. A group of letting companies posted more than **18,000 misleading SpareRoom adverts** and made **£18 million (about ₹229 crore)** between 2016 and 2021. They showed photos of one room and then handed over a worse one, which is called "bait and switch". They took deposits before people had seen the room. The victims included international students ([report on the Tower Hamlets case](https://bebeez.eu/2025/03/25/rogue-letting-agents-face-jail-for-massive-fraud/)). Your sales line to parents is therefore simple: **"₹7,999 to protect about ₹2 lakh."**

**It is just as important to be clear about what the service is not.** It is not a building survey. It is not legal advice. It is not a letting agent. You never collect or pass on rent or deposits. Your reports state facts and never call anyone a "scammer". An earlier quick idea card priced this service at ₹3,999 and suggested using Indian students in the UK as checkers. Fuller research changes both points. A lawful partner plus the official owner record costs about ₹4,800 per visit outside London. And Student visa holders are not allowed to do this kind of freelance work.

## Fewer Indian students each year, but 10,000 to 24,000 still rent unseen

**The Indian student market peaked in 2023 and is shrinking again in 2026.** Be honest with yourself about this from the start.

| Period | Indian study visas | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Year to June 2023 | about 142,700 (estimate) | The peak ([The Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/23-fall-in-indian-students-wanting-to-study-in-uk)) |
| Year to June 2024 | 110,006 | Down 23% after most students lost the right to bring family ([The Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/23-fall-in-indian-students-wanting-to-study-in-uk)) |
| Calendar year 2025 | 95,231 | India was the biggest country, with 23% of all study visas ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-december-2025/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)) |
| Year to June 2026 | **82,523** | **Down 16%.** India is now second after China ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)) |

The fall is continuing. Study visa applications from all countries were **down 22%** from January to August 2026 compared with the same months of 2025 (231,600 against 298,500) ([Home Office monthly data](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aa129a962ec7fe7bedf51e0/monthly-entry-clearance-visa-applications-data-tables-aug-2026.ods)). The January intake, your first season, is the smaller of the two intakes, and it is getting weaker. December plus January applications fell from 63,900 for the January 2025 intake to **49,500 for January 2026, down 23%** (my sums from the same Home Office tables). In a survey of universities, **66% had fewer Indian students** starting in January 2026, and master's starters were **down 31%** overall ([Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/742468)). More changes could push numbers down further. The Graduate visa, which lets students stay and work after their course, drops from 2 years to **18 months** for applications made from 1 January 2027 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/graduate-visa)). Universities in England will also pay a **£925 (about ₹1.17 lakh)** charge per international student each year from August 2028 ([ICEF Monitor](https://monitor.icef.com/2025/11/uk-confirms-international-fee-levy-of-925-per-student-starting-august-2028)). My working guess is **65,000 to 85,000** new Indian study visas in 2027 (estimate).

**Your buyer is a particular kind of student: a master's student renting a private room.** **81%** of Indian students come for a master's degree ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2025/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)). Most master's courses last one year, so a new group needs housing every year. A survey of 3,698 international students found that master's and research students mostly live in **shared private houses**, not university halls. Only **24%** found their home through a university website. **39% of Indian students** said they were worried about money, the highest of the main countries ([Unipol](https://unipol.org.uk/media/eamh54s5/the-international-student-housing-survey-briefing-paper.pdf)). That worry matters for pricing. January starters have a harder time: **29%** said finding a home was "much more difficult than expected", against 15% of September starters, because halls are mostly full by January ([Unipol](https://unipol.org.uk/media/eamh54s5/the-international-student-housing-survey-briefing-paper.pdf)).

| Step | Number per year | How I got it |
|---|---|---|
| New Indian students | about 80,000 | Home Office, year to June 2026 |
| Of these, rent a private room or flat | 32,000 to 48,000 | 40% to 60% (estimate, because most are master's students who rent shared houses) |
| Of these, pay before seeing it | **10,000 to 24,000** | 30% to 50% (estimate; the rest stay with friends first and view in person) |
| Of these, January intake | 2,000 to 6,000 | January is about 13% to 15% of the year's applications |
| You might win in year one | **100 to 700 checks** | 1% to 3% of those who pay unseen (planning guess) |

**Other new arrivals add a second, smaller pool.** Study visas rose sharply in 2025 for **Nepal (19,553, up 60%)**, **Bangladesh (10,828, up 71%)** and **Nigeria (30,204, up 59%)** ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-december-2025/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)). Nepal, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka use similar agents and the same Reels culture, so they are the easiest groups to add next. Nigeria would need separate marketing. Indian nurses are a much smaller group now. Only **4,215** Indian-trained nurses and midwives joined the UK register in 2025–26, **down 54%** ([NMC](https://www.nmc.org.uk/news/news-and-updates/sharp-drop-in-international-recruitment-slows-overall-growth-of-uk-register)). The overseas care worker route has been closed since July 2025 ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-work)). Indians already in the UK are another option. They received **64,495** Graduate visas in the year to June 2026 ([Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/immigration-system-statistics-year-ending-june-2026/why-do-people-come-to-the-uk-study)). Some of them move city for a first job, and they can pay you in pounds.

**A new law in England changed how much money is at risk.** From **1 May 2026**, a landlord in England can ask for **no more than one month's rent in advance**, and only **after** the tenancy agreement is signed ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act)). Before this, international students were often asked for **6 to 12 months' rent up front** ([Goodlord](https://blog.goodlord.co/rent-in-advance)). So each scam now takes less money. But the money that can still go is large. In London, rooms cost about £260 to £300 a week ([University Living](https://www.universityliving.com/blog/accommodation/affordable-london-accommodation-indian-students/)). A 1-week holding deposit plus a 5-week deposit is **£1,560 to £1,800 (about ₹2.0 to ₹2.3 lakh)** (estimate). Student unions also warn that landlords may now ask for a **UK guarantor** (a person or company that promises to pay if the tenant does not) ([Durham SU](https://ask.durhamsu.com/support/solutions/articles/202000105942-advance-rent-what-landlords-can-ask-for-and-what-is-changing)). That gives you a referral product, covered later.

**The honest summary: this starts as a business sized for one person, not a big company.** About 300 checks a year would mean winning 1% to 3% of the students who rent unseen. Growth beyond that has to come from more cities, more nationalities and extra products, not from the shrinking Indian student number.

**The exact gap is the combination, not any single feature.** Kinpany checks the owner, but only in London and without a live call. Moorhouse reports in 48 hours, but in one region. Viewber covers the whole UK, but serves landlords. Nobody found does all six of these together. First, cover every big UK student city, including Hatfield, Luton, Coventry, Leicester, Leeds and Newcastle. Second, work only for the tenant. I found no Indian Instagram or WhatsApp service selling paid UK flat checks with a public price. **Rivals could move into this gap, so speed and your Indian brand are your defence.** Kinpany could expand beyond London. Viewber could start selling to tenants. Amber or UniAcco could add a paid check for outside listings. ViewForYou's apparent closure is a warning sign. Find out whether it failed because too few people wanted the service or because it was badly run.

## Fourteen tests, half of them done from India before anyone travels

**The checklist is built on England's rules, because all ten universities with the most Indian students are in England.** These are BPP, Hertfordshire (Hatfield), East London, Coventry, Bedfordshire (Luton), Greenwich, De Montfort (Leicester), Anglia Ruskin, Northumbria (Newcastle) and Leeds Beckett ([UniAcco](https://uniacco.com/blog/uk-universities-with-most-indian-students)). Launch in England. Add Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland only after a lawyer has advised you.

### Before the visit: seven desk checks you do from India

These take about 30 to 45 minutes per flat (estimate). Most are free. A red result here can save the cost of a visit, so do them first.

| # | Test | How you check it | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the advert copied? | Reverse image search of the photos (Google Lens or TinEye) ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/4-ways-to-spot-a-rental-scam-a206R0v7tjRF)). Search the address on Rightmove, Zoopla, SpareRoom and OpenRent | The same photos appear under another agent or city |
| 2 | Who owns it? | Buy the **£7 (₹889) title register**, the official owner record from HM Land Registry for England and Wales ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/get-information-about-property-and-land/search-the-register)). If the landlord is a company, look it up free on [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/) | The owner's name differs and there is no written permission to let |
| 3 | Is the letting agent real? | Agents must belong to an official complaints scheme ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2013/24/section/83)). Agents who hold tenants' money must also have client money protection ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mandatory-client-money-protection)). Look up both numbers on the schemes' own websites | Not listed, or the certificate number is fake |
| 4 | Is the energy rating legal? | Search the postcode on [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/find-energy-certificate). A rented home needs an energy rating (EPC) of **E or better** ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/domestic-private-rented-property-minimum-energy-efficiency-standard-landlord-guidance)) | Rated F or G with no registered exemption |
| 5 | Does it need a shared-house licence? | An HMO is a house shared by people from different households. **5 or more people from more than one household** need a council licence ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/house-in-multiple-occupation-licence)). Search the council's public register | It needs a licence and is not on the register |
| 6 | Is the money request legal? | Ask for a written list of every payment. Compare it with England's limits (see below) | A "viewing fee", "admin fee" or "reservation fee"; more than the legal caps; rent before signing |
| 7 | Is the payment route safe? | Ask for the account name and payment method | Cash, crypto, gift cards, Western Union, an account abroad or in another name ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/4-ways-to-spot-a-rental-scam-a206R0v7tjRF)); "pay today or lose it" |

### At the visit: seven checks the partner does on the live call

**The partner visits only with the landlord or agent, at a time the landlord arranged.** If people live there now, the landlord must give them **24 hours' written notice** before a visit ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1985/70/section/11)). At the start of the call, the partner says the call is being recorded for the tenant's check and gets the landlord's "yes" on camera. The partner never films current tenants or their belongings without permission.

| # | Test | How the partner checks it | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | Can they get in? | The landlord or agent opens the door with keys, live on video | "The keys are with a friend" or "I'm abroad, I'll post the keys" |
| 9 | Is this person the owner or allowed to let it? | The landlord holds up their ID on camera. **Do not record or copy it.** Note only "name matches owner record: yes or no" | The names do not match and there is no written permission |
| 10 | Does the home match the advert, and is it safe? | Walk through every room. Look for damp and mould. Test the heating, hot water, windows and locks. Press the test button on every alarm. England requires a smoke alarm on **every floor** and a carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a fixed boiler or fire ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-alarms-explanatory-booklet-for-landlords/the-smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-alarm-england-regulations-2015-qa-booklet-for-the-private-rented-sector-landlords-and-tenants)) | A different room from the photos, mould, dead alarms, a boiler with no carbon monoxide alarm |
| 11 | Are the safety papers there? | Photograph the **gas safety record**, which must be renewed every year ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/private-renting/print)). Photograph the **electrical safety report** (EICR, an electrician's check of the wiring), which is needed at least every 5 years and must be given to new tenants before they move in ([Wandsworth Council](https://www.wandsworth.gov.uk/housing/private-housing/private-housing-landlords/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-rented-sector/)) | Missing or out of date. Write "not provided", not "illegal" |
| 12 | How many people live there? | Count the bedrooms and the people | 5 or more unrelated people and no licence; overcrowding |
| 13 | Will the deposit be protected? | Ask which of the three official schemes will hold it: DPS, mydeposits or TDS ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection)) | "We don't use schemes" |
| 14 | What is the area like? | Film the street. Check transport, phone signal, noise and bins | Things the advert hid |

A small extra signal is worth noting. In England, a real landlord must check that every adult tenant has the **right to rent**, usually with an online "share code" from their visa ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/check-tenant-right-to-rent-documents)). A "landlord" who asks for nothing except money is acting informally at best. This is a warning, not proof of a scam.

### The report within 48 hours

**Keep the report to one page, with photos attached.** List all 14 tests, each marked green, amber or red. Use facts only, for example: "The owner named on the title register is X. The person we met gave the name Y." Never write "this is a scammer". That keeps you safer from being sued for damaging someone's reputation. Add timestamped photos and a list of missing documents. End with a scope note: "a visual, non-specialist check on [date]; not a building survey; not a guarantee of the landlord's honesty". Thirty days after the family pays, send a free reminder to check that the deposit is protected. The landlord must protect it **within 30 days**, or a court can order up to **3 times the deposit** to be paid to the tenant. The tenant can check for free on the scheme's website or phone line ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection/print)). This free follow-up builds trust, and it is a natural moment to offer the move-in photo record.

### England's renting rules since 1 May 2026, in plain words

**These rules turn many old scam tricks into clear legal red flags, which makes them perfect Reel material.** The Renters' Rights Act became law on 27 October 2025, and most of its changes for tenants started on **1 May 2026** ([GOV.UK roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/renters-rights-act-2025-implementation-roadmap/implementing-the-renters-rights-act-2025-our-roadmap-for-reforming-the-private-rented-sector)).

| Rule in England | What your report checks |
|---|---|
| Rent in advance: **at most 1 month**, and only after signing and before the tenancy starts ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act)) | Any request for months of rent, or rent before signing, is red |
| **No bidding.** The landlord must publish one asking rent and cannot accept offers above it (same source) | "Offer more and it's yours" is red |
| Tenancies now roll on month by month, "no-fault" evictions have ended, and tenants can leave with **2 months' notice** ([GOV.UK roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/renters-rights-act-2025-implementation-roadmap/implementing-the-renters-rights-act-2025-our-roadmap-for-reforming-the-private-rented-sector)) | A contract that locks a student in for a fixed year is amber; ask a lawyer |
| Holding deposit: **at most 1 week's rent**, returned within 7 days in most cases. Main deposit: **at most 5 weeks' rent** when the yearly rent is under £50,000 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tenant-fees-act-2019-guidance-for-tenants)) | Anything above these caps is red |
| Most fees are banned, including admin and inventory fees. A landlord cannot force a tenant to buy a guarantor service or insurance (same source) | Any extra fee is red |
| Private student blocks (purpose-built student halls) are **exempt** from the new tenancy system ([GOV.UK roadmap](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/renters-rights-act-2025-implementation-roadmap/implementing-the-renters-rights-act-2025-our-roadmap-for-reforming-the-private-rented-sector)) | Your service is for private houses and flats, not big student blocks |

Two points are still uncertain. First, a national landlord register (the "PRS Database") starts rolling out region by region from "late 2026" (same roadmap). I could not confirm whether any area is live yet. Once it is, "Is the landlord on the database?" becomes a strong test. Second, rooms let by a landlord who lives in the same home (a "lodger" arrangement) are usually not covered by these tenancy rules, so the one-month cap may not apply to them. Treat lodger rooms case by case.

### Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have different rules

| Topic | Scotland | Wales | Northern Ireland |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biggest deposit | **2 months' rent** ([mygov.scot](https://www.mygov.scot/tenancy-deposits-tenants)) | Not re-checked; believed similar to England | **1 month's rent** ([nidirect](https://nidirect.gov.uk/articles/tenancy-deposit-scheme-information-tenants)) |
| Deposit protection deadline | 30 working days, with a Scottish scheme ([SpareRoom](https://m.spareroom.co.uk/content/info-flatsharing/tenancy-deposit-scheme-scotland)) | 30 days, same schemes as England ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection)) | 28 days, with details given within 35 days ([nidirect](https://nidirect.gov.uk/articles/tenancy-deposit-scheme-information-tenants)) |

**Most first businesses die because they run out of cash or because too few people want what they sell.** Global studies of failed start-ups find "no market need" in about 35% to 42% of failures and "ran out of cash" in about 29% to 38% ([LaunchRock, summarising CB Insights](https://www.launchrock.com/library/expert-advice/why-do-startups-fail)). Many Indian start-ups that shut in 2025 had tried to buy growth with paid users and discounts ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/729-startups-shut-in-2025-as-regulation-governance-reshape-indias-tech-landscape-86634.htm)). So split the money by stage, and open each box only when the one before it has earned the right.

This plan pays you no salary until the summer 2027 peak, so make sure your own living costs are covered from somewhere else.

## Register lightly in India; in the UK, buy advice and insurance, not a company

### India: four simple steps, and two questions for a CA

**Start as a sole proprietorship, which means the business is simply you, trading under a business name.** It needs no separate company registration and costs about ₹500 to ₹1,000 in its first year ([incorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/guide/how-to-register-a-company-in-india-in-2026)). Register on **Udyam**, the government's free small-business register. It is free and paperless, so use only [udyamregistration.gov.in](https://www.udyamregistration.gov.in/), because fake look-alike sites charge fees ([StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/msme-ministry-alerts-msmes-to-beware-of-fake-websites-charging-money-in-the-name-of-registration-91048.html)). Open a separate business current account. Banks usually want two proofs that the business exists, such as Udyam plus GST or a Shop and Establishment certificate ([Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/documents-required-for-proprietorship-bank-account-opening-in-india)). File a trademark for your brand name for **₹4,500** ([S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/ufaqs/trademark-filing-cost-inr-4500-india/)). You may use ™ straight away, but use ® only after registration, or you risk a penalty ([S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/trade-mark-symbols-india/)).

**GST (India's goods and services tax) needs a written CA opinion before you launch.** A service business normally registers only above **₹20 lakh** of sales a year ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-limit-registration-threshold-india/)). There is one catch. If you buy services from abroad, you may have to pay the GST on them yourself under the "reverse charge" rule, and anyone who must do that has to register whatever their sales ([TaxWink](https://www.taxwink.com/blog/gst-registration-rcm)). But for services "directly in relation to" a property, the tax place is where the property is ([KnowYourGST](https://www.knowyourgst.com/gstlaw/igst-act/13-place-of-supply-of-services-where-location-of-supplier-or-location-of-recipient-is-outside-india-187/)). A UK flat visit may therefore not count as buying services from abroad ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1066936)). Your CA's answer decides whether you register for GST from day one. Ask the same CA whether payments from customers abroad in pounds count as exports, which carry no GST. For income tax, a sole proprietor can usually declare **6% of digital receipts** as profit without keeping full accounts. This rule is now in Section 58 of the new Income-tax Act 2025 ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/section-44ad-presumptive-scheme); [taxconcept](https://taxconcept.net/income-tax/presumptive-taxation-new-thresholds-under-sections-44ad-44ada-and-44ae-for-2025/)). Ask your CA to confirm that it fits this business.

**Taking payments in and sending payments out both need care.** Razorpay charges 2% plus GST for Indian payments. It charges up to 3% for foreign cards, which you must apply for separately ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/international-payments/accept-payments-from-international-customers/)). Stripe accepts Indian businesses by invitation only ([Stripe](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-accounts-are-invite-only-in-india)). Sending money to UK partners is the harder part. The RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme, the yearly limit on what one person can send abroad, allows **US$250,000** per person, and a sole proprietor does not get a second limit for the business ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=115)). Paying for a business service normally goes through your bank as a business payment backed by the partner's invoice. The bank may ask for Indian tax forms 15CA and 15CB ([Skydo](https://web.skydo.com/blog/understanding-form-15-ca-cb-comprehensive-guide)). One review says new Indian Wise users cannot currently send money abroad ([Winvesta](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/wise-india-review-2026-features-fees-limitations-verdict)). Payoneer received in-principle RBI approval in January 2026 to handle payments into and out of India ([Payoneer](https://www.payoneer.com/press/payoneer-receives-in-principle-authorization-as-cross-border-payment-aggregator-in-india/)). Settle the route with your bank and CA in October, before your first partner invoice arrives. India's new data protection law (DPDP) applies its main duties to businesses from about **13 May 2027** ([AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)). Write a short privacy notice in English and Hindi now anyway, and collect only what you need.

### UK: no company yet, but four protections

**You do not need a UK company to start.** One costs **£100 (₹12,700)** to set up online plus **£50 (₹6,350)** a year ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-fees/companies-house-fees)). It needs a real UK postal address ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation/company-address)) and identity checks for directors ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/verify-your-identity-for-companies-house)). Owning a foreign company also adds yearly Indian filings under the overseas investment rules ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/fema-odi-regulations-guide/)). Open one later, only if insurers or a complaints scheme will not cover an Indian business, or if the volume of checks makes a UK bank account worth it.

**First, never handle tenants' money.** Then you do not need client money protection, which is required only for agents who hold tenants' money ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mandatory-client-money-protection)). **Second, the UK data protection fee.** The UK's data regulator, the ICO, charges organisations that use personal information **£52 (₹6,604)** a year at the smallest size, unless they are exempt ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/06/fee-payable-to-the-ico-to-increase-in-february-102jzb9)). Take its 10-minute self-assessment ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/self-assessment/)). UK data law probably applies to you, because your partners collect landlords' names and video in the UK. A business outside the UK may also need a "UK representative" ([LegalVision](https://legalvision.co.uk/data-privacy-it/uk-gdpr-article-27-representatives/)). For customers' data, your legal reason to use it is the contract with them. For landlords' data, it is preventing fraud ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/)). Do not record ID documents, and delete raw video after a fixed time, such as 90 days. **Third, insurance.** Professional indemnity insurance pays if a report contains a costly mistake. One in ten small UK firms paid **£62.72 (₹7,965) a year or less**, and one sole trader's quote was £114.50 (₹14,542) ([Simply Business](https://simplybusiness.co.uk/business-insurance/professional-indemnity-insurance)). Public liability insurance covers injury or damage and costs about **£115 to £155 (₹14,605 to ₹19,685)** a year ([NimbleFins](https://www.nimblefins.co.uk/public-liability-insurance-uk)). UK insurers may want a UK business, so also ask an Indian insurer for cover that includes UK claims. **Fourth, the complaints scheme question, which is a grey area.** The law says "lettings agency work" includes acting on instructions from "a person seeking to find a dwelling-house … to rent" ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2013/24/section/83)). A check service paid by the tenant could fall inside that. If so, you must join a redress scheme, an official complaints service for property firms. Property Redress costs **£155 plus VAT** a year (about ₹23,600) ([Property Redress](https://www.propertyredress.co.uk/business)). The Property Ombudsman costs about £278 a year plus a £74 joining fee (about ₹44,700 in the first year) ([TPO](https://www.tpos.co.uk/businesses/)). Never take commissions from landlords or agents. That would make you look like you work for them.

|---|---|---|
| Is my tenant-paid check "lettings agency work"? Must I join a complaints scheme? Can I sell in Scotland and Wales? | UK housing lawyer | Decides a yearly cost and where you may sell |
| How far can my terms limit my liability for a mistake? | UK lawyer | UK consumer law limits how much you can exclude |

## Go/stop rules and the main risks

**Write these rules down now, before you spend anything, and follow them even when you feel hopeful.**

|---|---|---|
| 31 October 2026 (set-up) | Lawful partners in at least 3 of the 5 first cities; 4 trial reports sent within 48 hours; the lawyer sees no licence you cannot afford | Fewer than 3 cities: start with London plus Coventry and Leicester, and use Viewber as backup. If the lawyer says you need a licence or a UK company, pause and cost it before spending more |
| 30 November 2026 (first sales) | 5 or more paid checks and 20 or more free Red-Flag Checks | Fewer than 5: change the message, price or channel once, then judge again on 31 December |

## Conclusion

The visits themselves are not what makes this business hard to copy. Viewber, Airtasker and Kinpany can all send someone to a door. What they lack is the trust of Indian families, Hindi and regional-language Reels, and a referral web of seniors, societies and small agents. Your selling and Reels skills are therefore the core of the business, not a side task, and the UK partner network is the part you can rent. England's new renting law cuts both ways. It shrinks the money each scam can take. It also hands you clear, legally true warnings, such as "six months up front is now illegal", that make strong content and turn worry into bookings.

The January 2027 season is a cheap test of whether people pay, not a test of the full market. The real decision is whether June to August 2027 brings 50 or more checks a month. If it does, grow sideways: add Nepali, Bangladeshi and Sri Lankan students, more cities and move-out checks, rather than spending on ads. If it does not, stop with ₹1 lakh still in the bank. Settle two open questions before the first paid visit: whether a tenant-paid check counts as letting agency work, and how you will legally pay UK partners from India. Everything else in the plan can be adjusted as you go.
