# Appendix B: Plain Words to Sanskrit

This book speaks English. When you open an app, sit with a family astrologer or pick up an older book, you will meet the Sanskrit. This list translates in both directions: the plain word used in these pages, the Sanskrit in parentheses, a one-line meaning, and the chapter where the idea first appears. Spellings vary from app to app (dasa or dasha, bhukti or antardasha); the sounds are the same.

## The timetable

- **season**, or **major season** when contrasting with a sub-season (mahadasha): the years when one planet runs your household; the top row of a dasha table (Ch 1).
- **sub-season** (antardasha; in South Indian usage bhukti): one of the nine divisions of a season, run by a second planet beside the first; the indented row in an app (Ch 3).
- **the small turns** (pratyantardasha): the nine divisions of a sub-season, lasting weeks to months; mentioned once and not lived by (Ch 3).
- **the 120-year timetable** (Vimshottari dasha): Parashara's season system, 120 years divided among the nine planets in a fixed order; the word means "a hundred and twenty" (Ch 1).
- **dasha** (dasha, dasa): literally "state" or "condition"; the system of seasons, and the one Sanskrit word this book keeps in prose (Ch 1).
- **season lord** (dasha nath, dasha swami): the planet whose years are running (Ch 1).
- **sub-lord** (antardasha nath, bhukti nath): the planet running the current sub-season (Ch 3).
- **the balance at birth** (dasha shesha, balance of dasha): the part of the first season still unspent at birth, in proportion to the part of the Moon's star still to cross (Ch 2).
- **the changeover**, the turning point between seasons or sub-seasons (dasha sandhi): the sensitive months on either side of a handover (Ch 3, Ch 15).
- **MD, AD, PD**: app abbreviations for mahadasha, antardasha and pratyantardasha, that is season, sub-season and small turn (Ch 3).
- **season map**, **the chart's timetable** (dasha chart): the table of your seasons and sub-seasons with dates, as an app prints it (Ch 2).
- **the 108-year timetable** (Ashtottari dasha): an alternative season system Parashara gives for some births; not used in this book (Ch 2).
- **Parashara**: the sage to whom the great textbook of Vedic astrology is attributed; "the tradition" in this book usually means his text (Ch 1).
- **BPHS** (Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra): Parashara's textbook, written as a conversation with his student Maitreya; the source of the 120-year timetable (Ch 1).
- **Vedic astrology** (Jyotish): "the science of light"; the Indian astrology this book uses (Ch 1).
- **the science of hours**, predictive astrology (hora shastra): the branch of Vedic astrology that reads birth charts, as distinct from calendar-keeping (Ch 1).

## The Moon and its stars

- **the Moon's star**, **your birth star** (nakshatra; janma nakshatra for the one at birth): one of 27 equal divisions of the Moon's path, each 13°20′ wide; its lord is your first season lord (Ch 1, Ch 2).
- **quarter of a star** (pada): one of four 3°20′ parts of a star; used in naming and in finer charts, not in the season sum (Ch 2).
- **Ashlesha**: the ninth star, 16°40′ to 30° Cancer, Mercury's; Meera's birth star (Ch 2).
- **Krittika**: the third star, 26°40′ Aries to 10° Taurus, the Sun's; Arjun's birth star (Ch 2).
- **Purva Ashadha**: the twentieth star, 13°20′ to 26°40′ Sagittarius, Venus's; Devika's birth star (Ch 2).
- **the Moon's sign at birth** (janma rashi, chandra rashi): the sign the Moon occupied at birth; the base from which Saturn's seven and a half years is counted (Ch 14).
- **the Moon's phase**, bright or dark fortnight (paksha: shukla, krishna): whether the Moon was waxing or waning at birth; it colours the Moon season (Ch 7).

## The chart

- **birth chart** (kundali, janma patrika, janma kundali): the map of the sky at the minute and place of birth (Ch 1).
- **rising sign** (Lagna, also "ascendant"): the sign rising in the east at birth; it fixes the twelve rooms and decides which planets are on your side (Ch 1).
- **lord of the rising sign** (lagnesh, Lagna lord): the planet that owns your rising sign; treated as on your side (Ch 13).
- **sign** (rashi): one of twelve 30° divisions of the zodiac belt (Ch 2).
- **the zodiac belt** (rashi chakra): the ring of twelve signs through which the Moon and planets travel (Ch 2).
- **room**, one of the twelve rooms of your chart (bhava; "house" in English books): an area of life, counted from the rising sign (Ch 1).
- **whole-sign rooms** (rashi bhava): the Vedic default in which one sign is one room (Ch 2).
- **lord of a room** (bhava adhipati, bhavesh): the planet that owns the sign in that room; its seasons bring that room's matters due (Ch 3).
- **planet** (graha; "that which grasps"): one of the nine actors of the chart; Rahu and Ketu keep their own names (Ch 1).
- **the two shadow planets** (chhaya graha): Rahu and Ketu, the two points where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's; the north and south nodes (Ch 1).
- **the star-fixed zodiac** (nirayana, sidereal): the zodiac measured against the actual stars, which Vedic astrology uses (Ch 2).
- **the season-fixed zodiac** (sayana, tropical): the zodiac measured from the spring equinox, used by Western astrology (Ch 2).
- **the standard Indian setting** for the zodiac (Lahiri ayanamsa, also Chitrapaksha): the official Indian value for the gap between the two zodiacs, adopted in 1955; set it once in your app (Ch 2).
- **the gap between the two zodiacs** (ayanamsa): the slowly growing offset, about 24°, between the star-fixed and season-fixed zodiacs (Ch 2).
- **almanac** (panchanga, panchang): the five-limbed Indian calendar of lunar day, weekday, star, and two further measures; the name behind Drik Panchang (Ch 2).

## Rooms by group

- **the four pillar rooms**, 1, 4, 7 and 10 (kendra): the angles of the chart; planets here act strongly and steadily (Ch 3).
- **the three lucky rooms**, 1, 5 and 9 (trikona): rooms of merit and fortune; a sub-lord here, counted from the season lord, works with the season (Ch 3).
- **the three hard rooms**, 6, 8 and 12 (dusthana, trik): rooms of struggle, upheaval and loss; the "wrong rooms" when counted from a season lord (Ch 3).
- **the growing rooms**, 3, 6, 10 and 11 (upachaya): rooms that improve with time and effort (Ch 13).
- **the two rooms of endings**, 2 and 7 (maraka): rooms whose lords are watched for health in old age in classical texts; this book does not predict from them (Ch 13).
- **the obstructing room** (badhaka): a room, varying by rising sign, whose lord brings obstacles in some schools; glossary only (Ch 13).
- **the room of marriage**, room 7 (kalatra bhava, yuvati bhava): spouse and partnership; Venus owns it for Meera (Ch 3).

## Strength and company

- **a planet that is on your side** (functional benefic, shubha for your rising sign): a planet that owns helpful rooms for your rising sign and so gives good results in its seasons (Ch 4, Ch 13).
- **a planet that tests you** (functional malefic, papa for your rising sign): a planet that owns difficult rooms for your rising sign; its seasons bring tasks (Ch 4, Ch 13).
- **your best planet** (yogakaraka): the single planet that owns both a pillar room and a lucky room for your rising sign (Ch 4).
- **naturally gentle planet** (saumya or shubha graha): Jupiter, Venus, the waxing Moon and well-placed Mercury (Ch 13).
- **naturally harsh planet** (krura or papa graha): the Sun, Mars, Saturn, Rahu, Ketu and the waning Moon; harsh by nature, not necessarily against you (Ch 13).
- **at its strongest place** (uccha, exalted): the sign where a planet works best, such as the Moon in Taurus or Jupiter in Cancer (Ch 13).
- **at its weakest place** (neecha, debilitated): the sign opposite its strongest, where a planet struggles, such as the Sun in Libra (Ch 13).
- **at home** (swakshetra, own sign): a planet in a sign it owns, such as Venus in Libra; steady and comfortable (Ch 3).
- **the planet's favourite stretch of its own sign** (moolatrikona): a particular span within one own sign where it is strongest (Ch 13).
- **outshone by the Sun** (asta, combust): a planet so close to the Sun that it loses strength, such as Meera's Saturn (Ch 3).
- **walking backwards** (vakri, retrograde): a planet that appears to move backwards against the stars for some months; its results come inwardly or late (Ch 13).
- **sits with** (yuti, conjunction): two planets in the same sign and room (Ch 13).
- **looks at**, casts its gaze on (drishti, aspect): a planet's influence on a room some distance from it; the 7th for all, with extra gazes for Mars, Jupiter and Saturn (Ch 13).
- **natural friendship** (naisargika maitri): the fixed friendships and enmities among the planets; Question one for any sub-season (Ch 3).
- **temporary friendship** (tatkalika maitri): friendship from position in a particular chart, combined with natural friendship by careful readers (Ch 13).
- **exchange of homes** (parivartana): two planets each sitting in the other's sign, fusing their rooms; Devika's Moon and Jupiter (Ch 7).
- **the planet whose star the season lord sits in** (nakshatra lord, "star lord" in the KP school): the hidden agenda of a season in the modern practice (Ch 13).
- **the planet that stands for something** (karaka): the natural signifier of a matter, such as the Sun for the father, Venus for marriage, Jupiter for children (Ch 5).
- **a combination** (yoga): a pattern of planets with a named result in the classics; this book describes such patterns in English (Ch 13).
- **a royal combination** (raja yoga): a pillar-room lord joined with a lucky-room lord, promising a rise in status in its seasons (Ch 13).
- **the ninefold chart** (navamsa, D9): a finer chart used for marriage and inner strength; not used in this book (Ch 13).
- **the divided charts** (varga): the family of finer charts of which the ninefold chart is one; Book 1 territory (Ch 13).

## The weather overhead

- **the weather** (gochar, gochara, transit): where the planets are moving now, laid over the birth chart (Ch 14).
- **Saturn's seven and a half years** (Sade Sati): Saturn's passage over the sign before, the sign of, and the sign after your birth Moon, about 2½ years each (Ch 4, Ch 14).
- **Saturn's two and a half years over the fourth or eighth** (dhaiya, kantaka Shani, ashtama Shani): shorter testing passages counted from the birth Moon (Ch 14).
- **the Moon as rising sign** (chandra lagna): reading the chart with the birth Moon's sign as the first room, used for all weather from the Moon (Ch 14).

## Practices and conduct

- **a practice** (upaya; never "remedy" alone in this book): a conduct, service or routine matched to a season's planet (Ch 17).
- **giving** (daan, dana): gifts of food, cloth or time as a practice for a planet (Ch 17).
- **repetition** (japa): repeating a short sacred phrase a set number of times (Ch 17).
- **a short sacred phrase** (mantra): the words repeated in japa; the planets each have one (Ch 17).
- **gemstone** (ratna): the stones sold for planets; this book advises against buying them out of fear (Ch 17).
- **the planet's weekday** (vara): each planet owns a day, Sunday for the Sun through Saturday for Saturn; practices are often done on that day (Ch 17).

## Karma, in three words

- **the whole store** (sanchita karma): everything ever set in motion, which no chart can read (Ch 1).
- **the portion allotted to this life** (prarabdha karma): what has ripened and is due now; the part a chart and its seasons show (Ch 1).
- **what you do now** (kriyamana karma): the choices made inside each season; entirely yours (Ch 1).

## The court

- **the King** (Surya, the Sun): authority, father, identity (Ch 1, Ch 6).
- **the Queen Mother** (Chandra, the Moon): mind, mother, home, moods (Ch 1, Ch 7).
- **the Commander** (Mangala or Kuja, Mars): energy, courage, property, conflict (Ch 1, Ch 8).
- **the clever young Prince** (Budha, Mercury): learning, speech, trade (Ch 1, Ch 12).
- **the Royal Priest** (Guru or Brihaspati, Jupiter): wisdom, teachers, children, growth (Ch 1, Ch 10).
- **the Minister of Pleasure** (Shukra, Venus): love, marriage, comfort, beauty (Ch 1, Ch 5).
- **the old Judge of Labour** (Shani, Saturn): work, limits, time, maturity (Ch 1, Ch 11).
- **the smoke-headed Stranger** (Rahu, the north node): hunger, foreign places, illusion, sudden rises (Ch 1, Ch 9).
- **the headless Sadhu** (Ketu, the south node): letting go, detachment, research, solitude (Ch 1, Ch 4).
