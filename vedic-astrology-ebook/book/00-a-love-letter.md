# A Love Letter, Before We Begin

Dear reader,

If you have picked up this book, I think I know something about you. You are curious about the sky, or about yourself, or about why the same year that was wonderful for your sister was so hard for you. Somewhere along the way you decided to learn astrology properly. So you bought the books everyone recommends. You opened them with real hope. And within ten pages you felt stupid.

I know, because that was me.

I bought the thick classics. I bought the modern ones with the confident titles. I joined a course and downloaded a hundred pages of notes that said "Saturn in the 7th house gives…" followed by a list, and "Mercury in the 8th house gives…" followed by another list, and I copied them into a notebook and forgot them by the weekend. Nobody told me *why*. Nobody told me why Saturn and the Sun cannot stand each other, or why the same planet that ruins one person's marriage house is the best thing in another person's chart. Without the why, nothing stayed.

So I did the only thing I could. I went back to the beginning and rebuilt the subject for myself, one reason at a time, until every rule had a story I could retell and a reason I could defend. It took years. It should not have. The grammar of astrology is small: nine planets, twelve signs, twelve houses, twenty-seven stars. What makes it hard is not the size of it. It is that the books were written for students who already knew the sky, and nobody translated them for the rest of us.

This book is that translation. It is my love letter to everyone who is struggling the way I struggled. I have tried to make it as simple as I possibly can without making it false, and I have tried to give you a reason for everything, because reasons are what stay.

Let me tell you honestly what I hope for you.

I hope that by the last page you can pick up any birth chart and read it, calmly, from the first line to the last. I hope you can time the seasons of a life with the dashas, answer the questions people actually ask (work, money, love, health, children), and sit across from someone with kindness and confidence. Part V of this book is about exactly that: how to consult, and how to do it ethically.

But I hope for something more basic too, and it is the real reason I wrote this. Life does not always let us do what we planned. You may learn all of this and never take a single paid consultation, because of work, family, health, or simply because life turned a corner. That is fine. If that happens, I want you to still have this: the ability to open your own chart and understand your own nature without fear. To look at your mother's chart, your child's, your partner's, your friend's, and know what season they are in and why they are behaving the way they are. To recognise a hard year as a hard year and not as a verdict on your worth. To know, when the ground shifts under someone you love, what is happening and roughly how long it will last.

That knowledge is quieter than a consulting practice, and I have come to believe it is the more important gift.

A few promises, so you know what kind of book this is.

Every rule comes with its reason. Where the reason is astronomy, I say so. Where it is the internal logic of the system, I show the derivation. Where it is the word of a classical author, I name them. Where it is tradition or folk practice, I tell you that too, so you always know how firm the ground is under your feet.

Every hard idea comes with a story. The nine planets are a royal court in this book: a King and a Queen Mother, a Commander, a clever young Prince, a Priest, a Minister of Pleasure, an old Judge, and two strangers at the gate. Who likes whom, who resents whom, what happens when two of them share a room: you will remember these as stories long after you have forgotten the tables. And every table that judges anything is colour-coded, because our eyes remember colour when our memory fails.

Everything from Lal Kitab is clearly labelled as Lal Kitab, in its own boxes, with the source stated. The mixing of systems without labels is, I believe, one of the biggest reasons students get confused today, and I refuse to do it to you.

And there is no fear in this book. Astrology has been damaged by people who frighten others into buying stones and rituals. You will learn the remedies, and you will learn to offer them gently, as a friend suggests a diet, with reasons and without threats.

Read it in order. Do the little exercises at the end of each chapter, because reading about swimming has never kept anyone afloat. Keep a notebook. Cast your own chart in the first chapter and carry it with you through the whole book; by the end, you will know that chart the way you know your own handwriting.

I am not writing to you as a guru on a hill. I am writing as someone who sat where you are sitting, with the same closed books and the same feeling of being locked out, and who found the door. Come, I will show you where it is.

With love,

Anushka Bharti
